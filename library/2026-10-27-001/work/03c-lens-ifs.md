## Internal Family Systems reading of Khadija Amrani

### Reading

Khadija's system is organised around one unpaid account. At its centre is an exile, **the Girl Under the Awning** (19). She holds a shoebox and a burden: *I am the one who didn't get on, and I kept what was hers.* Further down is a younger exile, **the Girl on the Wet Tiles** (14). She carried delight: a finger writing on her back, laughter. The delight was exiled along with the shame.

Protectors:
- **The Counter** (manager, about 35). Wants every drawer square. Fears that any unexplained sum will expose the 190. *"Count it twice."* Called up by money, paper and the pension letter.
- **The Card-Folder** (manager, about 10, a copy of her mother). Keeps the gone gone. *"When someone goes, they're gone."* Called up by anyone reaching toward her.
- **The Quick Mouth** (manager, ageless). Impressions, interruptions, *"Put comedy."* Fears where sentences end. Called up by tenderness.
- **Firefighters:** washing clean glasses, 4,112 games of solitaire, six minutes behind a toilet door.

She is most blended with the Counter: *"I'm not sentimental."*

**On the mechanism:** the double is Fatiha, the self who got on the bus. IFS shifts the reading, though. Khadija doesn't dread that Fatiha exists. She dreads being looked at by her, from behind, where she was once touched. And her managers are already walking toward the double, disguised as errands: meter coins, hammam hours, shoe-stretching. There is also a reversal. In Fatiha's life, Khadija is the face under the folded card.

### Evidence

- She lies about not crying. In fact she spent six minutes in the toilet after receiving the voucher *(dossier: §10, Naïma; interview: Q11)*.
- She says she listens to every message from Amine. In fact one voice note has sat unopened for nine days. The Card-Folder now silences her own son *(dossier: §10; interview: Q4)*.
- "That I'm a thief." She turns the earring and defends herself with the Vigilance Award, which hangs where she washes her hands *(interview: Q10; dossier: scene C, §11)*.
- She "never forgave herself" for five dirhams, an obvious stand-in, then folds the cup sleeve in four *(interview: Q17)*.
- She keeps a notebook of customers' small debts and never collects. She forgives everyone's debts but her own *(dossier: §7)*.
- She says of her aunt: "Maybe the sister did write. Maybe somebody didn't answer." This is a flicker of Self, said about her own unanswered phone calls *(interview: Q16; dossier: §10, Fatiha)*.
- "I'm afraid of being seen. From behind." Then she deflects to "the day" *(interview: Q13)*.
- Fatiha is reduced to "a friend… She went away. Tarek. Write Tarek." *(interview: Q18)*.
- Legacy burdens: the card over the aunt's face; the father's debt, never mentioned; the rule that those who go are gone *(dossier: §2)*.
- Self is visible in teaching Rachid's mother to sign her name *(dossier: §9)*, in "with their whole arm" *(interview: Q19)*, and in "Easy to say to other people" *(interview: Q9)*.

### Under pressure

**First move:** the Counter turns the hammam into logistics. She counts coins for the meter, checks the women's hours and wears the right djellaba for the day. It is an errand, not an approach.

**Escalation:** inside, she reads hands, as she does at the till. She scans for a woman pushing up sleeves she doesn't have, or a white scar under a jaw. The Quick Mouth chatters at the attendant. She sits with her back to the tiled wall.

**Worst five minutes:** she recognises Fatiha. The Girl Under the Awning floods her and the Counter grabs the wheel. Its idea of repair is settlement without contact. She takes the earrings out, presses them into the attendant's palm and says, *"For the lady with the bad knees. Tell her it's from Khadija."* Then she makes for the exit. It is 1981 again: a messenger sent to the phone while she stays out of sight. Then she stops in the doorway, letting the cold in, the exact person she can't stand. This is her polarisation made visible: the part that stays and the part that goes, both in one body, on one threshold.

### Tells

- She folds any paper she is holding into four and presses the crease flat with her thumbnail when talk turns personal.
- She turns one gold earring between finger and thumb whenever money, theft or the past comes up.
- She finishes other people's sentences for them, usually with a price or a punchline.
- She always takes the seat with her back to a wall and shifts away when anyone stands behind her.
- She runs the tap and rewashes clean glasses right after someone says something kind to her.

### Arc

**Possible change:** one moment where Self leads instead of the Counter. The Counter wants to pay the debt. Self wants to be seen.

**The concrete action:** in the hot room she turns her back to Fatiha, sloped shoulder and all, and lets it be seen. Or she reaches out and, with one finger, writes T-A-Z-A on Fatiha's wet back. It is a joke only one person alive would get, and it is curious, playful and connected. No speech, no apology. The earrings can come later, or never.

**What it needs:** the voucher's deadline, Mohamed's sentence, and the shoes that won't stretch, all closing in together, plus one instant in which the Quick Mouth runs out of material.

**What it costs:** the story that she "doesn't keep things," and with it her rule-keeping mother's version of strength. Possibly also an honest look at the separate bedrooms and at what "a friend" meant *(inference)*. She would have to let the 14-year-old's delight come back, and that delight brings its grief with it.

**Not changing:** the voucher expires, and she folds it in four and lays it in the shoebox beside seat 22. She takes the part-time night shifts Youssef doesn't know about and stays awake while the city sleeps until her hip quits. She tells Rachid, again, *"If you're going, go."*

### What my lens can't explain

- **Why she stayed in 1981.** Nobody stopped her, and nobody noticed she had left. IFS can name the part that froze her under the awning, but the material gives no outside cause. It may be truer that there wasn't one.
- **The Belkacem name.** The guard she feeds, "never once let go hungry," shares a name with her father's creditor. My lens wants a legacy-debt reading, but this may be coincidence and I won't build on it.

### Confidence

**High.** The interview leaks nearly every part in its own voice, and the earrings, the five dirhams and the Vigilance Award by the sink line up with the hidden debt almost too neatly to be accidental.
