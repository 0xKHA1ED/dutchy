## Core-dramatic reading of Souad Ben Salah

### Reading
Souad's **want** is concrete enough to film: her daughter on the October train to Rabat, and her own mouth shut on the platform. Her **lie**: *love is proved by not asking, and wanting to keep someone is theft.* Her wound is not the husband's fall, the obvious candidate. It is the two days she hid the acceptance letter (dossier: secret). In those two days her wanting outran her virtue, and she has been policing it ever since.

Her **psychological need** is to admit the keeping-wish as hers without letting it decide. Her **moral need** is smaller and sharper. She withholds herself from people. She recognised Khadija and chose silence. She says less than she knows (dossier: history, speech).

For the protagonist she is the **counter-double**. Khadija trails a stranger's back looking for a crime. Souad already reads every back in the room, including Khadija's. She is the one person who could tell Khadija what she looks like from behind. In miniature she is also the uncanny made kind: someone who knows Khadija intimately and whom Khadija knows only as a hand.

### Evidence
- Want, rehearsing not crying at the station (dossier: home)
- The hidden letter, and the shame (dossier: secret)
- Knows regulars "by her back" (dossier: work)
- Six years of 4 a.m. yogurt and bread, the 2-dirham debt under "the finger", recognition kept quiet (dossier: history)
- "I'm not going anywhere." "Do you want me to tell you what yours looks like?" (dossier: speech)

### Under pressure
- **First move:** patience as pressure. She keeps coming back with the bucket and keeps not leaving.
- **Escalation:** the line that lands. She stops tending Khadija's body and names her behaviour.
- **Worst five minutes:** Khadija's evasion mirrors her own two days of hiding, and Souad's rehearsed composure cracks. She scrubs too hard, or says one sentence about the station that she never meant to say aloud.

### Tells
- She counts the tips in her headscarf knot by touch without looking down.
- She reappears in the doorway at regular intervals holding the same bucket and towel.
- She looks at women's backs and shoulders, rarely their faces.
- She keeps her right hand, with its missing fingertip, low and half-curled near Khadija.
- She answers every postponement with the same unhurried "I'm not going anywhere."

### Arc
In her own story the **forced choice** is on the platform. If she asks the girl to stay, she keeps her daughter and becomes the thief her lie warned her about. If she lets her go, she loses her daughter's daily presence.

The deeper choice comes in Khadija's story. She can tell Khadija who she is, and maybe pay the two dirhams, or keep her silence. Speaking costs her the safety of being only the hand at 4 a.m. Staying silent keeps the lie intact.

Her true self-revelation would be "I can want her to stay and still send her." The **false self-revelation**, which is equally plausible, is "I didn't cry, so I was right." That ending is not-changing: she gets the want and buries the need under it.

### What my lens can't explain
- Why she has left a 2-dirham debt unpaid for years, perhaps as a deliberate thread.
- Why she decides not to reveal herself. Discretion, shame and tenderness each fit, and my lens can't choose among them.

### Confidence
**Medium.** The want and lie are well grounded, but with no interview there is no gap between what she says and what she does to test them against.
