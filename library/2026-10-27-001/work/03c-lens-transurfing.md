## Reality Transurfing reading of Khadija Amrani

### Reading

Read as plot physics, Khadija is a woman whose books do not balance. One account has been open since 23:42 on 14 October 1981. Her importance sits on it twice. The inner charge is *"I am not a thief."* The outer charge is Fatiha, the bus, and the 190 dirhams.

She has kept that potential from bursting by moving it into small, safe ledgers. There is a till short twice in 27 years. There are five dirhams that bother her "more than they should." There is a notebook of one-dirham debts she never collects. She is the store's thief-catcher, and she has hung the proof in the toilet. In the model, the person most vigilant about theft is the one guarding against her own reflection.

The mechanism file calls the double a *disowned self*. This lens reads it differently. Fatiha is less a lost self than a **creditor**. The uncanny here works through the dual mirror. Khadija last saw Fatiha from behind and never forward, and now she cannot bear to be seen from behind herself (back to the wall, the deleted photograph, no full-length mirror). She sent word that she was "out." Today she leaves her son's voice note unopened and Hamid's message unanswered. The world reflects that withholding back: a husband who says nothing, a home where she will be invisible by day.

Her slide is from the Egyptian films: *the person on the platform gets one more chance in the last ten minutes.* That is wanting. Her phone searches, though, read like intention: tariffs, timetables, opening hours, stretching. Only a cashier would plan a reckoning by counting two-dirham coins.

### Under pressure

**Pendulums.** The store holds her by belonging and by the night as territory. She fights it through Hamid, and the memo collection is her feeding it from the opposing side *(inference)*. The older pendulum is the family of silence, with its card folded over a face: *"When someone goes, they're gone"* *(interview: Q6)*. Its hook is shame.

**First move.** She over-prepares. She will try to make the debt exact before she can face Fatiha. *(Inference)*: she calculates what 190 dirhams of 1981 is worth now, four times, as she did with the pension. She counts it out in the coins the meters require. She wears the shoes at home with wet socks.

**The balancing force** comes out of her own grip:
- *Comic version:* the shoes squeak in the corridor, and Youssef finally asks a question. She feeds the Bab Boujloud meter so many carefully hoarded two-dirham pieces that she overpays by hours. The attendant at the hammam tells her to take off her jewellery. The woman who must not be seen from behind is face-down on the warm marble while a stranger scrubs the very back she has hidden for 44 years. The scrubber pushes up sleeves she doesn't have.
- *Tragic version:* she reaches the door and stands in it, the type she has no patience for. Through the steam she sees a woman's back. She pays the meter and drives home before the dawn prayer.

**Worst five minutes.** Fatiha recognises her and starts to speak. Khadija answers with a price, cuts the sentence off as she does with Rachid, and turns her earring until her ear bleeds a little.

### Tells

- When a name from her past comes up, she folds whatever paper is nearest into four and presses the crease flat with her thumbnail.
- She turns one gold earring between finger and thumb whenever money or honesty is mentioned.
- She answers personal questions with prices or numbers: "Twelve dirhams fifty."
- She picks seats against walls and steps sideways, never forward, out of doorways, so no one stands behind her.
- She sorts the coin jar in the Dacia into stacks of two-dirham pieces and counts them twice.

### Arc

**Possible change.** In the model, freedom comes from a move the pendulum's script can't absorb. Here the script is *withhold, guard, balance the drawer*. The one action is this: in the hammam, she takes off the hoops and puts them, unfolded and unwrapped, into Fatiha's wet palm, saying nothing about the arithmetic. Then she turns around and offers her back, sloped shoulder and all, for a word to be traced on it. She doesn't have to guess it right.

**What it needs.** The voucher's deadline and Fatiha's bad knees have to put them in the same steam room. Her plan has to fail, with the exact sum unmade and the shoes still too tight, so that she arrives with nothing to settle the account except the earrings and her back.

**Cost.** She loses the identity of the woman whose drawer is never short. She has to explain the earrings to Youssef after 39 years. She will be seen, unguarded, by the one person she let down.

**Not changing.** She goes to the party on the 28th and imitates Hamid saying "dynamique" for the cake. She folds the expired voucher in four. Part-time nights come through and the store pendulum takes her back. She buys new size-39 shoes and keeps the 37s under the bed. Amine's voice note stays unopened.

### What my lens can't explain

- **Why she stayed in 1981.** Importance and pendulums describe the freeze and the 44 years of holding it, but not what made her stand under the café awning. The lens has no account of that first cause, whether fear, love, family or the undiscussed father's debt.
- **Generosity without strain.** Teaching Rachid's mother to sign, paying for Amine's school, the uncollected debts. Some of her generosity carries no importance at all, and the lens is better at reading tension than kindness.

### Confidence

Medium. The plot physics fits almost too neatly: debt, mirror, back and coins all line up. That fit is a reason for some suspicion that the lens is describing her beautifully without explaining her.
