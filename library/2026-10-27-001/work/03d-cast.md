## Fatiha Kaddour, 63

**Role in the mechanism:** *The screen*, and in part *the model*. Khadija accuses her of being the thief, a con woman working a slow distraction with the glove. Fatiha is innocent of that. Of the two of them, she is the one who was robbed. She is also the model for the life Khadija did not choose: she got on the bus, and everything Khadija disowned has been lived inside Fatiha's thin, quick body for forty-four years.

**Their own want:** To come home to Fès for good and sign for a small ground-floor flat near her brother's stall before her knees decide for her.

**Short dossier:**
Fatiha Kaddour was born in 1962, three doors from the Amrani house off Talaa Kebira. She was the second of five children of a coppersmith who beat trays in the Seffarine and coughed all winter.

On the night bus of 14 October 1981 she kept her bag on seat 22 until Larache, then moved it to her knees when a soldier asked. She arrived in Tangier at dawn with 40 dirhams and the shoes she was wearing, black flats with a gold buckle, size 38. They did stretch.

**Work:**
- 1981 to 1994: she sewed jeans pockets in a factory in the free zone, nine hours a day.
- 1995 to 2024: she was a *kessala*, a scrubber, at a neighbourhood hammam in Beni Makada. She retired last year with no pension worth the name.
- She has scrubbed, by her own rough count, more backs than there are people in Fès el-Bali.

**Home:** For twenty-eight years she has shared a two-room flat on a steep street in Tangier with Khadija Trabelsi, a friend from the factory. They sleep in separate rooms and eat from one dish. She never married. Since 2019 she has spent every August and September in Fès at her brother Mohamed's house, and she goes to the hammam near Bab Boujloud three times a week for her knees.

**Habit:** Before any task she pushes her sleeves up past the elbow. She does it with the dishes and with the glove, and she does it naked in the steam, where there are no sleeves.

**Physical detail:** A small white scar under her jaw, from a bucket rim in 1976.

**The shoes:** She still owns the original pair, resoled three times by the same Tangier cobbler. She wears them only for the journey to Fès and back. They are worn soft at the heels.

**Secret:** In November 1981 she rang the corner grocer twice and asked for Khadija. The second time, behind Zohra's voice saying "She's out," she heard Khadija cough. She has always known Khadija was home. She has never told anyone, including Dija, that it was not the bus she lost. It was the knowledge that her friend chose not to come to the phone.

**Second secret:** In the cold room she recognised the walk, the left foot landing flat, before she saw anything else. She said nothing and kept working.

**History with the protagonist:** Seven summers on the same roof mattress, one shared lipstick, the back-writing game, and a tin of savings she has decided, over the years, to think of as a wedding present she gave without being asked.

**How they speak:** Fast, low and practical. Her Fès accent has been sanded down by Tangier, and she drops in Spanish words without noticing. She gives orders as if they were kindnesses and kindnesses as if they were orders. "Venga, Dija, the other shoulder. I'm not paid today, don't make me ask twice." To a stranger at the bucket tap: "Take it, take it. Water is not money."

**What would surprise the protagonist about them:** The slowness Khadija reads as a pickpocket's distraction is Fatiha's trade. For thirty years she was paid to scrub strangers exactly that slowly, and this back is the only one she has ever scrubbed for free.

**Lenses to run:** *existential* (she is the one who chose, and she is now choosing to come back, which threatens the whole arrangement of refusal), *defences-attachment* (the cough on the phone, and how she has organised a life of care around a door that was shut on her).

## Khadija Trabelsi ("Dija"), 64

**Role in the mechanism:** *The mirror*, here made literal as *the double*. She is only ever seen from behind. She has Khadija's sloped shoulders with the left one lower, Khadija's stiff left hip, henna-auburn ends over a grey parting, and a scarf tied at the nape. Fatiha once uses her full name, which is also Khadija's. She is the woman who did get on a bus: from Taza (the city Khadija guessed twice), alone, at eighteen. She carries the unlived life without knowing she carries it.

**Their own want:** To have her hip replaced in November and dance at her granddaughter's wedding in Tangier in December, on two working legs.

**Short dossier:**
Khadija Trabelsi was born in Taza in 1961, the eldest daughter of a baker. In the spring of 1979 her father promised her to a cousin. Two weeks before the engagement she took the morning bus to Tangier with a cardboard suitcase. A neighbour had told her the free zone was hiring girls who could count.

In 1982 she was put at the sewing machine next to a thin girl from Fès who worked faster than anyone and talked to nobody. Within a month Fatiha was eating lunch at her table.

**Family:** She married a fisherman in 1985, had one daughter in 1987, and divorced him in 1996 after he sold her sewing machine. That year she moved into Fatiha's flat "for a month". Her daughter lives in Tetouan. Her granddaughter is marrying in December, and Dija has already bought the fabric for the *takchita*.

**Work:** She was a supervisor at the factory from 1998 until her hip forced her out in 2021. She did the shift accounts in her head, and the young girls called her "the calculator".

**Body:** Heavy and broad-hipped. Osteoarthritis of the left hip has made her walk exactly the way Khadija Amrani walks.

**The operation:** The surgery at a Tangier clinic is booked for 12 November. She has paid the deposit, and the receipt is in her purse. She cannot bend to a bucket or carry a bag and hold the wall at the same time, so Fatiha carries the purse from bench to bench and never opens it. Seen from the outside, this looks exactly like what Khadija thinks it is.

**Habit:** She sings under her breath when something hurts. The worse the pain, the more cheerful the song.

**Secret:** One winter night in 2003, Fatiha told her about the girl who did not get on the bus, and said the name. Since that night Dija has let Fatiha call her "Dija" and never the full name. She understood without being told that Fatiha could not say it. She does not mind. She has never asked to see a photograph.

**Present tension:** She does not want to move to Fès. She wants the sea she sees every morning from the end of her street. Fatiha's flat-hunting this summer is the first real disagreement of their twenty-eight years.

**History with the protagonist:** None. She does not know Khadija Amrani is in the room.

**How they speak:** Loud, warm and funny. Her Taza vowels survived forty years of Tangier. She complains about her body like a comedian working an audience, and she laughs at her own jokes before the end. "Slowly, Fatiha, slowly. I'm a museum, not a carpet." When the glove reaches the bad hip: "Ah! There. That's where the bill is."

**What would surprise the protagonist about them:** She is not the victim of anything. She pays for everything, she is happy, and she does impressions of the factory boss that make Fatiha cry with laughter.

**Lenses to run:** *jungian* (she is the shadow life in flesh, the Khadija who ran from the arranged marriage and paid for it and was not destroyed), *lacanian* (the mirror image seen only from behind: the self the protagonist cannot see, made whole and unbearable).

## Souad Ben Salah, 52

**Role in the mechanism:** *The witness*, with a touch of *catalyst*. She is the attendant who is meant to give Khadija the scrub included in Naïma's voucher. Each time she comes to collect her, Khadija postpones it to keep following the "thieves". Souad sees from the first room that Khadija is not watching a crime. She has one line that lands.

**Their own want:** To get her daughter onto the train to Rabat in October for university, and not let herself ask the girl to stay.

**Short dossier:**
Souad Ben Salah has worked at the women's hammam near Bab Boujloud since 2004. She took the job after her husband, a builder, fell from scaffolding and could no longer work.

**Body:** She has forearms like loaves and a voice that carries through three rooms of steam. She lost the tip of her right ring finger to a door in a tannery when she was sixteen.

**Work:** She knows every regular by her back: the moles, the scars, the way each woman holds her shoulders when she lies down on the tiles. She knows which buckets belong to which families, and she settles bucket wars with one look. Two nights a week she also cleans offices at the railway station, from 23:00 to 04:00. On the way to the first bus she buys yogurt and bread at the 24-hour supermarket nearby.

**Home:** Two rooms in Bab Ftouh with her husband and her only daughter, who is eighteen and has a place to study law in Rabat. Souad wants her to go with a fierceness that frightens her. Every night she rehearses not crying at the station.

**Habit:** She tucks tips into the knot of her headscarf and counts them by touch at the end of the day.

**Secret:** When her daughter's acceptance letter came, she hid it for two days before showing her husband. She is ashamed of those two days.

**History with the protagonist:** Khadija does not know her face. But for six years, at 4 a.m., a hand with a missing fingertip has put yogurt and bread on Khadija's belt. There is a 2-dirham debt in Khadija's notebook under "the finger." Souad recognised Khadija the moment she handed over the voucher and has decided not to say so.

**How they speak:** Direct and loud, with the patience of someone who has seen every kind of body and every kind of shyness. She says less than she knows. Early on: "Your scrub is paid, lalla. Lie down when you're ready. I'm not going anywhere." Later, in the hot room, the line that lands: "You've been watching her back for an hour. Do you want me to tell you what yours looks like?"

**What would surprise the protagonist about them:** She is one of Khadija's own 4 a.m. regulars, known for years by her hands. She owes Khadija two dirhams and has always known it.

**Lenses to run:** *core-dramatic* (she is the scene's practical pressure, the paid scrub that keeps coming back, offering the touch on Khadija's own back), *ifs* (her protective part wants to keep her daughter, and her chosen self is sending her away, which is the move Khadija never made).

## The cast as a system

Khadija needs Fatiha to be a thief, because then the evidence in the hammam is a crime and not a kindness, and she can stay the detective rather than the defendant. She needs Dija never to turn around, because a back can be anyone's, while a face would be a person with a whole life lived under Khadija's name. Fatiha, without meaning to, needs Dija to stay a back as well. The whole household rests on the arrangement: "Dija," never the full name, and the flat in Fès as a way of finally going home while still not facing the woman who stayed. So Fatiha's choice to make Dija turn, once she has recognised the walk, is her first act against her own forty-year defence, and it is aimed at Khadija. Dija is the free agent. She is cheerful and unknowing, and she is quietly in conflict with Fatiha over Tangier versus Fès, the sea versus the brother's street. She does not need Khadija at all, which is the cruellest thing a double can be. Souad stands outside the triangle and sees it whole. She lives the same choice in the present, keeping a daughter or letting her go, and she has decided to let go. Every time she says "lie down, lalla," she offers Khadija the one thing she has refused since 1981: to have her own back touched and seen. Nobody is the villain. The thieves are innocent, the victim is happy, and the witness is kind. The only theft in the building happened at a tin in 1981 and has been worn as two gold hoops ever since. The person who could name it chooses the parking ticket instead.
