## Dramatic structure reading of Khadija Amrani

### Reading

Khadija's conscious want is concrete and small: **keep her till**. She wants part-time night cover after the 28th, the Dacia unsold, and the 3 a.m. view of every aisle. Underneath it is a second want she won't name: to get to Bab Boujloud before the voucher expires, in shoes that fit.

The obvious wound is the bus. The real ghost is **November 1981**. Fatiha phoned the grocer twice, and twice Khadija sent Zohra down to say she was out. The bus was paralysis; the phone was a choice. In June 1986 she chose again, turning Fatiha's 190 dirhams into wedding gold. Character is choice under pressure, and Khadija has made the same choice three times.

**The lie:** *if I stay where I am and say nothing, nothing is lost and nothing is owed.* She inherited it from the card over her aunt's face ("When someone goes, they're gone"). She enforces it with the exact count of a drawer.

- **Psychological need:** to be seen from behind, and to let someone touch the back she hides.
- **Moral need:** to answer a call. Fatiha's, but also Amine's unopened voice note. She is doing to her son what she did to her friend.

The mechanism holds, but it runs as plot rather than haunting. Fatiha is the double because she is the natural opponent: the woman who wanted the same thing and went. Khadija doesn't follow her double; she takes the long way round. The uncanny lives in the objects: two identical pairs of shoes, one walked in for forty-four years, one never worn.

### Evidence

- Asked Hamid twice about part-time night cover; hides it from Youssef (dossier: People; interview: Q24).
- Sent Zohra down to say she was out, twice (dossier: People, Fatiha).
- The 380 dirhams became the earrings she wears daily. She turns one while naming her worst accusation, "a thief" (dossier: Secret; interview: Q10, Q24).
- "When someone goes, they're gone" (interview: Q6). Later: "maybe the sister did write. Maybe somebody didn't answer" (Q16). This is her confession, made through her mother.
- She lies about listening to every message from Amine (interview: Q4).
- She avoids Mohamed's street for 30 years, and at her till answers "Twelve dirhams fifty" (dossier: Recent events).
- Her searches assemble the trip without deciding on it: the shoes, the hammam hours, the meter, the night bus (dossier: Phone).
- She keeps a notebook of others' debts she never collects (dossier: Handbag). The debtor makes everyone else a debtor and forgives them, never herself.
- "If you're going, go… Easy to say to other people" (interview: Q9).

### Under pressure

**First move:** she turns the problem into logistics. She checks the coin jar for two-dirham pieces, takes the shoes to a cobbler, and complains about the meters to a fourth customer. She is preparing to go in a way that never requires going.

**Escalation:** she drives to Bab Boujloud, changes into her slippers, feeds the meter, and sits in the Dacia. That is the doorway she says she despises. If she does go in, she takes the wall bench, keeps the scarf on as long as she can, and refuses the attendant's scrub. She does impressions for the other women: Hamid's "dynamique", the CNSS clerk. She turns herself into a comedy so nobody looks at her back.

**Worst five minutes:** Fatiha, older, with bad knees and the white scar under her jaw, pushes up sleeves she doesn't have and offers to do her back. Khadija can't stop her in time. Fatiha traces a word, and Khadija says "Taza", on purpose this time. Or she cuts Fatiha off mid-sentence, as she does to Rachid. Either way she makes sure the other woman never finishes.

### Tells

1. She folds any paper within reach into four and presses the crease with her thumbnail, hardest when someone asks about the past.
2. She turns her left earring between finger and thumb whenever the subject is money, theft or the future.
3. In any room she chooses the seat against the wall, and she shifts sideways rather than let someone walk behind her.
4. She stops mid-task to watch anyone push their sleeves up past the elbow, and goes quiet while she does.
5. She finishes other people's sentences out loud before they can.

### Arc

**The forced choice:** in the hot room at Bab Boujloud, Fatiha is there, and Khadija is wearing the earrings.

- **If she speaks,** she has to say what the gold is. That means taking the earrings off in front of the woman she robbed and admitting the marriage was paid for, in part, with the escape. She becomes the thing she says people would be wrong to call her. The Vigilance Award opposite the sink turns into a joke at her own expense. Fatiha may not forgive her, or worse, may not care.
- **If she stays silent,** she keeps "not a thief", her marriage's founding gold and her self-image. She also becomes her mother: a woman keeping a face under a folded card.

The second blade of the dilemma is Hamid's answer. If part-time nights come through, she never has to be awake in the day, and the women's hours are daytime.

**Psychological self-revelation:** "I didn't miss the bus. I chose not to get on it, and I chose again every time the phone rang."

**Moral self-revelation:** "I made Zohra carry my lie. I took her money and her calls, and I'm letting Amine's voice note sit there the same way."

Change costs her the night and the clean count, the two things that make her someone.

**The false self-revelation** is the likeliest ending and the funniest. She calculates 190 dirhams of 1981 in today's money, puts the exact sum in an envelope folded in four, and leaves it at Mohamed's phone stall without a word. She calls the account closed. She has paid the right change and said nothing, and the lie survives because she obeyed it perfectly.

**Not changing** looks like this. The cobbler stretches the 37s, and she wears them to Hamid's cake on the 28th, limping on the bad side. The expired voucher sits in her handbag, folded in four. Amine's note stays unopened. She tells Rachid, "If you're going, go."

### What my lens can't explain

- **Her generosity.** She fed Rachid, taught his mother to sign her name and paid Amine's school fees. My lens can only call it compensation, and that is too cheap. It may simply be who she is, independent of the lie.
- **What Fatiha was to her.** The sleeves, the shared mattress, the lipstick and the decades-long celibacy may point to love rather than friendship. My lens needs only the debt. It cannot say what she lost, only that she owes it.

### Confidence

High: the material supplies a clock (21 days, the voucher's expiry), a guilty object worn in plain sight, an opponent who wants what she wanted, and a dilemma with a real cost on both sides.
