## Lacanian reading of Khadija Trabelsi ("Dija")

### Reading
Dija is the image that won't turn round. For the protagonist she is the mirror at its most unbearable: one's own body, gait and hair, seen from behind, never returning the gaze. It is a self that got on the bus and doesn't need to be looked at.

For Dija herself, the key is her **cut name**. Since 2003 she has lived as "Dija", a name with a hole where "Kha" used to be, so that Fatiha never has to say the lost girl's name. Her unspoken answer to *what does the Other want from me?* is: *to stand in the empty place without filling it.* That is why she never asks for the photograph. Seeing the original would make her a copy. Not asking keeps her a person.

She has an unusually solvent relation to debt. She broke the father's promise and pays in instalments she names herself: "That's where the bill is." Her jouissance runs through the cheerful song. Pain is turned straight into an audience, which is a comedian's symptom, not a martyr's. "I'm a museum, not a carpet" says it exactly: *look at me, don't walk on me.* She lets herself be an exhibit on her own terms.

Her desire is the leftover the hip operation can't satisfy. The surgery and the wedding dance are what she demands. Underneath that is the **sea at the end of the street**, the thing she ran to at eighteen and won't trade for Fès.

### Evidence
- She lets herself be called "Dija" and never the full name, and has never asked for a photo (dossier: secret).
- She sings more cheerfully as the pain worsens (dossier: habit).
- "Ah! There. That's where the bill is." / "I'm a museum, not a carpet" (dossier: how they speak).
- Fatiha carries the purse and never opens it (dossier: operation).
- She refuses the move to Fès, the first real disagreement in 28 years (dossier: present tension).

### Under pressure
- **First move:** a joke, aimed so that she laughs before the punchline and nobody else has to.
- **Escalation:** she sings louder and brighter.
- **Worst five minutes:** someone says "Khadija" in front of Fatiha. Dija steps into the gap at once with noise, a complaint about her hip, anything, to shield Fatiha from her own name. Then, quietly, she refuses Fès without a joke. The missing punchline is how we know.

### Tells
- She starts laughing before her joke is finished.
- She hums something jaunty at exactly the moment she grips the wall.
- She hands her purse over without looking at it, the way you trust a pocket.
- When anyone begins "Kha…", she interrupts with a louder subject.
- She turns her back to the room to face the window over the sea.

### Arc
She needs little change. What is possible is a single serious sentence, "I'm staying by the sea", said to Fatiha without comic cover. The cost is admitting she is more than the stand-in for a lost girl, and risking the friendship that rests on that role. Not changing means she goes to Fès, keeps singing, and her desire shrinks to a takchita that stays folded in a drawer.

### What my lens can't explain
Her happiness. Lacan reads contentment suspiciously, but nothing here suggests it is a defence. Her not asking for the photograph may also be plain tact rather than structure.

### Confidence
Medium: the cast sheet gives sharp speech and one decisive secret, but no interview to catch her slipping.
