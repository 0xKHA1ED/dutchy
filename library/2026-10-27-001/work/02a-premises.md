### 1. The Woman Who Stayed
Round: 1
Zohra Bennani has spent forty-one years on the night till of a 24-hour supermarket in Saint-Denis. Three weeks before she retires, she flies back to Fès to sell her dead aunt's house. Clearing the wardrobe, she finds the heels she bought at nineteen for an engagement she escaped by taking the night bus north, still in tissue and still a size too small. She carries them to the hammam of her girlhood on women's day, meaning to leave them at the door for whoever they fit, with her rental car outside Bab Boujloud and an hour on the meter. In the cold room the attendant calls out "Zohra!", and a woman with Zohra's sloped shoulders and Zohra's hitch in the left hip answers. Zohra decides this is a cousin's trick to claim a share of the house, and she follows the back deeper into the steam to catch her.
Surface question: Who is the woman answering to her name, and what is she after? (mystery)
Turn: Zohra goes out to feed the meter before the woman turns around. When she comes back for her clothes, the shoes at the door are gone, taken by someone they fit.
Invention: An inheritance "fraud" investigated entirely by following one naked back from room to room.

### 2. The Shoe Thief
Round: 1
Rachida works nights at a supermarket in the ville nouvelle and catches shoplifters by watching their hands. She brings the never-worn shoes to the hammam in a plastic bag, planning to take them straight afterwards to the stall in the Talaa Kebira whose owner swore in 1988 that they would stretch. The bag disappears from her bench in the changing room. Across the cold room she sees a woman walking away with a carrier just like it, and the woman walks exactly like Rachida. She has one hour on the meter at Bab Guissa, and she does not lose thieves.
Surface question: Will she catch the thief and get the shoes back before the meter runs out? (suspense)
Turn: Rachida loses the woman in the hot room and gives up to go and move the car. Outside, she watches a back walk away down the alley in her shoes, easy on its feet.
Invention: A professional catcher of thieves chasing her own evidence, naked, through a building with no exits she knows.

### 3. Monitor Four
Round: 1
For eleven years Saadia has watched the same shoplifter on the night security screen. It is a woman seen only from behind, who takes one small thing each week: a lipstick, a jar of olives, a scarf. Only once did she take something big, a pair of shoes from the seasonal rack. They were the twin of the pair Saadia had bought that same week one size down, because hers was gone, and has never worn. Three weeks before retiring, Saadia wants that woman caught. On women's day, under the hammam's cold tap, she sees the same shoulders, the same way of standing with one hand on the hip.
Surface question: Is this the shoplifter, and can Saadia prove it before her hour runs out? (mystery/suspense)
Turn: Saadia realises that everything the woman stole was something Saadia had picked up at her own till and put back. She leaves without the proof she came for.
Invention: A thief known for a decade only as a back on a grainy screen, whose list of stolen goods matches the cashier's own unbought wishes.

### 4. The Other Fatima Idrissi
Round: 1
At the pension office the clerk tells Fatima Idrissi, 63, night cashier, that her file is "complicated". A woman with her name, her birth year and her street in the medina is tangled in it, and the money cannot be paid until the two of them are separated. Fatima knows this other one exists. For twelve years she has been getting her post, and once she collected her shoes from a cobbler's slip, a size too small, and kept them in a box out of spite. The clerk says the other Fatima goes to the hammam on Derb Rcif on women's day. Fatima parks outside Bab Ftouh with an hour on the meter and goes in to sort out whose life is whose.
Surface question: Will she find the woman and get her pension untangled in time? (suspense, comic)
Turn: Close enough to tap the woman's shoulder, Fatima instead puts the shoes beside the other wet slippers at the door, and leaves the files tangled.
Invention: A bureaucratic identity muddle in which the only "evidence" is a stranger's shoes, kept for twelve years and never worn.

### 5. Day Shift
Round: 2
Hafida has worked nights for thirty years and sleeps with foil on her windows. This month her 2 a.m. customers keep telling her they saw her by day: laughing at the spice stall, haggling over a carpet, singing at a wedding on Derb Lamtiyine. Then a shoe-seller stops at her till and asks when she will collect the pair she ordered and half paid for. She hasn't ordered any shoes, but she goes and pays the other half to close the account, and the shoes are a size too small. A neighbour says "she" goes to the hammam on women's day. Three weeks before her days become empty, Hafida parks at Bab Boujloud with one hour and goes in to catch whoever has been living them.
Surface question: Who has been living Hafida's daytime life, and what has she been stealing? (mystery)
Turn: In the hot room Hafida is close enough to touch the woman's shoulder, and she goes out to feed the meter instead. On her first morning of retirement she closes the curtains and sleeps until dark.
Invention: The night shift as the condition that makes a double possible. While she sleeps, someone has been using her days.

### 6. The Blind Kessala
Round: 2
Old Lalla Rkia, who scrubs backs at the hammam, has lost most of her sight and knows every woman by touch. When Malika, a night cashier back after twenty years away, lies down on the mat, the glove stops and the old woman says, "Aicha. You came back. Where is what you owe me?" No matter what Malika says, the hands insist. Rkia tells her she has the same shoulder blades, the same knot at the base of the neck. Malika hears a woman humming in the hot room, sees a back with her own shoulders, and goes after this Aicha to clear her name with the only person who has touched her in years. Her meter at Bab Guissa has under an hour.
Surface question: Will Malika find Aicha and make the old woman's hands tell them apart? (suspense)
Turn: Malika gives up and pays Aicha's debt. When the coins are not enough, she adds the never-worn shoes, and lets the old woman go on believing she is the one who came back.
Invention: An attendant who identifies women by their backs, so that guilt passes by touch from one body to another.

### 7. Somebody Else's Hour
Round: 2
Mina doesn't drive. She arrives at Bab Boujloud by petit taxi and watches a woman park, feed the meter, and walk towards the hammam without ever turning her head. The woman has Mina's walk and Mina's heavy left shoulder from forty years of sliding tins across a scanner. On the back seat of her car, Mina sees the exact shoes that sit in a box under her own bed, but worn soft at the heels. The ticket on the dashboard says 11:14, so Mina, a night cashier who times everything, knows to the minute how long she has to see this woman's face. She follows her in.
Surface question: Will Mina see the stranger's face before the stranger's hour runs out? (suspense, with a clock that belongs to the double)
Turn: At 11:10 Mina walks out and feeds the woman's meter with her own coins, buying the other life more time inside, and walks away so that she will never have to see it.
Invention: A countdown that belongs to the double rather than the protagonist, read off a dashboard by a woman who has never had a car.

### 8. Lalla Hniya's Shoes
Round: 2
At twenty-two, Touria learned the night till from Lalla Hniya: how to fold receipts, how to stand, how to walk to the back store with one hand pressing her lower back. When Hniya retired, she gave Touria a pair of shoes a size too small. "When you stop standing all night, your feet come down." Then Hniya disappeared, and the rumour went that she had emptied the safe on her last night. Now Touria is three weeks from her own last night, and in the hammam she sees a back moving with Hniya's walk, which is also her own. With an hour on the meter, Touria sets out to catch the woman who stole from the store, or at least to see her face.
Surface question: Is that Lalla Hniya, and did she take the money? (mystery)
Turn: Touria learns that Hniya took nothing. She simply never came back. Touria leaves before she can see the face of a woman whose feet came down.
Invention: A double from the future rather than the past. The protagonist is the copy, and the shoes measure a life she has not yet dared to stop.

### 9. The Thirteenth Pair
Round: 3
Latifa sizes up the women in the hammam the way she sizes up customers' trolleys at 3 a.m., and her commentary is merciless and very funny: cheap soap, show-off henna, a woman who claims the hot wall as if she owns it. She fixes on one back. Its shoes at the door are a model her supermarket sold for one week only. She rang up twelve pairs and bought one herself, a size too small. The thirteenth was stolen. She follows the back with growing glee and mocks everything about it: the bra-strap groove, the hand rubbing the spine after a long night, the grunt when it sits down. All the while the meter at Bab Guissa runs down.
Surface question: Can Latifa prove this woman is the shoe thief? (dramatic irony that the listener only slowly realises they are inside)
Turn: The attendant says "Turn over, Hajja," and Latifa lets her own meter expire rather than watch the woman turn, because by now even the listener knows whose body she has been laughing at.
Invention: A cashier's habit of reading people from their trolleys turned on one naked back, so that the listener's laughter at a stranger becomes laughter at the cashier herself.

### 10. The Twins of Aisle Six
Round: 3
In 1983 Naima and her best friend Bahia were the two youngest cashiers in Fès. They dressed alike, walked alike, and were called "the twins". Together they skimmed coins from the till, and together they bought matching shoes with the money, in Bahia's size so they would match, which was a size too small for Naima. When the manager noticed the shortfall, Naima told him it was Bahia. Bahia wore her shoes to the police station, and Naima never wore hers. Forty years later, three weeks from a clean retirement, Naima sees a woman in the hammam take her bucket and her spot. The woman has her walk, and Naima starts building a case against her.
Surface question: Will Naima expose the woman who is taking what's hers? (suspense, turning to dread)
Turn: Naima recognises the back. She is about to accuse it a second time when she leaves to feed the meter, and the shoes go back in their box with their tissue paper.
Invention: A "double" made deliberately by two girls, so that the betrayal falls on the copy the protagonist made of herself.

### 11. Looking for Bruises
Round: 3
At twenty, Habiba said no to the jeweller's son. His gift had been shoes a size too small, "because a bride's feet should be dainty", and she took the supermarket night shift instead. Now 63 and three weeks from retiring alone, she overhears attendants say his wife is in the hot room. From behind, the wife has Habiba's build and Habiba's walk, as if he went and found the nearest copy. With an hour on the meter, Habiba moves closer, room by room. She tells herself she is checking on a woman she suspects of stealing her soap. What she really wants is to find a mark on that back.
Surface question: Will Habiba find proof that the woman who took her place has suffered for it? (desire, and should she want it)
Turn: The attendant's glove passes over smooth, unmarked skin and the wife laughs at something. Habiba walks out and lets the meter expire while she sits in the alley.
Invention: An investigation whose real evidence is the absence of a bruise, and a cashier hoping for a crime against the woman who said yes.

### 12. The Pickpocket Pair
Round: 3
Khadija is certain the two women working the hammam on women's day are a team. One distracts, the other lifts purses from the benches, and she has seen this kind of pair at her night till a hundred times. She follows them through the rooms with a cashier's sharp eye, and the commentary is comic. Each time she catches them, one woman is scrubbing the other's back, slowly, with a care Khadija has never been shown. The scrubbed back has Khadija's shoulders. At nineteen, Khadija and a girl called Fatiha bought shoes in a hurry, at night, for a bus to Tangier. Khadija's pair was a size too small, and she never got on the bus.
Surface question: Will Khadija catch the thieves in the act before her meter runs out? (suspense, comic, turning to dread)
Turn: Khadija watches the glove move over the back for a full minute. Then she leaves for the car before the scrubbed woman can turn and show her the face of a life she could have had.
Invention: The double is shown being loved. What Khadija disowned is a body that was touched.

## Notes for the judge
The strongest are **5 (Day Shift)** and **8 (Lalla Hniya's Shoes)**. In both, the night shift and retirement are what make the double possible, and both give the shoes a reason to be too small. In 8, Hniya's line "When you stop standing all night, your feet come down" makes the shoes a measure of the life Touria has never dared to stop. **9 (The Thirteenth Pair)** is the best route to complicity, because the listener laughs at a body that turns out to be the cashier's own. It needs exact planting to avoid feeling like a trick. **12** is the riskiest and could be extraordinary: the double carries desire rather than guilt, which suits a bitter ending. It must stay implied and concrete, a glove on a back, never a statement.
