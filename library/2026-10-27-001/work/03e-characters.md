# Characters

## Conference notes

### Convergence

**1. The back.** All seven lenses land here, which is rare.
- The Lacanian lens reads her as the one who watches from across the road and is never seen herself.
- The Jungian lens calls the back the side of herself she cannot see.
- IFS finds her dread is of Fatiha's eyes on her from behind, where she was once touched.
- The Transurfing lens reads it as a mirror: she last saw Fatiha from behind and now cannot bear to be seen from behind.
- Core dramatic names it as her psychological need.
- Defences and existential both put it at the centre of her worst five minutes.

In plain words: she has built a life in which she sees everyone and nobody sees her back. There is the till "like a roof", the seat against the wall, the deleted photo, the mirror that stops at the collarbone. Her back was where Fatiha wrote TANJA, and she read it wrong.

**2. One unpaid account, hidden inside a hundred small ones.** Every lens finds the 190 dirhams behind the five dirhams of 2017, the uncollected notebook of debts, the Vigilance Award by the sink, and the exact 200 dirhams in her purse.

In plain words: she cannot close one account, so she keeps every other account perfect. She forgives strangers their one-dirham debts and never forgives herself, and she wears the money she took in her ears.

**3. A choice told as fate, inherited from her mother.**
- Existential lens: "When someone goes, they're gone" is bad faith.
- Core dramatic: the bus was paralysis, but the two phone calls she refused were choices.
- IFS and Lacanian: the folded paper is her mother's card over the aunt's face.
- Jungian and defences: she has become her mother's copy.

In plain words: she tells it as something that happened to her. She was the one who didn't come to the phone. She half-knows it ("which side the silence started from").

**4. Approaching by errand.** Core dramatic, IFS, existential, Transurfing and Jungian all read her phone searches as movement toward Fatiha disguised as logistics: meter tariff, hammam hours, shoe stretching, the night bus.

In plain words: she gets closer by organising errands around the thing, so she never has to decide to go.

**A shared blind spot.** Every lens admits it cannot say why she stayed under the awning in 1981. **The story must not supply a reason.** Her not knowing, and the listener's, is part of the refusal. Every lens also fails to explain her generosity. We will treat it as real, not as a cover. She is genuinely kind, and that kindness is what makes the listener like her.

### Productive conflicts

**A. Pay, or be seen.**
- Core dramatic, Lacanian, defences and Transurfing all predict the same repair: settle the account. She presses the earrings into a palm, counts out the exact sum, leaves an envelope at Mohamed's stall.
- Jungian, IFS and existential all say payment changes nothing. Only being seen counts.

*What it gives the story:* the parking meter is the paying instinct made literal. The bitter ending is the paying side winning in its most absurd form. She settles with the city, a fine for staying too long, rather than with the woman whose back is turning. The earrings stay on. The account she closes is the wrong one, and it is perfectly closed.

**B. Face the double, or protect the fantasy.**
- Jungian and existential: she can only become whole by facing it.
- Lacanian: her desire is held together by staying impossible. Tangier is perfect because it was never lived. Facing it would destroy the thing that holds her up.
- Defences adds that meeting a worn, sixty-three-year-old "other life" collapses the fantasy and leaves only the debt.

*What it gives the story:* Dija is happy, in pain, funny, and ordinary. If the back turns, the refused life becomes a real woman with bad knees who doesn't need her. That may be worse than tragedy. Khadija's flight protects the dream, not just her guilt. The listener's relief at the end is the same protection, and that is the recognition.

**C. The comedy as a lid, or the comedy as who she is.**
- IFS reads the impressions and running commentary as a protector that fears where sentences end.
- Defences calls humour a mature defence.
- Core dramatic can't explain her warmth at all.

*What it gives the story:* the first half must be truly funny and her eye truly expert. The turn is not that the jokes were fake. The same eye that reads every back in the room refuses one back. Dija is also a comedian who jokes about her own body ("I'm a museum, not a carpet"). Two women who perform their bodies for laughs: one does it out loud and lives, the other does it under her breath and watches.

### Lenses chosen

1. **Lacanian.** It best explains the mechanism and the ending. The fantasy is to watch the back of a head and never be seen. The dread is the gaze from behind. The double must never turn, because a face would end the dream. It also gives the strongest image: she watches from the collarbones down, and her own mirror shows her only from the collarbones up.
2. **Core dramatic.** It supplies the clock, the forced choice, and the false settlement. The phone calls were the real choice, and the meter is the exact sum paid to the wrong creditor. It also keeps the crime costume honest: an opponent, evidence, a deadline.
3. **IFS.** It gives the most filmable behaviour and carries the comic register: the counting side, the quick mouth, and the girl on the wet tiles who still wants a word written on her back. The comedy and the turn both live here.

The other lenses stay in the iceberg. The existential reading feeds the deep question. The Jungian reading explains Hamid, the doorway and "Hajja". The Transurfing reading supplies the comic version of the balancing force.

## Khadija Amrani: character sheet

**Central contradiction:** She will follow a stranger's back through every room in the building, as long as it never turns around, because a face would prove that the only thief in the building is her.

**The mechanism in this person:**
- **Trigger:** A heavy woman ahead of her walks with the left foot landing flat. A thin woman carries the heavy woman's purse and pushes up sleeves she doesn't have.
- **Inner move:** She cannot let it be what it is, one woman caring for another and a life she didn't take. So she makes it a crime. A crime has hands, purses and evidence, and it doesn't need faces.
- **Visible behaviour:** She follows them room to room with a running commentary. Her eyes stay on the glove and the purse and never go above anyone's collarbone. She checks the time, and she keeps putting off her own scrub.
- **Cost:** The closer she gets, the more the "victim" moves like her. She has to leave before the back turns. She pays a fine instead of the truth and goes home to the shoes.

**Want:** To catch the pair with the purse in the act, out loud, the way she did in 2014 ("Lalla, your purse is in the thin one's hand"), and to get back to the car before the meter runs out.

**Need:** To let her own back be seen and touched. That means admitting the girl under the awning wasn't stopped by anything. She stayed.

**The lie she lives by:** As long as I am the one watching, I can't be the one who took something.

**Where the lie could break:** Any scene where the watched person watches back. The attendant tells her what her own back looks like. The thin woman's glove stops and her eyes come up. The heavy woman begins, slowly and on a bad hip, to turn.

**Symbolic object:** The shoes. Black flats with a gold buckle, size 37, never worn, in yellowed tissue under her bed. They are the "evidence" in her case. They were bought in a hurry, in the dark, for a life she would have had to squeeze into, and someone promised they'd stretch. Their twin stands at the door of the hammam among the wet slippers: size 38, worn soft at the heels, resoled. Khadija arrives in black Velcro pharmacy sandals, size 39. Three pairs of feet tell the whole story without a word.

Second object, secret: **the gold hoops**, worn every day and turned between finger and thumb. They pay off only at the end.

Closing echo: **the parking ticket**, folded in four, pressed flat with the thumbnail like a receipt.

**Tells** (most revealing first):
1. She never lifts her eyes above anyone's collarbone. She watches hands, purses, the glove, feet. The camera can live at her eye line and the listener will never see a face she doesn't choose to see.
2. In every room she keeps her back against the tiles and holds a towel over her shoulders until it is soaked and heavy and slides off.
3. She turns her left earring when the words "thief", "money" or "owe" come near. When the attendant says the glove will catch them and tells her to take them off, she says no.
4. She folds every paper in four, corner to corner, and presses the fold with her thumbnail: the voucher, the hammam receipt, and finally the ticket.
5. She answers a feeling with a price or a time: "Twelve dirhams fifty." "Thirty-one minutes."
6. She stops mid-sentence to watch anyone push their sleeves up past the elbow, and goes quiet while she does.
7. She finishes other people's sentences fast, and often wrongly on purpose.
8. She counts coins by touch: two-dirham pieces in the car jar, and the coins in her djellaba pocket before she undresses.

**Inner parts:**
- **The Counter.** Wants every sum closed and every minute paid for. *"Forty-four minutes. Two coins left in the jar. Safi."* This side checks the meter, sorts the coins and keeps time. It wins at the end.
- **The Detective.** Quick mouth and sharp eye. Wants a case, a joke, anything but a pause. *"Black soap from a tub. Eight dirhams at Bab Ftouh. She's paying for the tub."* This side carries the comedy and the chase.
- **The Girl on the Wet Tiles.** Fourteen years old. Wants a finger to write on her back again, slower, so this time she gets it right. *"Write it again."* She never speaks. She shows up when Khadija leans a fraction toward the slow glove, or when she can't stop watching the sleeves go up.

**Voice sheet:**
- **Rhythm and length:** Short bursts and fragments. Numbers. She corrects herself mid-line ("Twenty-one days. Nights, really."). Questions she answers herself. She never pauses long enough to be interrupted.
- **Favourite words and phrases:** "Safi." "Wallah." "Khlas." "Ya wlidi." "Aafak." "Can you believe it." "If you're going, go." "Write that." Prices, always exact. Carry the Darija words into French untranslated (*safi*, *wallah*, *khlas*, *lalla*). "Dynamique" and "process" stay in French in both versions, in Hamid's voice.
- **What she says when cornered:** a price, a time, or a count. Then a change of subject to coins or the meter, or "Next." Or she finishes your sentence for you and walks off.
- **What she never says:** Fatiha's name aloud. "Tangier", except as a joke. "Sorry." "I miss." Anything about her own back. "Love", except about a thermos of tea.
- **Humour:** Running commentary on strangers' purchases and bodies, delivered under her breath like a cashier at 3 a.m. Mock-detective deadpan. Impressions: Hamid's "dynamique", the CNSS clerk with "a voice like a door". She laughs properly rarely, and when she does, the gold molar shows.
- **Register and formality:** Plain, quick, working-class Fassi. She talks down to everyone younger and sideways to everyone her age. **For French:** she uses *tu* with every woman in the hammam, as Darija does, and lets *lalla* carry the respect. She uses *vous* only in mockery, in her Hamid impression.
- **Sample lines:**
  - "Nobody scrubs a back that slowly. Nobody. Unless the other hand has a job."
  - "Later, lalla. I'm busy. Your glove isn't going anywhere."
  - "She carries it like eggs. Like eggs! That's not her purse, I promise you."

**Pleasures and competence:**
- She spots a con in three seconds and does change in her head before the screen.
- She threads a needle without glasses and knows the Egyptian films well enough to say the lines first.
- She cracks sunflower seeds, loves slapstick, and loves the 3 a.m. lull when she can see every aisle.
- She feeds people. Twenty-seven years of other people's one-dirham debts sit uncollected in a notebook. She taught a woman to sign her name over six weeks of 2 a.m. breaks.
- She is funny, exact and fair. The listener should want her on their side at a till, and should want her to be right about these two women.

**Arc options:**
- **Break:** She stays as the back turns. She sees a tired, happy stranger's face above her own shoulders. Fatiha says "Khadija" and two women answer. She takes off one earring, lies down for the scrub, and the ticket waits.
- **Deepen:** She leaves. The ticket is already there, and she feeds the expired meter two more coins anyway, paying for time that is already lost, because paying is the only move she has.
- **Tragic (the premise's):** She looks at the clock, gathers her things, and hurries through the alleys on the bad hip. The ticket is on the windscreen and she folds it in four. The listener is relieved with her.
- **Comic:** She calls the attendant and makes her accusation. The purse is opened in front of the hot room: a clinic deposit receipt, a scrap of wedding fabric. The whole room laughs. The heavy woman laughs hardest, bent over, and never turns around.

**Never say:**
- Never say that the heavy woman looks like Khadija. Show the left shoulder, the flat left foot, the henna over the grey parting. Never describe the heavy woman from the front or show her face.
- Never say that the slowness of the glove is tenderness, or that the "crime" is care. Let the listener feel it.
- Never say that Khadija chose not to get on the bus, or why she stayed. Never name love, friendship or longing between the two girls.
- Never say she is afraid of being seen, or that she is avoiding a face.
- Never say what Fatiha traces on a back, if she traces anything.
- Fatiha's name should come late and only once, in Khadija's head, as a fact she pushes away.
- The phone calls of November 1981 and Fatiha's knowledge of the cough stay in the iceberg.
- Do not use the green butterfly hair clip in the drawer. It is on the banned list.
- **The one fact that may be revealed only at the end:** the gold hoops were bought with the tin, 190 of the 380 dirhams Fatiha's. Show it through objects and numbers only (the tin, the sum, her thumb on the hoop as she folds the ticket), never in a sentence of confession.

**Recognition setup:** The listener gets the detective's pleasure. The clues are planted as evidence of a crime, and the listener enjoys her contempt and wants the exposure:
- a purse carried from bench to bench and never opened;
- a glove moving far too slowly;
- two women working together, exactly like 2014.

The wildcard puts the listener inside her blindness. Like her, they only ever see the heavy woman from behind, so they share her vantage and her not-knowing. The turn comes when the slowness starts to read as care, and the listener realises they wanted a thief too, because a thief is easier. At the end the listener wants her to stay and see, and is also quietly glad she doesn't, glad not to see the face either. That relief is the recognition.

If direct address is used, it belongs at the clock, once, short, and never explaining: a line that lets the listener notice they have looked at a clock like that themselves.

## Supporting characters

### Fatiha Kaddour, 63

- **Central contradiction:** She has spent her life touching strangers' backs, carefully and for money, so she never has to ask the one person who didn't come to the phone.
- **Want:** To sign for a ground-floor flat in Fès before her knees decide for her. Underneath, never admitted: for this back, at last, to answer.
- **Role in the mechanism:** The accused "thief", innocent, and the only person who was actually robbed. She is the life Khadija didn't take, now come home worn. She recognised the walk in the cold room and kept working. She is the one who stops the glove and lets the back turn. It is her first act against her own forty-year silence.
- **Three tells:**
  - She pushes up sleeves she isn't wearing before she touches the glove.
  - Her scrubbing speed does not change by a second when she feels watched.
  - She carries the heavy woman's purse like something fragile and never opens it. Her eyes stay on the back she's working, never on a face.
- **Voice in two lines:** Fast, low and practical, Fès vowels worn down by Tangier, a Spanish word slipping out when it hurts. Orders that sound like kindness: "Venga, the other shoulder. I'm not paid today, don't make me ask twice."
- **Symbolic object:** Her own pair of buckle flats at the door, size 38, resoled three times, worn soft at the heels. She wears them only for the journey to Fès.

### Khadija Trabelsi ("Dija"), 64

- **Central contradiction:** She is the life Khadija refused, lived out loud, and she doesn't need Khadija at all.
- **Want:** Her hip replaced on 12 November, a dance at her granddaughter's wedding in December, and to stay by the sea in Tangier, not move to Fès.
- **Role in the mechanism:** The double, seen **only from behind, always**. She has the sloped shoulders with the left one lower, the stiff left hip, henna-auburn ends over a grey parting, and in the changing room a scarf tied at the nape. She is the "victim" of the con. She doesn't know Khadija is in the room. Fatiha once calls her by her full name, which is also Khadija's.
- **Three tells:**
  - She sings under her breath when something hurts, and the worse the pain, the brighter the song.
  - She starts laughing at her own joke two words before the end.
  - She hands her purse to Fatiha without looking at it, the way you trust a pocket. When "Khadija" is said aloud, she turns a full second late.
- **Voice in two lines:** Loud, warm, Taza vowels intact, complaining about her body like a comedian working a room. "Slowly, Fatiha, slowly. I'm a museum, not a carpet." When the glove reaches the bad hip: "Ah! There. That's where the bill is."
- **Symbolic object:** The clinic deposit receipt inside the purse Khadija thinks is loot. It is a receipt, the kind of paper Khadija folds in four.
- **Caution:** She teaches nothing and delivers no lesson. She is not a free spirit. She is a supervisor with a bad hip and a happy life, and her happiness is real.

### Souad Ben Salah, 52

- **Central contradiction:** She is sending her daughter away with all her strength, and her hands keep reaching to hold on.
- **Want:** Her daughter on the October train to Rabat, and her own mouth shut on the platform.
- **Role in the mechanism:** The witness and the pressure. She is the paid scrub that keeps coming back, offering Khadija the one thing she has refused since 1981: a hand on her own back. She delivers the one line that lands, in the hot room: "You've been watching her back for an hour. Do you want me to tell you what yours looks like?" She is also one of Khadija's 4 a.m. regulars, "the finger", 2 dirhams in the notebook. Khadija knows her only by her hand. Souad has known Khadija all along and decided not to say.
- **Three tells:**
  - She keeps reappearing in the doorway with the same bucket and towel, arms folded, unhurried.
  - She tucks tips into the knot of her headscarf and counts them by touch.
  - She lays the hand with the missing fingertip flat on a back, openly, never hiding it.
- **Voice in two lines:** Direct, loud enough to cross three rooms, and saying less than she knows. "Your scrub is paid, lalla. Lie down when you're ready. I'm not going anywhere."
- **Symbolic object:** The hand with the missing fingertip, the one thing Khadija might recognise. Possibly also a two-dirham coin, the debt, which is also the only coin the meter takes. This is an option for the plot stage.
- **Caution:** She must not become the wise woman delivering the moral. She says one line, does not explain it, and has her own unfinished business at a station.

**Off-stage, in the iceberg:**
- **Naïma** gave her the voucher and has no idea what she gave.
- **Mohamed Kaddour's** sentence at the till, "She likes the hammam", is why Khadija is here at all.
- **Youssef's** thermos is waiting at home.
- **Amine's** voice note has sat unopened for nine days.
- **Zohra** carried the lie down to the phone twice.
- **Hamid** ordered a cake.

None needs to appear. Any one can be a single concrete detail.

## Relationship map

**Khadija and Fatiha**
- *Who needs whom to stay the same:* Khadija needs Fatiha to be a thief, so she can stay the detective and not become the defendant. Fatiha needs Khadija to stay a back she can recognise without naming, so she never has to say "you were home".
- *What each sees that the other can't:* Fatiha knew the walk from behind in one glance, the thing Khadija has never seen about herself. Khadija sees Fatiha's hands perfectly and reads them as a con, blind to thirty years of trade.
- *Whose habits clash:* Both answer pain with work and keep their eyes on hands.
- *Where the conflict comes from:* A tin and a phone. Nobody is the villain. One froze at nineteen. The other turned a robbery into a "gift" so she wouldn't have to be someone who was left.

**Khadija and Dija**
- *Who needs whom:* Khadija needs Dija never to turn. Dija needs nothing from Khadija, which is the cruellest thing a double can be.
- *What each sees:* Khadija sees her own body and calls it a victim. Dija sees nothing.
- *Whose habits clash:* Both make jokes about their bodies. Dija does it out loud to a room. Khadija does it under her breath to nobody.

**Fatiha and Dija**
- *Who needs whom:* Fatiha needs Dija to stay "Dija" and never the full name. Dija needs Fatiha to let her stay by the sea.
- *What each sees:* The unopened purse is their trust.
- *Where the conflict comes from:* Fès against Tangier, a brother's street against the sea. It is their first real disagreement in twenty-eight years, carried quietly into the steam.

**Khadija and Souad**
- *Who needs whom:* Khadija needs Souad to stay a hand at 4 a.m. and a scrub that can be put off. Souad needs nothing from Khadija except the chance to do her job.
- *What each sees:* Souad sees what Khadija is doing from the first room, and sees the back Khadija hides. Khadija might, once, recognise the finger.
- *Where the conflict comes from:* Souad is living the same choice now, keep the girl or let her go, and has chosen to let go.

**The relationship the story turns on:** Khadija and the heavy woman's back, reached only through Fatiha's glove.

## The deep question

What life did you refuse, and how much have you paid since then so you'd never have to watch someone else live it?
