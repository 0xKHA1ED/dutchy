## Psychodynamic reading of Khadija Amrani

### Reading

Khadija runs her inner life like a till: everything counted, folded, balanced, except one drawer that has been short since 1981. Her main defence is **displacement into accounting**. The 190 dirhams she owes Fatiha become the five dirhams of 2017 that "bother me more than they should". The theft becomes a Vigilance Award hung opposite the sink. The debt becomes a notebook of other people's debts she never collects. That last is **undoing**: she forgives strangers the debt she cannot settle herself. Over all of it runs **humour**, a genuinely mature defence, and the impressions are how she stays likeable while letting no one finish a sentence.

Her attachment is **dismissing on the surface, fearful underneath**. At nineteen she approached and then froze across the road: packed bag, shoebox, never boarded. She stood in the doorway, and the doorway-stander is now the person she despises (interview: Q9). Her style is obsessive-compulsive, with a depressive undertow of guilt.

On the mechanism: Fatiha is less an uncanny stranger than a **known double kept behind a folded card**, exactly as her mother covered the aunt (dossier: §2). Khadija has become her mother's copy, and half-knows it (interview: Q16). Her dread is concrete. It is of being seen *from behind*, the place where Fatiha wrote TANJA. The uncanny moment the library file predicts will land differently. It will come from meeting a 63-year-old Fatiha with bad knees, the "other life" just as worn as her own. That collapses the fantasy and leaves only the debt.

### Evidence

- Five dirhams named as her unforgiven fault, followed by folding the cup sleeve and "Something else, yes" (interview: Q17).
- Touches her earring at "a thief, let's say" (interview: Q10). The earrings are the Nido money (dossier: §11).
- Debt notebook, never collected (dossier: §7; interview: Q21).
- Stood under the awning and watched the bus leave; sent Zohra to refuse Fatiha's two phone calls (dossier: scene B, §10).
- Childhood told in three-word bursts, then a lapse: "which side the silence started from" (interview: Q5, Q6, Q16).
- Gives Tarek as first love, then the friend slips out: "That's not what you mean… Write Tarek" (interview: Q18).
- Fear of being seen from behind; the deleted photo; no full-length mirror (interview: Q13; dossier: §6).
- Searches for stretching the shoes, hammam hours, the night bus to Tangier (dossier: §7).

### Under pressure

**First move:** she goes practical. She pays a price, counts, gets her hands busy. To Mohamed Kaddour she said only "Twelve dirhams fifty" and scanned the bread twice (dossier: §12).

**Escalation:** displacement gets louder. Complaints about the parking meters spread to three customers. Glasses are washed while already clean. She asks Hamid about night work. She lets Amine's voice note sit unopened. The withdrawal she used in 1981 returns in its old form: she sends someone else to the phone, takes the long way round, keeps the day out.

**Worst five minutes:** this is likely the hammam at Bab Boujloud, with Fatiha in the room.
1. She feeds the meter with exact two-dirham coins from the jar.
2. Inside, she takes the bench against the wall.
3. She recognises Fatiha by her hands first, before her face (dossier: §4). It might be the sleeves pushed up on bare arms, or the white scar under the jaw.
4. She makes a remark about the price of the scrub, then does a quick impression for the attendant. The joke buys her twenty seconds.
5. She gathers her bucket and cup and gets as far as the doorway, then stands in it, letting the heat out, being exactly the person she despises.
6. She returns and, without a sentence, unscrews the gold hoops and presses them into Fatiha's palm. It is a cashier's repair: the account settled, the feeling left unspoken.
7. If Fatiha refuses, or simply says "Khadija", Khadija has no move left. She sits down with her back exposed.

### Tells

- When a conversation comes near the past, she folds whatever paper is nearest into four and presses the crease with her thumbnail.
- She turns one gold earring between finger and thumb whenever money, theft or honesty comes up.
- She answers a personal question with a price, a number or a count ("Twenty-one nights").
- To get out of a serious moment she stands, opens her palms and does Hamid's high voice saying "dynamique", and everyone laughs.
- In any room she sits with her back to a wall, and she shifts her chair if someone walks behind her.

### Arc

**Possible change:** she lets someone see and touch her back. That could be the attendant's scrub, or Fatiha tracing a word, with Khadija guessing it right this time. For that to happen she would have to stop paying instead of speaking. She would have to say the word "Fatiha" aloud, perhaps first to Zohra, who has held the secret for 44 years without a word.

**The cost:** two identities go at once. She loses "not a thief, whatever else" and "Tarek, write Tarek". She would have to admit the love and the theft together. Once she can speak, she can no longer be the woman who stands in the night, watching the whole shop from her till like a roof.

**Not changing:** the voucher expires unused, folded in four. She takes part-time nights. The shoes go back in the tissue. In ten years she is her mother, with a square of card over one face in the photograph.

### What my lens can't explain

- **Why she didn't board.** The material never says, and defence theory can only guess among fear of leaving, family duty, and fear of what she felt. The lens predicts the freeze but cannot say what she was protecting.
- **Why she wants the shoes to fit now.** The "élargir" search and the trying-on suggest something forward-looking: a wish to walk in them, not just to settle a debt. That points to hope rather than defence, which my reading underweights.

### Confidence

High. The defences and the avoidance pattern recur consistently across dossier and interview, but the core motive behind the 1981 freeze stays opaque to this lens.
