## Pitch intent

You spend the first half enjoying a sharp-tongued cashier who has caught someone at something: a woman in the steam who has her walk, her shoulders, maybe her name. Like her, you want the thief exposed. Somewhere in the hot room the chase stops being funny. You realise she isn't trying to catch the woman so much as trying not to see her face. When the meter runs out and she chooses the parking ticket over the truth, you feel the small, familiar relief of not looking, and you remember a life of your own that you kept in a box, unworn.

## How the ingredients meet

A hammam is the one public place where women are seen almost naked, and still nobody sees their own back. That makes the wildcard the engine of the double. The other woman is only ever seen from behind, in the way the cashier has never seen herself. The crime costume gives the listener a clean surface question: who is she, what did she take, will she be caught? The uncanny does its work underneath, in plain detail: buckets claimed by territory, the black soap, the attendant's rough glove, wet slippers lined up at the door. The night-shift cashier is a professional watcher of strangers' hands and strangers' purchases. She is about to stop watching for good, and retirement is a door she cannot see past. The shoes, a size too small and never worn, are the evidence in this "case." Were they bought for a life she would have had to squeeze into? The parking meter belongs in the ville nouvelle or just outside a gate like Bab Boujloud, beyond the car-free medina. That gives her a real, petty reason to leave before the reveal, and the bitter ending can hang on choosing the fine. Keep the double ordinary: a living woman, no ghosts and no mechanics. The premise stage should answer three questions. What disowned possibility does the back carry: the woman who left Fès, the one who married the other man, the one who said yes to something at twenty? Who is really the criminal here, and could the cashier be the one taking something? What does the night shift let her avoid that a day in the steam won't? For B1, keep the hammam vocabulary to five or six concrete things, and let the bodies and the clock do the work.
