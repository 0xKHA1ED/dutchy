## Overview
**Structure:** A countdown inside a closed building. Each chapter is a room of the hammam: cold, then warm, then hot, then the door and the alleys. Each room is hotter than the last and brings Khadija closer to the back. The meter runs outside the walls, and every chapter ends on a minute-mark and a step closer to the truth.

Register by chapter:
- Chapters 1–2 are comic.
- The comedy cracks at the end of Chapter 2.
- Chapter 3 turns tragic.
- Chapter 4 is bitter, with one half-laugh left in it.

**Chapters:** 4. Approximate total 4,100 words: Ch1 ~1,050, Ch2 ~1,000, Ch3 ~1,050, Ch4 ~1,000.

**Refrain:**
- **Main phrase refrain:** the paired exchange *"Your scrub is paid, lalla."* / *"Later."*
  - It returns in 1.4 and 2.3.
  - In 3.2 it comes back without the "Later", because Khadija can't produce it.
  - Its last return is inverted in 4.4. Nobody offers her anything. A ticket demands payment, and she reaches for coins to pay for time already gone.
- **Structural refrain:** the minute count that closes every chapter: 1.4 *Forty-three*, 2.4 *Twenty-four*, 3.4 *Nine*, 4.3 *−4* (printed on the ticket).
- **Two short echo pairs:**
  - *"Same price for one minute or one day. Can you believe it."* (1.1). In 4.3 it goes unsaid, and the sum is read aloud instead.
  - *"If you're going, go."* (1.2 → 4.1).

**Symbolic object:** the shoes.
- 1.2: the black buckle flats at the door; her joke about her own size-37s "under the bed, still in the tissue"; her Velcro 39s.
- 2.1: her size-guessing game. The thin woman is "thirty-eight, narrow", the heavy woman "thirty-nine."
- 4.1: three sizes in one doorway.

**Secret object:** the gold hoops.
- 1.1: thumb to the hoop at a debt (the notebook).
- 1.3: turned at "thief."
- 2.3: "Take them off." / "No." / "Old gold." / *"A hundred and ninety."*
- 3.1: turned at "paid for."
- 4.4: the thumb stays.

**Closing echo:** the ticket, folded in four. The fold runs from the voucher (1.1) to the receipt (1.2) to the ticket (4.4).

**One sound per place:**
- Bab Boujloud: the clunk of each coin into the meter.
- Cold room: buckets scraping on tile, echoing.
- Warm room: the tap filling buckets, always running.
- Hot room: water hissing on the hot floor, and drips falling from the dome.
- Alleys: a porter's *"Balak!"* and hooves on stone.

**Hammam vocabulary (B1, keep to these six):** *le seau*, *le savon noir*, *le gant* (*kessa* once, for colour), *les claquettes*, *le carrelage*, *la serviette*. The rooms are simply *la salle froide / tiède / chaude*.

**Upstream fixes carried from the first pass:**
- Dija's joke has no "Fatiha" in it.
- Khadija's correction to Souad is "Twenty-six minutes." The arithmetic: she parks at 60, walks 11 minutes downhill and arrives at 49, spots the pair at about 46, and Souad speaks at about 20.

**Fair-play fixes applied on this pass:**
1. "Dija" is moved out of 2.1 and into 2.4 (*"Yes, Dija."*). The double now builds as an itch in 2.1, a doubt in 2.2, and the name at the chapter's end.
2. In 2.3 Khadija answers "Old gold" with a price: *"A hundred and ninety."*
3. Earring at a debt is planted in 1.1, so "owe" is now earned. "Under the bed" is planted in 1.2. All "Fatiha's glove" claims are corrected to "any glove on her back."
4. Optional fix taken: the clock over the door is planted in 1.3.
5. Optional fix taken: one light northern marker for the pair (the sea), in Dija's mouth, in 2.2.

## Chapter 1: Like Eggs
**Exit hook:** *Forty-three minutes.* She sits down one bench behind a back.

### Scene 1.1
**Where and when:** Outside Bab Boujloud, mid-morning on women's day. The meter at the kerb. Minute 60.

**Who:** Khadija, alone.

**What happens:**
- She feeds the meter two-dirham coins from the coffee jar in the cup holder.
- The sticker reads: two-dirham coins only, one hour maximum, flat fine. She reads it like a price label: *"Same price for one minute or one day. Can you believe it."*
- One coin is left over, and the meter refuses more than an hour. She pockets it and counts it by touch.
- Two dirhams calls up "the finger": a 4 a.m. regular she knows only by a hand with a short fingertip. *Bread and a lighter. Two dirhams short. March. Still in the notebook.* Her thumb goes to her left hoop. No comment.
- Twenty-one days before she leaves that notebook behind. *"Nights, really."*
- Optional: one Hamid impression in mocking *vous* ("a cake for your *dynamique*, Madame Amrani").
- A price joke: for the fine you could take the night bus to Tangier. This is the only "Tangier" in her mouth all story.
- She takes out Naïma's voucher, folds it in four, corner to corner, and presses the fold with her thumbnail. *"Eleven minutes. Downhill."* She sets off.

**The turn:** From a woman who owns every minute and every coin to a woman walking down into a place where she will have to undress, carrying something she didn't pay for.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Prices and times as answers.
- Coins counted by touch.
- The fold in four.
- Self-correction ("Nights, really").
- Thumb to the hoop at a debt.
- *Safi*, *wallah*, "Can you believe it."

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious opening is backstory about retirement or loneliness, a look at the gate. Instead it is logistics and jokes, and the only interior is arithmetic.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - She is three weeks from retirement and here on a gift voucher.
  - Fatiha Kaddour, the girlhood friend who left for Tangier in 1981, is in this hammam today. With her is Khadija "Dija" Trabelsi, her companion of 28 years.
  - The fine is flat, so the clock is meaningless as a reason to hurry.
  - The hoops were bought with Fatiha's half of the tin.
- **Character believes:** It is an errand: a scrub, an hour, back to the car.
- **Listener believes:** A funny, exact, prickly working woman on a day out. Low-stakes comedy.
- **Clue planted:**
  - The flat-fine sticker.
  - "Eleven minutes. Downhill."
  - The last coin pocketed.
  - The finger, two dirhams, in the notebook.
  - Thumb to the hoop at a debt.
  - Tangier as a bus-price joke.
  - The fold.

### Scene 1.2
**Where and when:** The hammam door and changing area. Minutes 49 → 46.

**Who:** Khadija; a woman dawdling at the door; the desk woman.

**What happens:**
- A woman stands half in, half out of the doorway, talking on her phone. *"If you're going, go,"* says Khadija, and squeezes past.
- She lines up her black Velcro pharmacy sandals, size 39, among the wet slippers. Beside them stand black flats with a gold buckle, resoled so often the heel is a little stack.
- Under her breath: *"Same buckle as mine. Mine are thirty-seven. Under the bed, still in the tissue. They said they'd stretch."*
- She pays the entry fee and folds the receipt in four, thumbnail on the fold. She counts the coin in her djellaba pocket by touch.
- She undresses with her back to the tiles, towel over her shoulders at once.
- She unpins her scarf: henna two weeks old, grey showing at the parting. The small mirror stops at her collarbones. She gives it a cashier's nod.
- To nobody: *"Sixty-three years. Look, don't touch. Like the museum at Batha."*
- She crosses the cold room to the far wall. It is the only time she shows the room her back. Only at the wall does she turn.

**The turn:** From covered and in control, to almost naked in a public room, to safe again with her back against the tiles.

**Tells and voice used:**
- "If you're going, go."
- The fold.
- Coin-counting.
- Back to the tiles, towel on.
- Body comedy under her breath.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she lingers over the twin shoes, frowning. Instead they get a throwaway joke, and the listener files it as colour.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - The flats are Fatiha's, size 38, twins of Khadija's 37s, bought together for the 1981 journey.
  - Fatiha sees Khadija's back and walk crossing the room and knows them in one glance.
- **Character believes:** It is a common model, with a funny story attached to her own pair.
- **Listener believes:** A charming detail about a woman who buys things and never uses them.
- **Clue planted:**
  - "If you're going, go."
  - The buckle flats.
  - The 37s under the bed, "said they'd stretch."
  - The Velcro 39s.
  - Her henna over grey.
  - The mirror at the collarbones.
  - The "museum" joke.
  - Her exposed back, crossing.

### Scene 1.3
**Where and when:** The cold room. Minutes 46 → 44. Buckets scrape and echo.

**Who:** Khadija; the thin woman (Fatiha) and the heavy woman (Dija), both seen at Khadija's eye line. Dija is seen only from behind.

**What happens:**
- Within three minutes the Detective has a case. A thin woman carries a heavy woman's purse from bench to bench and never opens it. She keeps pushing up sleeves she isn't wearing. *"She carries it like eggs. Like eggs! That's not her purse, I promise you."*
- The commentary runs on:
  - Black soap scooped from a tub: *"Eight dirhams at Bab Ftouh. She's paying for the tub."*
  - Which bucket belongs to whom.
- She recalls 2014, her proudest catch: *"Lalla, your purse is in the thin one's hand."* That got her the Vigilance Award, framed by her sink. At "thief" she turns her left hoop.
- The thin woman's head lifts once toward Khadija's wall, then goes back down. *"See? A thief always checks for the guard."*
- Khadija keeps her eyes on hands and feet. *There's a clock over every door in this place. She doesn't need to look; she's never once been wrong.*
- Everyone is described collarbone-down.

**The turn:** From a day out to a case. The comedy has a target, and the listener is recruited.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Eyes never above the collarbone.
- Detective deadpan.
- Exact prices.
- Earring at "thief."
- She goes quiet watching the sleeves.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she glances at faces and something nags at her. Instead she never looks up. The glance *at her* is read as guilt.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - The purse is carried out of trust. It holds a clinic deposit receipt for Dija's hip.
  - Fatiha has recognised Khadija.
  - The only stolen goods in the building are in Khadija's ears.
- **Character believes:** A two-woman purse con like 2014, and she is the guard.
- **Listener believes:** The same, and enjoying it.
- **Clue planted:**
  - "Like eggs."
  - The purse never opened.
  - Phantom sleeves.
  - The glance at the wall.
  - Earring at "thief."
  - The clock over the door, which she "doesn't need to look" at.

### Scene 1.4
**Where and when:** The cold room doorway, then the threshold of the warm room. Minutes 44 → 43.

**Who:** Khadija; Souad (her first appearance); Fatiha and Dija.

**What happens:**
- Souad stands in the doorway with a bucket and towel, arms folded. *"Your scrub is paid, lalla."*
- Khadija doesn't look up: *"Later, lalla. I'm busy. Your glove isn't going anywhere."*
- Souad lingers and watches. Khadija sees only the bucket.
- The heavy woman walks ahead toward the warm room, her left foot landing flat. The thin woman follows with the purse.
- Khadija checks the time in her head, rises, and takes the bench directly behind them. *Forty-three minutes.*

**The turn:** From watcher at a distance to follower. She chooses to go deeper, and refuses a touch she has already been given.

**Tells and voice used:**
- "Later, lalla."
- Time as an answer.
- Back to the wall.
- Eyes on the bucket.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she takes the scrub and drops the case. Instead she defers it and follows, and the refrain is born.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - Souad is "the finger" and has recognised her.
  - Dija's flat left foot is Khadija's own walk.
- **Character believes:** The scrub can wait. The case can't.
- **Listener believes:** The chase is on, and Souad is local colour.
- **Clue planted:** "Your scrub is paid" / "Later." The left foot landing flat.

## Chapter 2: A Museum, Not a Carpet
**Exit hook:** *Twenty-four minutes.* She rises on her hip and follows them into the heat.

### Scene 2.1
**Where and when:** The warm room. Minutes 43 → 37. The tap fills buckets endlessly.

**Who:** Khadija; Fatiha (hands, feet, voice); Dija (from behind only).

**What happens:**
- Khadija claims a bucket directly behind the pair, back to the tiles, towel on.
- The glove starts on the heavy woman's back, far too slowly. *"Nobody scrubs a back that slowly. Nobody. Unless the other hand has a job."*
- From behind she sees a henna-auburn line over a grey parting, and a left shoulder lower than the right. A back. Only a back.
- Her till game, guessing shoe sizes from bare feet: the thin woman *"thirty-eight, narrow"*; the heavy woman *"thirty-nine, like a boat."* She says nothing about her own.
- Fatiha pushes up her phantom sleeves before taking the glove. Khadija stops mid-sentence and goes quiet.
- Fatiha, low and practical, with **no name**: *"Venga, the other shoulder. I'm not paid today, don't make me ask twice."*
- Khadija hears a con artist's wink: *"Not paid today. Write that."*

**The turn:** The case gets stronger, since a slow glove is a classic distraction. But the watched pair now have voices, and one of them a working life ("not paid today").

**Tells and voice used:**
- Eyes at feet and hands.
- The sleeves silence.
- "Write that."
- The size game as cashier competence.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she notices the heavy woman's hair is like hers. Instead she notices nothing. Only the listener holds both henna lines.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - The slowness is care for a bad hip.
  - Fatiha scrubs backs for a living in Tangier.
  - "Venga" is northern Spanish slipping out.
  - Dija's feet are Khadija's size.
- **Character believes:** The slow hand is a decoy.
- **Listener believes:** Mostly with Khadija. A careful listener feels an itch at the mirrored henna and "thirty-nine", but it is not yet a certainty.
- **Clue planted:**
  - The heavy woman's henna over grey.
  - Lower left shoulder.
  - 38 (which links to the flats at the door).
  - 39.
  - "Venga."
  - "Not paid today."

### Scene 2.2
**Where and when:** The warm room. Minutes 37 → 34.

**Who:** Khadija, Dija, Fatiha.

**What happens:**
- The glove passes a sore place. Dija, loud and warm, works the room: *"Slowly, slowly. I'm a museum, not a carpet."* She starts laughing two words before the end. Two other women laugh.
- Optional, one line, Dija to the room: *"At home I just walk into the sea and let it do this."*
- Khadija laughs under her breath, gold molar almost showing. Then she stops.
- She doubles down: *"A woman who jokes like that doesn't watch her purse. That's why they picked her."* She shifts to watch the purse at the thin woman's knee.

**The turn:** From contempt to an unwelcome kinship she won't name. She hardens the case to cover it.

**Tells and voice used:**
- A rare real laugh, cut off.
- Finishing the victim's story for her.
- Detective deadpan.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she warms to Dija or recognises her joke out loud. Instead she stops laughing and makes the victim's charm part of the crime.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** Dija makes the body joke out loud to a room. Khadija made it (1.2) to nobody.
- **Character believes:** The victim is easy prey.
- **Listener believes:** Laughs, then catches the "museum" echo. This is the first real doubt that this is only a crime story.
- **Clue planted:**
  - The museum echo.
  - Dija's laugh before the punchline (it returns as song in Ch3).
  - Optional: the sea, which marks the pair as from the coast and pairs with the 1.1 Tangier joke.

### Scene 2.3
**Where and when:** The warm room. Minutes 34 → 29.

**Who:** Khadija, Souad.

**What happens:**
- Souad returns with the same bucket and towel. *"Your scrub is paid, lalla."*
- *"The glove will catch your earrings. Take them off."* — *"No."* Khadija turns the left one.
- Souad: *"Old gold. They don't make that yellow now."* Khadija, flat, still turning it: *"A hundred and ninety."*
- Optional: one line of Souad's own life, about tips for her daughter's October train to Rabat, counted in the headscarf knot.
- Khadija presses her last two-dirham coin into Souad's palm without looking at the hand. Her eyes stay on the purse. *"Later, aafak. Go and do someone who wants it."*
- Souad tucks the coin into her headscarf knot, above Khadija's eye line, and goes without a word.

**The turn:** From refusing a gift to paying to refuse it. She has spent the meter's only usable coin to keep a hand off her back.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Earring at money.
- A price as the answer to a feeling.
- "No" as a full sentence.
- "Later, aafak."
- Eyes never on the hand she pays.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she sees the fingertip as the coin passes. Instead her eyes are on the purse. The one hand she doesn't watch is the one she pays. Recognition is held for 3.2.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - The hoops were bought with 190 dirhams that were Fatiha's, half of a tin of 380.
  - Souad already owes Khadija exactly this coin.
- **Character believes:** She has tipped an attendant to go away. "A hundred and ninety" is just what the gold cost.
- **Listener believes:** A comic bribe, a stubborn "No", and a cashier who prices everything, even her own earrings.
- **Clue planted:**
  - "Take them off." / "No."
  - "Old gold."
  - "A hundred and ninety" (it snaps onto the 4.2 flash).
  - The coin leaves the pocket.

### Scene 2.4
**Where and when:** The warm room, then rising toward the hot room. Minutes 29 → 24.

**Who:** Khadija, Fatiha, Dija.

**What happens:**
- The glove moves lower, slower. Without knowing it, Khadija leans a fraction off the wall.
- A sensory flicker: wet tiles at fourteen, a fingertip moving slowly on her own back, letters she guessed. No word, no person, no meaning.
- The Counter cuts in: *"Thirty minutes. Twenty-nine."*
- Her towel has grown heavy, and she hitches it up.
- Dija stands and holds her purse out behind her without looking. The thin woman takes it like eggs. Dija: *"Careful with that."* The thin woman: *"Yes, Dija."*
- Khadija's commentary misses a beat. There is no comment, only the gap.
- They go toward the hot room, Dija slow on a stiff left hip. Khadija rises, one hand going to her own hip, and follows. *Twenty-four minutes.*

**The turn:** From the chase as sport to something older pulling at her body. The heavy woman now has a name, and the name is half of Khadija's. She covers it with a number and keeps going.

**Tells and voice used:**
- The Girl on the Wet Tiles (leaning toward the glove).
- The Counter.
- The towel.
- A time as the answer to a feeling.
- A missed beat in the commentary.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is a full flashback, or Khadija reacting to "Dija." Instead the memory is three physical details cut off by a number, and the name gets only silence.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - Fatiha once wrote on Khadija's back in a place like this. The word stays in the iceberg.
  - "Dija" is short for Khadija.
- **Character believes:** Nothing she will admit. "Twenty-nine."
- **Listener believes:** Something is under the case. Listeners who hear "Dija" as Khadija are now unsettled, not certain. The fingertip memory is not yet tied to the thin woman.
- **Clue planted:**
  - The fingertip on a back.
  - The purse handed over without looking.
  - "Careful with that."
  - "Dija."
  - The stiff left hip.
  - Khadija's hand on her own hip.

## Chapter 3: Two Women Answer
**Exit hook:** *Nine minutes.* Her eyes rise for the first time in the story, to the clock, while a shoulder turns.

### Scene 3.1
**Where and when:** The hot room. Minutes 24 → 21. Water hisses on the floor, and drops fall from the dome.

**Who:** Khadija, Fatiha, Dija.

**What happens:**
- At her eye height the steam turns the room into shapes.
- Her towel soaks through and slides onto the tiles. She reaches for it once: heavy, useless. She leaves it. Her back is bare to the room.
- Fatiha sets the purse on the marble. Dija, over her shoulder: *"Careful with that, my new hip's in there. It's paid for."* Khadija turns her earring at "paid."
- The Detective tries: *"They always say paid for."* It comes out weaker.
- The thin woman's hand goes under the heavy woman's elbow and lowers her onto the hot marble.

**The turn:** From certain to unsure. The "loot" has been described, innocently, and her back is uncovered.

**Tells and voice used:**
- The towel sliding.
- Earring at "paid."
- The Detective losing strength.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she admits she was wrong and drops the case. Instead she keeps it alive with a weaker line, because the case keeps her eyes low.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** The purse holds the clinic deposit for the hip operation on 12 November.
- **Character believes:** She tells herself it is still a con. Part of her knows otherwise.
- **Listener believes:** The purse is probably innocent. The fun is draining away, and the case begins to look like a cover.
- **Clue planted:** "My new hip's in there. It's paid for." The bare back. Earring at "paid."

### Scene 3.2
**Where and when:** The hot room. About minute 20.

**Who:** Khadija, Souad.

**What happens:**
- Souad sits beside her and lays her hand flat on her own knee, at Khadija's eye height. *"You've been watching her back for an hour. Do you want me to tell you what yours looks like?"*
- Khadija, at once: *"Twenty-six minutes. Not an hour."*
- Then she sees the hand: the missing fingertip, laid flat, not hidden. *The finger. Two dirhams, in the notebook.* The coin in the knot was owed to her already.
- No "Later" comes. Souad waits, then stands: *"Your scrub is paid. I'm not going anywhere."* She goes.

**The turn:** From watcher to watched. The woman she knew only as a hand has known her face all along, and has seen her back.

**Tells and voice used:**
- A number when cornered.
- Recognising someone by the hand.
- The refrain with "Later" missing.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she breaks, confesses, or lies down. Instead she corrects the arithmetic and is silenced by a debt.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** Souad has known her from the till for years and chose not to say. Khadija has paid every creditor except the one.
- **Character believes:** She is caught, and doesn't know at what.
- **Listener believes:** Laughs at "Twenty-six", then feels it turn. The question shifts from "is she a thief?" to "what is Khadija avoiding?"
- **Clue planted:** The finger (paying off 1.1 and 2.3). The offer of being seen, refused in silence.

### Scene 3.3
**Where and when:** The hot room. Minutes 19 → 11.

**Who:** Khadija, Fatiha, Dija.

**What happens:**
- The glove moves toward the bad hip. Dija sings under her breath, bright, and brighter as the glove nears. *"Ah! There. That's where the bill is."*
- The slowness now plainly reads as waiting. Khadija's commentary has stopped.
- Around minute 12 the thin woman says *"Khadija,"* low, to the back she is working. Two women answer:
  - the heavy woman, a full second late, *"Na'am?"*, without turning;
  - Khadija, whose mouth is already open. She closes it on nothing.
- A name rises in her head, once: *Fatiha.* She pushes it back like a coin across a counter. *Next.*

**The turn:** From a case about strangers to a name she knows. The "thief" has a face she has refused for forty years.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Dija's song brightening with pain.
- Khadija's open mouth.
- "Next."
- The coin pushed across a counter, as a gesture of the mind.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that Fatiha turns and says the name *to her*, a reunion. Instead it is spoken to the back. Who it was meant for is never settled, and Dija answers.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** Fatiha always says "Dija" (2.4). Saying the full name is her first act against forty years of silence, aimed at both women.
- **Character believes:** She will not think it.
- **Listener believes:**
  - The thin woman is from Khadija's past.
  - The heavy woman is a version of her, the life that went (the shoes, the sea and the Tangier joke align).
  - Dread: will the face be seen?
- **Clue planted:** "Dija" → "Khadija." *Fatiha*, once. The song as pain.

### Scene 3.4
**Where and when:** The hot room. Minutes 11 → 9.

**Who:** Khadija, Fatiha, Dija.

**What happens:**
- The glove stops. The thin woman's hands come to rest.
- The heavy woman begins, slowly, on the stiff left hip, to turn. The lower left shoulder comes round first.
- Khadija's eyes rise above collarbone height for the first time in the story. They go past the turning shoulder and up the wall, to the clock over the door she has "never needed to look at." *Nine minutes.*
- **Direct address (optional, at most once):** one short second-person line just before the minute-mark, letting the listener notice their own clock. Cut it if it reads as commentary.

**The turn:** From the back turning to her eyes lifting, to a number. The Counter wins.

**Tells and voice used:** The eyes rise for the only time, and only to a number. The Counter.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is to reveal the face: mirror, reunion or stranger. Instead the camera follows her eyes to the clock. No face appears in the story.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - Nine minutes cannot get her back uphill on that hip ("Eleven minutes. Downhill").
  - The fine is flat, so the meter is permission, not a reason.
  - She has never needed the clock before.
- **Character believes:** She has to go, or she'll get a ticket.
- **Listener believes:** Relief. She is going, and we don't have to see the face either.
- **Clue planted:** None new. This cashes in the eye-line rule and the 1.3 clock.

## Chapter 4: Same Price
**Exit hook:** None. The story ends: *Her thumb went to the left hoop, and stayed there.*

### Scene 4.1
**Where and when:** The door and changing area. Minutes 9 → 7.

**Who:** Khadija; Dija's voice through the wall.

**What happens:**
- She comes out fast, eyes back at feet. The wet slippers stand in their line.
- She passes the buckle flats: size 38, the number worn half off the insole, resoled three times, soft at the heels.
- She steps into her Velcro 39s, bending no more than her hip allows.
- She pulls the djellaba over wet skin, and it sticks to her unscrubbed back. She ties the scarf over dripping hair.
- Through the wall Dija sings brightly. Khadija doesn't turn toward it.
- Under her breath: *"If you're going, go."*

**The turn:** From the edge of the truth to leaving it, by her own rule.

**Tells and voice used:** Eyes at feet. Her own phrase turned on herself. Her back covered, wet.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she hesitates and goes back. Instead there is no hesitation on the page. Three sizes stand in one doorway, and no sentence connects them.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** The 37 never walked. The 38 walked to Tangier and back. The 39 is what her feet became anyway.
- **Character believes:** She is beating the meter.
- **Listener believes:** Connects the 38 to the thin woman (2.1) and to the buckle twin under the bed (1.2).
- **Clue planted:** None. This scene pays off earlier plants.

### Scene 4.2
**Where and when:** The street door, then the alleys up toward Bab Boujloud. Minutes 7 → −6. A porter's *"Balak!"*, hooves on stone.

**Who:** Khadija; a porter with a mule loaded with gas bottles.

**What happens:**
- She counts coins that are no longer in her pocket. A flash comes, numbers and objects only: *A biscuit tin. Three hundred and eighty. Half. One hundred and ninety. A jeweller's glass counter. A velvet tray.* It cuts off as the street door opens.
- She climbs, uphill now, the alleys she came down in 1.1. Her left foot lands flat. Nobody comments.
- She counts steps against minutes, out loud, under her breath.
- *"Balak!"* The mule comes through. She flattens her back against the stone to let it pass. She loses a minute and recounts.

**The turn:** From escape to losing the race. The listener still hopes she makes it, and is complicit in the hoping.

**Tells and voice used:** The Counter out loud. Back to the wall. Numbers instead of memory.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is a memory of the bus, the awning or the friend. Instead the flash is arithmetic and furniture, with no "I", no name and no why.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** They saved 380 dirhams in a tin. Khadija kept it and spent Fatiha's half, 190, on the hoops.
- **Character believes:** She is counting minutes. She does not look at the other count.
- **Listener believes:** "One hundred and ninety" snaps back to 2.3, her price for the hoops, and "half" points to the woman whose name came in 3.3. A careful listener now has the theft. The last line confirms it.
- **Clue planted:** The flash numbers. The left foot landing flat, on Khadija.

### Scene 4.3
**Where and when:** Outside the gate, at the car. Minute −6.

**Who:** Khadija.

**What happens:**
- A ticket is under the stiff wiper. She lifts the wiper with two fingers, like something hot.
- She reads the ticket twice, the way she reads a price that scanned wrong. It hasn't.
- The printed time is four minutes past the meter's last minute. She reads the sum aloud, flat, like a 3 a.m. total. It is the sticker's sum.

**The turn:** From racing the clock to discovering the race bought nothing. Relief becomes a bill.

**Tells and voice used:** Reading a total like a cashier. The 1.1 joke left unspoken.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is a bitter laugh, or *"Same price for one minute or one day."* Instead there is silence after the sum, and the punchline is left to the listener.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** She could have stayed until the back turned, seen the face, and paid exactly this.
- **Character believes:** She will not let herself think it.
- **Listener believes:** Recognition. The relief at "Nine minutes" was paid for and bought nothing.
- **Clue planted:** None. This pays off the sticker.

### Scene 4.4
**Where and when:** At the car, just after −6.

**Who:** Khadija.

**What happens:**
- She reaches through the window into the coffee jar for coins, to feed the dead meter for time already lost. The jar is empty.
- Her hand goes to the djellaba pocket. Nothing.
- She folds the ticket in four, corner to corner, presses the fold with her thumbnail, and puts it in the empty pocket.
- The same thumb rises, from habit, to the left hoop. It doesn't turn it. *Her thumb went to the left hoop, and stayed there.*

**The turn:** From paying to the one account she cannot pay. The wrong account is perfectly closed, and the real one is worn.

**Tells and voice used:** The paying instinct. The fold and the thumbnail. The earring, now still.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she takes off the earrings, cries, or goes back. Instead the thumb only rests.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** The hoops are the only stolen goods in the story. The coin went to someone who already owed it to her.
- **Character believes:** Unsaid.
- **Listener believes:**
  - Every touch of the hoop was a confession made with a hand: at the notebook debt (1.1), "thief" (1.3), "a hundred and ninety" (2.3) and "paid" (3.1).
  - The "No" in 2.3 now reads differently: any glove on her back would have caught the thief.
- **Clue planted:** None. This is the final payoff.
