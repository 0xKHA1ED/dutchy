**Twist 1: the thin woman is not a thief but a carer. Verdict: fair**, and the clues are generous.

**Twist 2: the kessala is "Le doigt", and the debt is repaid in reverse. Verdict: fair.**

- In chapter 2, the coin is given "sans regarder la main", and the hand goes "vers le nœud d'un foulard".
- The repeated "Ton gommage est payé" is the kessala reversing Khadija's lifelong bookkeeping.

**Twist 3: the thin woman is Fatiha, Khadija's other half of "la paire". Verdict: fair but thin at the decisive point.** The clues:
- The memory of age fourteen: "un doigt passait lentement sur son dos, une lettre".
- The thin woman's shoulders turn toward Khadija's wall in chapter 1.
- "La même boucle que les miennes", with the ballerinas resoled "comme des crêpes".
- "Cent quatre-vingt-dix" and "aucune en or" on the lost-property nail.
- "La bouche de Khadija était déjà ouverte": she answers Fatiha's voice before the other Khadija does.
- The ballerina sole in chapter 4: "un trois, et la moitié d'un huit".

There are enough clues for the identity. The *history*, though (two girls, a biscuit box, 380 dirhams, a jeweller's velvet tray, matching shoes, and whatever made them a broken pair), arrives only in a six-noun fragment after the climax. A B1 reader may never reconstruct that the earrings were bought jointly, or why Khadija kept the whole pair. The emotional freight is there, but it is under-signposted for this audience.
