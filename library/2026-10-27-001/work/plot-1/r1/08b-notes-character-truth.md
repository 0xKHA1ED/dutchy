## Behaviour checks

**Khadija: honoured, almost throughout.**

- **Tell 1 (eyes never above the collarbone)** holds with real discipline.
  - She looks only at feet ("Khadija a regardé les pieds"), hands, the glove, the purse.
  - With Souad: "Khadija n'a regardé que le seau." Souad's hand lands "juste à la hauteur de ses yeux."
  - In the mirror she looks "jusqu'aux épaules, pas plus." That is the Lacanian inversion exactly: she sees herself from the shoulders up and everyone else from the shoulders down.
  - The listener never sees a face. The one time her eyes rise is the climax, and they rise *past* the face to a clock. This is the sheet obeyed so precisely that the obedience becomes the drama.
- **Tell 2 (back to the wall, towel until it falls)** is staged in every room.
  - The changing room: "le dos contre le carrelage, la serviette déjà sur les épaules."
  - The warm room: "Son dos a quitté le mur d'un centimètre." The Girl on the Wet Tiles surfaces and the Counter cuts her off: "Trente minutes… Vingt-neuf."
  - The hot room: the towel slides, "Khadija a tendu la main vers elle, une fois. Puis elle l'a laissée par terre."
  - The progression is physical and unexplained.
- **One soft spot:** "pour une fois, elle a laissé son dos à tout le monde." Crossing a room is ordinary. The narrator flags it as a break, and the story gives no cause in her for it. It reads as the author arranging for Fatiha to see the walk. (Note 1.)
- **Tell 3 (earring)** fires correctly at money, thief and owe: after the notebook, after "Une voleuse", after "pas payée", at Souad's "Du vieil or", at "Elles disent toujours payée", and at the end.
  - That is seven times. The last one ("il est resté là") depends on the habit being established, so most of these are earning their keep.
  - Two of them sit very close together in §2. (Note 5.)
- **Tell 4 (fold in four)** runs through the voucher, the receipt and the ticket. It is clean and builds to the ending.
- **Tell 5 (answers a feeling with a time or price)** produces the story's best cornered moments:
  - "Vingt-six minutes, a dit Khadija tout de suite. Pas une heure."
  - "Cent quatre-vingt-dix."
- **Tell 6 (sleeves)** is done twice with real restraint: "Ça, c'est le geste d'une…" / "Khadija s'est tue." and "Regarde comme elle…" / "Khadija s'est tue."
- **Tell 7 (finishing others' sentences)** is unused. That is not a fault. It would have competed with her own trailing-off.
- **Tell 8 (coins by touch)** opens the story: "Elle les a comptées au toucher, contre sa paume."

**Generosity is real, not a cover.** The conference asked for this, and the story delivers it:
- "Khadija les avait mis de sa poche, comme toujours."
- The coin pressed into Souad's palm "sans regarder la main." It is kindness and avoidance in one gesture, which is exactly right.

**One behaviour slightly out of register.** At the shoes she says aloud, "Les miennes, c'est du trente-sept. Sous le lit, encore dans le papier. On m'avait dit qu'elles allaient se faire." Her running commentary is about *strangers'* purchases and bodies. This is the one moment she volunteers something private and rueful, and nothing provokes it. (Note 4.)

**Fatiha's one break is earned.** Her tell is that her speed never changes when watched. In §1 her hands stop and "Ses épaules se sont tournées vers le mur de Khadija, une seconde." This surprise comes from inside her: she has just recognised the walk and the voice. Khadija's "Tu vois ? Une voleuse vérifie toujours où est le gardien" turns the recognition into evidence, which is the mechanism at work. Afterwards the tell is restored: "Ses mains continuaient, toujours à la même vitesse." That is good.

## The climax

**Verdict: true to the person, and earned.**

All the lenses predicted the same thing under pressure:
- logistics and counting (core dramatic, existential);
- the Counter grabbing the wheel (IFS);
- reaching for accounts rather than words (Lacanian);
- flight that ends in a settlement with the wrong creditor (core dramatic's false self-revelation, and the conference's "pay, or be seen" resolution).

The story does all of it, in sequence:
1. Fatiha says "Khadija." Khadija's mouth is already open to answer to her own name. Dija answers a second late, which is her tell: "Khadija a fermé la bouche sur rien."
2. The name *Fatiha* rises once and is pushed away "comme une pièce qu'on fait glisser sur le comptoir… Au suivant." Her cornered word is "Next," and here it is enacted as a gesture.
3. As the shoulder turns, her eyes climb over it to the clock. This was planted in §1: "Elle n'en avait pas besoin : elle ne s'était jamais trompée d'une minute." A woman who never needs a clock looks at one. That is flight with no word of explanation. It is the best psychological move in the story.
4. "Neuf minutes." She leaves. She murmurs her own rule, "Si tu pars, pars," which she first gave a stranger in the doorway.
5. She pays the city in full and wrongly. The pocket is empty because she gave the coin to the woman whose debt she once paid.

The direct address ("Vous aussi, vous avez déjà regardé une horloge comme ça.") sits exactly where the sheet allows: once, short, at the clock, explaining nothing.

The ending gesture is right too. The thumb goes to the hoop and "il est resté là." The habitual turning stops. The account she closes is the wrong one, and it is perfectly closed.

## Voice

- **Khadija** sounds like her sheet.
  - Bursts and numbers: "Six pièces. Une heure."
  - Self-correction: "Vingt et un jours… Des nuits, plutôt."
  - Darija carried untranslated: *safi, khlas, aafak, lalla*.
  - Her catchphrases: "Écris ça" twice, "Tu te rends compte", "je te le dis".
  - She uses *tu* with every woman, and *vous* only inside the Hamid impression ("Un gâteau pour votre dynamique, Madame Amrani").
  - Tangier appears only as a joke: "tu prends le car de nuit pour Tanger. Avec un sandwich."
  - The rare laugh shows the gold molar ("assez pour montrer presque la dent en or du fond"), then is shut down.
  - The only line slightly off-register is the shoe confession (above).
- **Fatiha** sounds like hers. She is low, fast and practical, and her Spanish arrives at the moment of care and pain: "Venga… L'autre épaule. Je ne suis pas payée aujourd'hui," then "Ya está. Ya está." as she lowers Dija into the hollow of the marble. Her "Oui, Dija." uses the nickname deliberately and sets up the full name later.
- **Dija** sounds like hers. She is loud and works the room.
  - She laughs before the punchline: "pas un… — elle riait déjà — pas un tapis !"
  - On the hip: "C'est là qu'est la facture."
  - The writer cut "Fatiha" from her sheet line ("Doucement, doucement" with no name) to protect the single late naming. That is a smart choice.
- **Souad** sounds like hers. She is direct, loud enough to cross rooms, and says less than she knows. "Ton gommage est payé, lalla" works as a refrain.
- **No two voices merge.** The one deliberate rhyme is Khadija's "On regarde, on ne touche pas. Comme le musée Batha," said under her breath to nobody, which precedes Dija's "Je suis un musée" said aloud to a room. That is conference conflict C made audible: two women performing their bodies, one silently and one out loud. It is not a voice collision.

## Announced psychology

Feelings are never named. There is no psychological vocabulary anywhere (no *peur, honte, culpabilité, regret*), and no character understands her own mechanism aloud. The narrator glosses meaning in four small places:

1. "pour une fois, elle a laissé son dos à tout le monde": flags the mechanism.
2. "Elle n'a pas regardé. On ne regarde pas sa propre poche.": an aphorism that tells the listener the purse-handing is trust, right after the action has already shown it.
3. "Plus le gant approchait de la hanche, plus la chanson montait. Plus elle montait, plus elle était gaie.": states Dija's pain-song tell as a rule rather than letting it happen once or twice.
4. "le tissu a collé tout de suite à son dos, que personne n'avait frotté": borderline. It is physical and earned by everything before it. I would keep it.

"Son dos a quitté le mur d'un centimètre. Elle ne l'a pas senti." also tells us what she doesn't feel, but it enacts the Girl on the Wet Tiles and is worth its cost.

## The contradiction

**Alive, and enacted to the last line.** She follows the back through three rooms, "Elle les a suivies et elle a pris le banc juste derrière elles," and she leaves at the instant it turns.

The thief half is built entirely by irony:
- her prepared accusation, "Lalla, ton sac est dans la main de la mince," is never spoken;
- "Elles disent toujours payée" is her answer to the one woman who really did pay;
- the ending shows the gold in her own ears was half someone else's.

The story never flattens her into either side. The detective really is expert: she sizes feet correctly, and "Le gilet commence par le bas de la rue." The case does not collapse with a revelation. It frays: "Loin de sa main… Comme ça, c'est plus facile pour…" / "Elle n'a pas fini." That fraying is the right shape for this woman.

## Supporting characters

- **Souad: own want clearly visible.** "C'est pour le train de ma fille. Rabat. En octobre." She has her own tells (the doorway, the folded arms, the tip into the "nœud d'un foulard"). She delivers one line, does not explain it, and leaves with "Je ne vais nulle part." She is not a sage. The finger recognition pays off a §1 plant without a word of commentary.
- **Dija: own want visible.**
  - The hip: "Ma nouvelle hanche est dedans. Elle est payée."
  - The sea: "je rentre dans la mer jusqu'au ventre… Gratuit !"
  - The wedding: the "chanson de mariage".
  - Her happiness is real and owes Khadija nothing, which is what the sheet calls the cruellest thing a double can be.
- **Fatiha: present, but thinnest.** Her deep want, for this back to answer, is enacted superbly by the low "Khadija" spoken "presque dans l'épaule". It is aimed at Dija and lands on the other Khadija. Her surface want (the ground-floor flat in Fès) and the Fès/Tangier disagreement with Dija, which the relationship map says is "carried quietly into the steam", leave no trace. Dija's sea line is the only half of that argument on the page. She is not a device, but one concrete trace would give her a life beyond Khadija. (Note 6.)

## Notes

1. [minor] "Khadija a traversé la salle, et pour une fois, elle a laissé son dos à tout le monde." The narrator flags a break in her pattern but gives it no cause in her, so it reads as the author arranging Fatiha's recognition of the walk. Direction: either let the crossing be ordinary and unremarked (crossing a room exposes anyone's back), or give the exposure a source in her, such as absorption in the hunt, and show it rather than label it.

2. [minor] "Elle n'a pas regardé. On ne regarde pas sa propre poche." This aphorism hands the listener the reading (trust, care) that the never-say list reserves for them, immediately after the action has already shown it. Direction: let the blind hand-off stand alone, or route any comment through Khadija's suspicion so it still reads as evidence against the thin one.

3. [minor] "Plus le gant approchait de la hanche, plus la chanson montait. Plus elle montait, plus elle était gaie." This states Dija's tell as a rule, a small explanation. Direction: let the song brighten at one specific moment of pressure on the hip and trust the listener to connect it. Her "C'est là qu'est la facture" already does the work.

4. [minor] "Les miennes, c'est du trente-sept. Sous le lit, encore dans le papier. On m'avait dit qu'elles allaient se faire." This is the only time Khadija volunteers something private and rueful aloud, unprovoked. Her commentary otherwise goes outward, at strangers, in prices. Direction: keep the plant, but carry it in her register (a price, a size said as a fact, a curt joke) or in a gesture toward the ballerinas, so the shoes stay evidence rather than confession.

5. [minor] "Le pouce de Khadija a trouvé la boucle." / "Le pouce de Khadija a tourné la boucle gauche." / "sans arrêter de tourner". In §2 the earring fires two or three times within a short span and starts to feel mechanical. Direction: thin the middle occurrences so each remaining turn sits on a real trigger word, and let the final "il est resté là" land against a habit rather than a drumbeat.

6. [minor] "Je ne suis pas payée aujourd'hui, ne me fais pas demander deux fois." Fatiha's own want beyond Khadija (Fès, the flat, the quiet disagreement with Dija about leaving the sea) is invisible. Direction: one concrete trace in her mouth or hands that answers Dija's sea, so the pair has its own unresolved business in the steam.

## Protect

- "Elle n'en avait pas besoin : elle ne s'était jamais trompée d'une minute." … "ils ont traversé la vapeur jusqu'à l'horloge blanche au-dessus de la porte, la seule chose nette dans la salle." The plant and its payoff are the climax's whole psychology. The one woman who never needs a clock lifts her eyes over the turning shoulder to look at one. It is flight, fully enacted, without a single word of explanation.
- "— Khadija. / Deux femmes ont répondu. / La bouche de Khadija était déjà ouverte. / Une seconde plus tard… — Na'am ? / Khadija a fermé la bouche sur rien." Shared name, Dija's late-turn tell, Fatiha's act against forty years of silence, and Khadija's self almost answering all happen in five lines, with nothing named.
- "— Vingt-six minutes, a dit Khadija tout de suite. Pas une heure." This is her cornered voice exactly. Souad says the one true thing, and Khadija corrects the arithmetic. It is the sheet's mechanism compressed into seven words. Do not soften it or add a reaction.

## Scores

**Psychological truth: 5.** Every character acts from her sheet, and the one surprise (Fatiha's hands stopping) comes from inside her. The climax follows the lens predictions to the letter: count, look at the clock, "Si tu pars, pars", pay the wrong creditor. The contradiction stays alive to the last gesture, "Son pouce est monté jusqu'à la boucle gauche, et il est resté là." Generosity is played as real, as the conference asked. The only thinness is Fatiha's surface want.

**Lightness (jargon and announced feelings): 4.** There is no psychological vocabulary and no named feeling, and every never-say item is kept. Four small narratorial glosses remain: "pour une fois", "On ne regarde pas sa propre poche", the "plus… plus" rule, and the unprompted shoe confession. They lean a little on meaning the actions already carry. Cutting or rerouting those would earn a 5.
