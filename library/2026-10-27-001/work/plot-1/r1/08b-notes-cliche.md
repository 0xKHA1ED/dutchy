## Stock images and moves

- **Ticking clock as a symbol of time** (banned list): "l'horloge blanche au-dessus de la porte, la seule chose nette dans la salle." The use is earned. The "Vous aussi" line is what pushes it back toward symbol.

Bab Boujloud and the musée Batha are also the city's best-known landmarks, though the Batha joke belongs to the characters and earns its place.

## The new thing

There is a great deal here that the reader has not met before:

- "ses mains ont poussé deux manches invisibles au-dessus du coude." A woman naked to the waist pushes up sleeves she isn't wearing, and the gesture reveals her as a worker, not a thief.
- "Le doigt. Pain, briquet. 2 dh. Mars." This is a ledger of other people's shortfalls that she has covered for twenty-seven years and never reclaimed. The coin she saves then circles back to that same hand: "C'est pour le train de ma fille. Rabat. En octobre."
- "— Khadija. / Deux femmes ont répondu. / La bouche de Khadija était déjà ouverte."
- The round woman's "Je suis un musée, pas un… pas un tapis !" echoes Khadija's private "On regarde, on ne touche pas. Comme le musée Batha." A shared joke reveals a shared past without a word of exposition.
- Smaller fresh details: the clocks bagged against the steam, the sock tied over the tap, the heels "refaits tant de fois qu'ils faisaient une petite pile, comme des crêpes", and the lost-property nail with "trois boucles d'oreilles seules, aucune en or."
- "Cent quatre-vingt-dix." / "Trois cent quatre-vingts. La moitié." A pair of earrings bought in halves is the title's pair, told only as arithmetic.

Direction: remove the reader-address so that Khadija's eyes climbing to the clock stand alone. The planted line "elle ne s'était jamais trompée d'une minute" already makes her looking mean avoidance.

## Protect
- "— Khadija. / Deux femmes ont répondu. / La bouche de Khadija était déjà ouverte." This is the story's best invention. The shared name does the whole recognition, with no reveal speech.
- "On regarde, on ne touche pas. Comme le musée Batha." echoed by "Je suis un musée, pas un… — pas un tapis !" One shared joke carries a whole history. It must not be explained.
- "Puis elle avait écrit dans le carnet : « Le doigt. Pain, briquet. 2 dh. Mars. »", together with the coin to the kessala, "C'est pour le train de ma fille. Rabat. En octobre." A whole moral economy in two dirhams: her quiet charity is returned to her and spent forward, and she ends with an empty pocket.

## Scores
