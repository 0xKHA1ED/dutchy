## Questions

**Deep question:** When we refuse a life, do we spend the rest of ours paying every small debt on time, so we never have to see that life lived by someone else?

**Surface question candidates:**
1. **Suspense:** Will Khadija catch the thin woman with the purse in the act before her meter runs out?
2. **Mystery:** What is in the purse the thin woman carries "like eggs" and never opens, and who are these two women?
3. **Dread / dramatic irony:** The heavy woman walks like Khadija, stands like Khadija, and is slowly being turned toward her. Will Khadija see the face before she can get away?

The design link: question 1 is answered "no, she ran out of time", and the reason she ran out of time is that she would not see the face. That "no" answers the deep question without stating it. She paid the city on time instead.

## Skeleton 1: Countdown + Closed room (the rooms are the chambers)

**Surface question:** 1 at first, which becomes 3 in the hot room.

**Ladder:**
1. **Cold room. 60 → 43 minutes.**
   - At Bab Boujloud she feeds the meter with two-dirham coins from the car jar. Two coins are left. She folds Naïma's voucher in four.
   - Inside, she undresses with her back to the tiles and the towel over her shoulders.
   - Within three minutes she has the pair: a thin woman carrying a heavy woman's purse from bench to bench, never opening it, pushing up sleeves she isn't wearing.
   - The Detective wakes up: "She carries it like eggs. Like eggs!"
   - Souad appears in the doorway: "Your scrub is paid, lalla." Khadija: "Later, lalla. I'm busy."
   - The heavy woman goes into the warm room ahead of her, left foot landing flat. Khadija follows, one bench behind.
   - The chapter closes on the minute-mark: forty-three minutes.
2. **Warm room. 43 → 24.**
   - The glove starts on the heavy woman's back, slowly, far too slowly. "Nobody scrubs a back that slowly. Nobody. Unless the other hand has a job."
   - Khadija sits against the wall directly behind them. From there she can see a henna-auburn line over a grey parting, a left shoulder that sits lower than the right, and a back. Only a back.
   - Dija says, "Slowly, Fatiha, I'm a museum, not a carpet." Khadija laughs under her breath at the joke, then stops. She has made that joke herself, to nobody.
   - Her own towel is getting heavy. Souad passes and says the glove will catch her earrings and she should take them off. "No." She turns the left one.
   - Chapter ends at twenty-four minutes, as the pair rise toward the hot room. Khadija gets up too, though it costs her bad hip.
3. **Hot room. 24 → 9.**
   - Steam. The towel soaks through and slides off her shoulders. Her back is bare to the room.
   - Souad sits beside her, flat hand with its missing fingertip on her own knee: "You've been watching her back for an hour. Do you want me to tell you what yours looks like?" Khadija: "Forty-one minutes. Not an hour." She has recognised the finger. "The finger." Two dirhams, in the notebook.
   - Then the thin woman says "Khadija", low, to the back. Two women answer: the heavy woman a full second late, and Khadija's mouth already open.
   - One name comes into Khadija's head, Fatiha, and she pushes it away like a coin back across a counter.
   - The glove stops. The heavy woman begins, slowly, on a stiff left hip, to turn.
4. **The point of no return.** Khadija looks at the clock above the door. Nine minutes. The chapter ends there.
5. **Door and alleys. 9 → –4.**
   - Among the wet slippers by the door stand black flats with a gold buckle, size 38, resoled, soft at the heels. They are the twin of the pair in tissue under her bed. She steps into her Velcro pharmacy sandals, size 39.
   - She takes the alleys on the bad hip, counting steps against minutes.

**Expected beats at the main turn:**
- the face turns and it's a mirror / reunion;
- Khadija exposes the "con" and is proved right or wrong;
- Khadija breaks and confesses to Souad.

**Chosen instead:** She checks the time. The Counter wins with a number ("Nine minutes"), because a number has always been her answer to a feeling. This is the exact tell from the sheet, so the flight is not cowardice staged for us. It is her oldest move, made at the worst moment. The one direct address goes here, a single line: *You have looked at a clock like that.*

**Ending:** The ticket is already under the wiper, written four minutes after the meter ran out. She folds it in four, corner to corner, and presses the fold with her thumbnail. Her thumb goes to the left hoop and stays there. The last image is two coins in the car jar: she had time to buy, and no hand free to buy it with.

**Reframe:**
- "She carries it like eggs" was care.
- The slow glove was the crime she couldn't name.
- The earrings she refused to take off become the evidence of the only theft in the story. In the alley, when she sees the shoes, a flash of number: 380 in a biscuit tin, half of it someone else's, 190 at a jeweller. That flash, with her thumb on the hoop, is all the reveal gets.

**Recognition moment:** At "nine minutes" the listener is relieved she's leaving, because they don't want to see the face either. Then the ticket shows they were relieved alongside a woman paying the wrong creditor.

**Wildcard:** Dija is seen from behind in every room, because Khadija always sits behind. The turn is the only time she starts to face us, and the story cuts to the clock.

**Chapters:** 4 (cold / warm / hot / door-and-alleys), each ending on a minute-mark.

## Skeleton 2: Escalation spiral (comic, turning tragic)

**Surface question:** 1. Will she catch them before the meter runs out?

**Refrain:** At every rung, *"She still had one more move."*

**Ladder:**
1. **The small problem (cold room, 60 → 45).**
   - She wants to watch the pair without being seen watching, and without being scrubbed herself, because a scrub means lying on her front with her back to the room.
   - Fix: she takes the bench against the wall behind them, back to the tiles, towel up.
   - It works. It also puts her in the heavy woman's exact posture, one bench back, the same shoulder lower than the other. Souad notices. "Your scrub is paid, lalla."
2. **The fix creates a new problem (warm room, 45 → 30).**
   - Souad keeps coming back with her bucket.
   - Bigger fix: Khadija buys time. She presses a two-dirham coin into Souad's hand: "Later, aafak. Go and do someone who wants it." This is the coin she kept for the meter. It is also the coin Souad owes her from the notebook, and neither says so.
   - Souad tucks it into her headscarf knot and folds her arms in the doorway, watching her now instead of the room.
3. **Others drawn in (warm into hot, 30 → 18).**
   - To get closer, Khadija claims the bucket next to theirs. Bucket territory turns into a whispered war with Dija's things.
   - Dija complains out loud to the room: "Someone's stolen my bucket, there's a thief in here." The room laughs. Dija laughs two words before the end of her own joke.
   - Khadija is now holding the "victim's" bucket. The defence is becoming the attack.
4. **The point of no return (hot room, 18 → 10).**
   - Fatiha sets the purse down on the bench to rinse the glove. Khadija's cashier hands do what they have done for twenty-seven years: they take the evidence.
   - She opens the purse that has never been opened.
   - Inside is a clinic deposit receipt for a hip operation on 12 November, made out to *Mme Khadija T.*: her own first name, on someone else's paid account.
5. **The summit / collapse (10 → –3).**
   - Fatiha says "Khadija" to the back. Two women turn their heads.
   - The heavy woman starts to turn on the bad hip.
   - Khadija has a stranger's open purse in her lap. She is the only thief in the hot room.

**Expected beats at the main turn:**
- Fatiha catches her with the purse and the roles flip loudly;
- she confesses and the old friendship opens;
- she runs with the purse.

**Chosen instead:** She folds the receipt in four, presses it with her thumbnail, puts it back, closes the purse, and sets it down neatly on the wrong bench. That is a cashier squaring the till, the only repair she knows. Then she leaves on "Ten minutes." It follows the sheet's paying instinct exactly: she settles the purse's account and leaves the person's unsettled.

**Ending:** At the car the ticket is there. She reaches for the meter jar to pay the extra time anyway, and the jar is empty: the last coin went to Souad, to avoid being touched. She folds the ticket in four. Behind her, in the steam, Fatiha is looking for a purse that someone tidied.

**Reframe:**
- The Vigilance Award by her sink at home, mentioned in chapter 1 as a joke ("I have a certificate, lalla"), now reads as the record of a woman who has spent her life catching other people's hands.
- "Someone's stolen my bucket, there's a thief in here" turns out to be true.

**Recognition moment:** The listener laughed at each move because each one was so sensible. The point of no return, a careful woman opening a stranger's purse "to make sure", is the step any of us takes when proving someone guilty is easier than looking at them.

**Wildcard:** Dija is seen from behind throughout. The bucket war is fought entirely with the back of her head and her loud voice, which makes the comedy work: Khadija is fighting someone who doesn't know she exists.

**Chapters:** 4. Minute-marks at 45, 30, 10 and –3.

## Skeleton 3: The loop (ritual × 4, with a meter clock)

**Surface question:** 3. Will she see the face? Number 1 serves as the comic cover.

**The repeated passage** (five sentences, opening every chapter):
> She looked at the time. She turned the left earring once. She put her back to the tiles. She pulled the towel up over her shoulders. She watched the glove.

Inside each chapter, Souad's line returns: *"Your scrub is paid, lalla. Lie down when you're ready."* Khadija answers: *"Later."*

**Ladder:**
1. **First time (cold room, "Fifty-two minutes").**
   - The ritual is pure competence: the Detective at work.
   - The Detective's jokes run: black soap from a tub ("eight dirhams at Bab Ftouh, she's paying for the tub"), the purse carried like eggs.
   - The heavy woman's flat left foot is noted in passing as "a bad hip, easy mark."
   - "Later." She is comic and in control.
2. **Second time (warm room, "Thirty-four minutes").**
   - Same five sentences, but the towel is heavier and the glove slower. The listener begins to see the double before she does.
   - Souad's offer gains a clause: "Take off the earrings, the glove catches them." Khadija says "No," not "Later." The refusal has moved from time to money.
3. **Third time (hot room, "Fourteen minutes").**
   - The ritual can't finish. The towel slides off at "She pulled the towel up". Her bare back faces the room, and Fatiha's eyes come up for the first time, above the back she is scrubbing, to Khadija's back.
   - Souad says the line about what Khadija's own back looks like, and lays the hand with the missing fingertip flat between Khadija's shoulder blades. It stays one second.
   - The Girl on the Wet Tiles leans a fraction toward it. A flash of a word written on a back at fourteen. *Write it again.* It is never decoded.
   - Fatiha says "Khadija". The back starts to turn.
   - Khadija stands up: "Later."
4. **Fourth time (the car, "Minus three").** The ritual, word for word, in the wrong place:
   > She looked at the time. She turned the left earring. She put her back to the car door. She held the ticket. She folded it in four.

**Expected beats at the main turn:**
- the towel falls and she finally lies down for the scrub;
- she is seen and weeps;
- the turning back reveals the mirror face.

**Chosen instead:** She says "Later", the cheap refusal from chapter 1, now to the most expensive thing she has ever been offered. It is the same word with the opposite meaning. It follows from her voice sheet: when cornered, she gives a time.

**Ending:** The ritual is completed at the car. The back-to-the-wall becomes back-to-the-door. "She watched the glove" becomes "she folded it in four", with the thumbnail pressed along the fold. Her thumb rests on the hoop. That is all.

**Reframe:** The very first "She turned the left earring once", which played as a nervous tic in a funny chapter, is now the start of every rescue she has ever performed on herself.

**Recognition moment:** By the third repetition the listener is predicting the five sentences and wants the variation, wants her to lie down. When she says "Later" they recognise their own small, reasonable "later" to something that will never be offered again.

**Wildcard:** The loop is built on backs. Her back to the wall in every ritual, Dija's back in every room, and finally her own bare back seen. Dija never turns on the page; the turn is interrupted by "Later".

**Chapters:** 4. The minute-marks are inside the ritual's first sentence, which also gives each chapter its closing hook.

## Skeleton 4: Reversal (the accusation made) + Unreliable close third

**Surface question:** 1, played straight as a crime story, with question 2 (what's in the purse?) driving the climax.

**Ladder:**
1. **The case (cold room, 60 → 41).**
   - The narration is soaked in the Detective's voice. It never calls Khadija biased; it just reports, with relish, what she sees.
   - The clues are planted as evidence, each also fitting the other reading:
     - the purse carried like eggs and never opened;
     - sleeves pushed up that don't exist;
     - the thin woman's eyes always on the back, never on the room. "Professional. She doesn't look up."
   - The story mentions the 2014 catch, "Lalla, your purse is in the thin one's hand", and the certificate by her sink.
2. **The evidence that doesn't fit (warm room, 41 → 22).**
   - The heavy woman sings under her breath when the glove reaches her hip, and brighter when it hurts. Khadija reads this as "the mark's distracted".
   - Shoulders, hip and henna parting are all reported as "an easy mark: bad hip, can't turn fast."
   - The listener hears her own body being described as a victim's.
3. **The smoothing (hot room, 22 → 12).**
   - The fairest clue: Fatiha says "Khadija" and Khadija's own head turns. She covers it with a joke under her breath: "Half of Fès is called Khadija. The other half is called Fatima." Then a pause where a name nearly comes, and doesn't.
   - The glove slows further and almost stops. *Now. The hand is free.*
4. **The point of no return (12 → 6).**
   - She does it. She calls Souad over and speaks the 2014 line to the whole hot room: "Lalla, your purse is in the thin one's hand."
   - Souad opens the purse in front of everyone: a clinic deposit receipt for a hip, a scrap of wedding-dress fabric, a CTM ticket, Tangier–Fès.

**Expected beats at the main turn:**
- the purse holds stolen goods and she's vindicated;
- she's humiliated and apologises;
- the thin woman, recognising her, cries out her name.

**Chosen instead:** The whole room laughs. The heavy woman laughs hardest, bent over, still facing away, slapping her own bad hip: "She thinks I've got something worth stealing!"

Only one person does not laugh. Fatiha's eyes come up above Khadija's collarbone for the first time. Khadija, whose eyes never go that high, feels it the way you feel heat on a back.

She says "Next", as at the till. She gathers her things and leaves at "Six minutes", inside the laughter, which covers her exit like a towel. It follows from the Detective part: she needed a crime so badly that she committed one out loud.

**Ending:**
- At the door, the shoes: size 38, gold buckle, resoled.
- She doesn't stop. The alleys.
- The ticket. Fold, thumbnail, hoop.
- The one-line flash of number: 380, half, 190.

**Reframe:** Every "clue" from chapter 1 was true, and she read every one of them correctly except for what the hands were doing. The thin woman "doesn't look up" not because she's a professional, but because she has been not-looking at Khadija since the cold room.

**Recognition moment:** The listener has been on her side, wanting the exposure, and laughs with the room when the purse opens. That laugh is the same cover she leaves under. We all used the joke so we wouldn't have to watch the one woman who wasn't laughing.

**Wildcard:** Dija's back gets the biggest laugh in the story without ever turning. This is the cruellest version of "Dija needs nothing from Khadija".

**Chapters:** 4. Minute-marks at 41, 22, 12 and 6, plus a final ticket time.

## Skeleton 5: Two windows (Khadija's hour, then Fatiha's same hour)

**Surface question:** 1 in window one; 3 in window two, now as dramatic irony.

**Ladder:**
1. **Window one, chapters 1–2: cold and warm room, Khadija's close third (60 → 20).**
   - The full comic chase: commentary, purse, slow glove, the towel getting heavy, Souad's offers.
   - Ends on "Twenty minutes" as the pair enter the hot room. Khadija follows, already turning her earring.
2. **Hinge, chapter 3.** A clear audio marker: *"Fatiha had known since the cold room."*
   - Same hour, from the scrubber's side, kept spare. She saw the walk from behind in one glance: the left foot landing flat, the shoulder. She didn't stop working, because her scrubbing speed never changes when she's watched.
   - The purse is unopened because it is Dija's trust, and it holds the deposit for a hip that will keep Dija by the sea. That deposit is their first real quarrel.
   - Fatiha keeps her eyes on the back under her glove. That back is Dija's, seen from behind, the wildcard kept. She knows the second back is against the wall behind her.
   - No iceberg spill: no phone calls, no bus, no "why". Only the shoes she left at the door, which she wears only for the trip to Fès, and her hand slowing on purpose. That slowness is the only message she has ever been willing to send.
3. **The overlap, end of chapter 3: hot room.** One moment seen from both sides.
   - Fatiha stops the glove and says, to the back, "Khadija, turn." It is her first act against forty years.
   - Two women hear it.
   - In Fatiha's window we hear only a bench creak, and wet sandals on tiles moving away. She does not look up. Her tell holds.
4. **Chapter 4: back to Khadija.**
   - In the hot room, the turning back, the clock: "Nine minutes."
   - The door. The 38s beside the 39s.
   - The alleys. The ticket, fold, thumbnail, hoop.

**Expected beats at the main turn:**
- Fatiha calls out to the fleeing back;
- Fatiha follows her into the alleys;
- Dija turns and recognises the stranger.

**Chosen instead:** Fatiha does nothing visible. She lifts the glove again, the speed unchanged, and finishes the scrub. Dija completes the turn, but only in Fatiha's window, and Fatiha keeps her eyes on the hip, never the face. The listener knows the face is there and still doesn't get it. Both women answer pain with work; the overlap shows them doing the same thing at the same second.

**Ending:** Khadija's last chapter closes on the folded ticket. A final short "after" belongs to neither woman: the slipper row at the door, one gap where the size 39s stood, the 38s with their buckles catching the light.

**Reframe:** Window one's funniest line, "Nobody scrubs a back that slowly. Nobody. Unless the other hand has a job", is reread in window two. The other hand did have a job: waiting.

**Recognition moment:** In window one the listener judged with Khadija, enjoyed the case, and wanted the thief caught. Window two shows they were watching a woman not watching them back.

**Risk to flag:** This skeleton breaks the recipe's single close-third POV and risks explaining Fatiha. Keep window two to one chapter and keep it all hands and objects.

**Wildcard:** Dija is seen from behind in both windows, by both women. The only person who sees her face is offstage from the narration.

**Chapters:** 4.
