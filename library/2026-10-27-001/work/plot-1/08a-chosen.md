## X
Best moments:
- "Personne n'est aussi lent sans raison. Elle l'a gardée sur la liste." Her suspicion swallows a child carrying water. It is a perfect comic beat for the first half, and it twists the title (*La Paire*): she is hunting for the accomplice while her real double sits in front of her.
- "Voilà une relation honnête. Tu donnes, elle rend. Personne ne regarde personne." This states her whole ethic on page one in a joke, and the ending pays it off.

Most serious problem: The final clock sequence is muddled. "Trois minutes" are left when she reaches the street, yet the ticket says 12:04. Her race against time never turns into the self-deception it should. The countdown is just a countdown.

## Y
Best moments:
- "Elle a cherché l'autre main. Elle était posée à plat sur l'épaule de la femme ronde, et elle ne faisait rien. C'était peut-être ça, le travail." Mid-chase, the evidence quietly flips from theft to care. Khadija half-sees it and the listener fully does. This is exactly where the pitch wants the chase to stop being funny.
- "Six cents. Zéro. Moins une. Moins deux… Le gilet commence par le bas de la rue. Et il prend son café avant." She keeps counting past zero while bargaining with the warden's habits. This is the small, embarrassing, recognisable act of knowing you've lost and running anyway. It turns the parking ticket into the price she chose.

Most serious problem: It is nearly identical in structure to X, and it shares X's announced line "Vous aussi, vous avez déjà regardé une horloge comme ça." Speaking straight to the listener names the recognition instead of letting it land. The cryptic earring-sale flash ("Trois cent quatre-vingts. La moitié…") is also still under-seeded.

## Comparison
The two drafts share a skeleton almost beat for beat, so the choice comes down to texture. Y's texture is consistently more alive.

On **life**, Y adds observed details that serve the theme of not looking:
- "Khadija n'a regardé que le seau. Il avait une fissure sur le côté, réparée avec du scotch." She studies the bucket so she won't see the woman.
- "Elle avait déjà la phrase pour aujourd'hui", her rehearsed accusation.
- "La main de Khadija s'est fermée, par habitude. Il n'y avait plus de pièce dedans."

On **pull**, Y's first half is funnier and gives the listener more reason to share her appetite for exposure: "Ton sac à toi, tu le jettes par terre et tu t'assois dessus," "Internationale, en plus," "Aujourd'hui, c'est le sac qui paie." X has its own gem in the child-suspect line, but fewer of them.

On **the turn**, Y lets the chase break on the page. "Loin de sa main… Comme ça, c'est plus facile pour… Elle n'a pas fini," followed by the thin woman's tender "Ya está." X keeps Khadija's patter running longer and loses that crack.

On **recognition and the ending**, Y's negative countdown and "Le fond. Le côté. Le fond." in the empty jar make the cost felt. X's timestamps blur the cost.

On **pitch fit**, both hit every beat, but Y more clearly enacts "trying not to see her face."

X's one structural advantage is planting the warden early ("Il regardait les plaques. Jamais les gens."), which Y keeps only at the end.

## Choice
Y, because it has more moments that feel observed rather than generated, and more of them show her avoidance while it is happening. The hand on the shoulder and the bucket's taped crack are examples. Its countdown past zero gives the ending a real, recognisable flinch where X's version is merely arithmetic. The rewrite's main job is to cut the direct "Vous aussi" address and seed the earrings.

Worth keeping from the other draft: "Personne n'est aussi lent sans raison. Elle l'a gardée sur la liste."
