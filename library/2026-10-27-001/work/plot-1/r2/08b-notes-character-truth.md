## Behaviour checks

**Khadija: honoured, nearly everywhere.**

- **Tell 1, eyes never above the collarbone.** The story keeps this rule without ever stating it. "Elle s'est regardée jusqu'aux épaules, pas plus." "Khadija n'a regardé que le seau." "La main est montée plus haut que le regard de Khadija." Her feet-reading ("on connaît les gens par leurs chaussures… par leurs pieds") makes it a professional skill, not a symptom.
- **Tell 2, back to the tiles, towel heavy, towel slides.** The sequence runs exactly to the sheet: "le dos contre le carrelage, la serviette déjà sur les épaules" → "La serviette pesait sur ses épaules, lourde d'eau" → "Khadija a tendu la main vers elle, une fois. Puis elle l'a laissée par terre. / Son dos était nu contre le mur." The single reach for the towel is the right amount of resistance.
- **Tell 3, the earring.** It turns at the notebook of debts, at "Une voleuse", at the attendant's "Enlève-les" (answered "Non."), and at "Elles disent toujours payée". At the end it stops turning: "il est resté là." Five uses, each tied to money, theft or owing. None is explained.
- **Tell 4, folding in four.** The voucher, the receipt and the ticket are each folded "coin sur coin". The full thumbnail ritual is saved for the first paper and the last. Correct.
- **Tell 5, a price or a time instead of a feeling.** This is the spine of her defence. "Onze minutes. En descendant." After the memory of the wet tiles: "Trente minutes, a dit Khadija. Vingt-neuf." To Souad's line about her back: "Vingt-six minutes… Pas une heure." Best of all, to "Du vieil or": "Cent quatre-vingt-dix." The question asks for the gold's price, and she gives the sum of her debt. That leak works because the reader cannot decode it yet.
- **Tell 6, sleeves.** She stops mid-sentence twice: "Ça, c'est le geste d'une…" / "Regarde comme elle…". Both times the story simply lets her fall silent. Correct.
- **Tell 8, counting by touch.** "Elle les a comptées au toucher, contre sa paume."
- **Laughter and the gold molar.** "assez pour montrer presque la dent en or du fond. Puis elle a fermé la bouche." The "presque" is exact: she almost laughs properly, then closes the lid.
- **Paying instead of being seen, in miniature.** She hands Souad the seventh coin "sans regarder la main" to make the scrub go away. She pays the right creditor (the finger) without knowing it, as a way of refusing to be touched. Conflict A is acted out at small scale two chapters before the ending.
- **Doorway contempt planted early.** "Si tu pars, pars… Si tu restes, reste. La porte, c'est pour tout le monde." This sets up her murmured self-instruction at the exit.

**Fatiha.** "Her scrubbing speed does not change by a second when she feels watched." The story breaks this once: "les mains de la mince se sont arrêtées. Ses épaules se sont tournées vers le mur de Khadija, une seconde." The break is earned. It is the sheet's own "where the lie could break" beat (the glove stops), and it is her recognition of the walk. Khadija's detective reading covers it immediately: "Une voleuse vérifie toujours où est le gardien." After that, "toujours à la même vitesse" restores the tell. The slowing on the lower back is about the hip, not about being watched.

**Dija.** All three tells are present:
- She hands the purse back "sans se retourner".
- She laughs before the punchline: "— elle riait déjà —".
- She turns late: "Une seconde plus tard, sans se retourner… Na'am ?"
- Her song gets brighter with pain: "Elle a chanté plus fort, et elle a tapé une fois dans ses mains… C'est là qu'est la facture."

**Souad.** She appears in the doorway with "deux bras croisés", the coin goes into "le nœud d'un foulard", and her hand lies "à plat sur un genou". All tells are present and shown without explanation.

Nothing in any of the four characters happens for the plot's convenience.

## The climax

The climax is earned, and it is the best-executed moment in the story.

Every lens predicts the same thing under pressure: the Counter grabs the wheel, she answers with a time, and she flees into logistics. The story does this through her tell rather than through a decision. "Les yeux de Khadija sont montés. Plus haut que les pieds. Plus haut que le sac. Ils ont passé l'épaule qui tournait… jusqu'à l'horloge blanche… / Neuf minutes."

For the first time, her eyes rise above the collarbone, and they go past the face to the clock. Tell 1 and tell 5 merge into a single action. That is the contradiction ("as long as it never turns around") enacted at the exact second it is tested.

Fatiha's act is legible, which matters because it triggers the turn. She has said "Oui, Dija" earlier, so her switch to "Khadija", spoken "presque dans l'épaule", is marked as deliberate. Khadija answers her name physically before Dija does: "Le dos de Khadija a quitté le mur d'un centimètre. / La bouche de Khadija était déjà ouverte." The shut mouth, "sur rien", and the name pushed away "comme une pièce qu'on fait glisser sur le comptoir… Au suivant" follow the never-say instruction to the letter. The name comes once, late, in her head, as a fact she pushes away. "Au suivant" is her cornered phrase ("Next"), used at the right moment.

The exit also fits. She doesn't stand in the doorway, as some lenses predicted. She applies her own maxim, "Si tu pars, pars", and this time it means going *away*. That inversion is in character rather than a departure from it.

## Voice

**Khadija** is on-sheet throughout:
- short bursts and self-correction: "Vingt et un jours… Des nuits, plutôt";
- "Tu te rends compte", "Safi", "khlas", "aafak", "lalla", "Écris ça";
- *tu* to every woman, *vous* only in the Hamid impression: "Un gâteau pour votre dynamique, Madame Amrani";
- all three sample lines used almost verbatim, and they sound natural in context;
- her one "Tanger" is inside a joke ("le car de nuit pour Tanger. Avec un sandwich"), which is allowed, and it plants her night-bus searches.

**Fatiha** is fast, low and practical, with Spanish breaking through ("Venga", and "Ya está" at the moment of easing Dija down, which is the moment it hurts). This is exactly her sheet.

**Dija** is loud, comic and talks to the whole room: "Je suis un musée, pas un… pas un tapis !" Leaving her "Slowly, Fatiha" without the name is a good choice, because it saves names for the climax.

**Souad** is direct, uses few words, and says less than she knows: "Je ne vais nulle part."

No two voices blur together. The museum echo is the relationship map made audible. Khadija's "On regarde, on ne touche pas. Comme le musée Batha" (under her breath, to nobody) is answered by Dija's "Je suis un musée" (out loud, to a room that laughs). That is the same joke told by the two lives.

## The contradiction

The contradiction is alive, and visible through action alone. She follows the back through cold, warm and hot rooms with growing commitment (the towel abandoned, the back bare against the wall, the lean). She leaves at the exact moment the back turns. The second half, that a face would prove the only thief is her, lands through the 190 she leaked early, the biscuit-tin montage, and the thumb stopping on the hoop as she folds the ticket.

The story does not flatten her into either side. The Detective is truly funny and truly sharp all the way to the hot room ("Elles disent toujours payée"). The Girl on the Wet Tiles surfaces twice, involuntarily, each time for exactly one centimetre.

## Notes

1. **[minor]** "Deux femmes ont répondu." The narrator summarises the climax's key beat before the action delivers it. It also muddies the action: the next lines show Khadija *not* answering aloud ("a fermé la bouche sur rien"). The sequence that follows already does all the work: the back leaves the wall, the mouth is already open, then Dija's late "Na'am ?". **Direction:** let the physical sequence alone say that two women answered, so the listener realises it a beat before Khadija closes her mouth.

2. **[minor]** "le tissu a collé tout de suite à son dos, que personne n'avait frotté". The relative clause points at Khadija's need (the unscrubbed back) in the narrator's voice. The wet djellaba sticking to her skin is already eloquent after Souad's repeated offers. **Direction:** let the object carry it without the narrator's gloss.

3. **[minor]** "Khadija s'est levée. Sa main est allée à sa propre hanche." It comes immediately after "sa hanche gauche qui pliait mal". By this point the listener has had the left shoulder, the flat left foot, the henna over grey, size 39, and now the hip, all within two chapters. Each is shown rather than said, but this gesture, placed back to back with the hip, comes closest to a narrator elbow. The ending's "Son pied gauche se posait à plat sur les pavés" is the echo that matters. **Direction:** keep the parallels spaced and unpaired, so the resemblance accumulates without one beat that reads as "look, the same."

4. **[minor]** Khadija's warmth. The sheet treats her kindness as real, not a cover, and as what makes the listener like her. In the present action, the kindness lives only in backstory (the notebook) and in a tip handed over to get rid of someone. Everything she does on the page is refusal ("Non, merci. Ça va." / "Va frotter quelqu'un qui en a envie"). Her humour keeps her likeable, but the generous side is not acted out. **Direction:** consider one small, unthinking act of care toward a stranger in the first room, never commented on, so her refusal of care for herself has a visible counterweight.

5. **[minor]** Fatiha has no want of her own on the page. Her climactic act is earned through the shoulder turn and the shift from "Dija" to the full name, but nothing shows what she is carrying apart from Khadija. **Direction:** one concrete trace of her own stake (the Fès/Tangier disagreement with Dija, or her knees) would keep her a person rather than the mechanism that turns the back.

6. **[minor]** "elle ne s'était jamais trompée d'une minute" against the uphill count to "Zéro" and the ticket at "11 h 24". The Counter's infallibility breaks at the very end, which could be rich (the one day she lost her margin was the day she stayed for the name). But it is unclear whether the story means it, or whether the listener should read the ticket as her overstay or as bad luck. **Direction:** decide whether the Counter failed, and make that failure legibly hers. Do not explain it.

## Protect

- "Ils ont passé l'épaule qui tournait, ils ont monté le mur, ils ont traversé la vapeur jusqu'à l'horloge blanche au-dessus de la porte, la seule chose nette dans la salle. / Neuf minutes." Her two deepest tells fuse into the climax. This is the contradiction acted out, with no word of interpretation.
- "— Cent quatre-vingt-dix, a dit Khadija, sans arrêter de tourner." Asked the gold's price, she gives the debt. It is an unconscious leak delivered as her normal price reflex, and the ending pays it off in objects and numbers only.
- "On regarde, on ne touche pas. Comme le musée Batha." / "Je suis un musée, pas un… — elle riait déjà — pas un tapis !" The two lives tell the same body joke: one under her breath to nobody, one out loud to a room that laughs. It is the relationship map in two lines.

## Scores

Psychological truth: 5. Every character acts from their sheet, and the one deviation (Fatiha's glove stopping) is the sheet's own designed break point. The climax uses exactly the behaviour every lens predicts under pressure, through a tell rather than a decision: her eyes rise past the turning shoulder to "l'horloge blanche… Neuf minutes." The contradiction survives intact to the last gesture, "il est resté là." The minor gaps (Fatiha's own want, Khadija's enacted warmth) don't falsify anyone.

Cent quatre-vingt-dix."). Two narrator pointers remain: "Deux femmes ont répondu" at the climax, and "que personne n'avait frotté". Removing them, so the climax and the ending stand on action alone as the calibration 5 does, would earn the 5.
