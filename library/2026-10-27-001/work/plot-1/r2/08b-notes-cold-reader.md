## The four questions

1. About: A woman who has spent her life keeping accounts and suspecting others watches the love she gave up being lived, gladly, by another woman with her name, her feet and her hip, and she chooses to count minutes rather than turn and be seen, and pays anyway.
2. In myself: It made me think about how vigilance, being right, and being on time can be a way of not being touched. The story suggests that the theft we keep suspecting in others may be the thing we never took for ourselves.
3. Hit hardest: "Khadija a fermé la bouche sur rien."
4. At the last line: A tight, quiet ache. She is back to her one gesture, the thumb on the earring, with nothing in either pocket. I felt that the fine, "même prix pour une minute ou pour un jour", is the price of the life she didn't stay for.

## Hook

"Devant Bab Boujloud, Khadija a lu l'autocollant du parcmètre comme une étiquette de prix. / — Pièces de deux dirhams uniquement. Une heure maximum. Amende : cent dirhams. / Elle a fait claquer sa langue."

These lines raise a character question rather than a story question: who is this woman who reads everything as a price? The voice is immediate and specific, and "Même prix pour une minute ou pour un jour" turns out to be the whole story in miniature. But nothing is yet at stake. The real narrative question ("what is she watching, and why does it matter to her?") does not arrive until "elle avait la paire," roughly a third of the way in.

## Notes

1. [serious] "Les petits taxis rouges attendaient en ligne… Le parcmètre avait un sac en plastique… À un clou, près du comptoir, pendaient les choses perdues… Comme le musée Batha… Un seau rouge, avec un numéro de téléphone…"
   - Problem: Section 1 is a long run of well-observed texture before the engine (the pair) starts. A reader with other things to do may leave before "Trois minutes plus tard, elle avait la paire."
   - Direction: Keep every detail that pays off later (café chairs, seed-seller, the clocks, the limping stool, the rubber door, the ballerinas, the notebook). Compress or cut the pure texture so that the pair arrives much sooner and the opening carries a question as well as a voice.

2. [serious] "*Fatiha.* Le nom est monté, une fois. Khadija l'a repoussé…"
   - Problem: The whole story depends on the reader linking the thin woman to the 14-year-old memory, the sleeve gesture, and this name. I assembled it, but by deduction, and I am not fully sure. Many readers, especially learners, will register only "a name from her past" and miss that the woman she has been calling a thief is that person.
   - Direction: Add one more felt, physical bridge between the memory and the thin woman, an echo the reader experiences rather than infers. Do not name the relationship.

3. [serious] "Une boîte à biscuits. Trois cent quatre-vingts. La moitié. Cent quatre-vingt-dix. Un comptoir en verre. Un plateau de velours."
   - Problem: This flash, placed at the emotional exit, reads as a code. The earlier "Cent quatre-vingt-dix" to the scrubber was already opaque. I can guess at shared savings and earrings bought half each, but guessing at the climax pulls me out of feeling.
   - Direction: Plant one concrete earlier image so the flash resolves instantly when it arrives (whose half, which pair), or cut it and trust the thumb on the earring.

4. [minor] "Sur la semelle intérieure, il y avait un chiffre à moitié effacé : un trois, et la moitié d'un huit."
   - Problem: I stopped to work out whether this is meant to read as 37 (her unworn pair) or 33, and whether the two pairs of ballerinas were a matched gift. The ambiguity reads as accidental rather than meaningful.
   - Direction: Decide what the reader should conclude and make it legible at reading speed.

5. [minor] "— Vingt et un jours avant la retraite… Un gâteau pour votre dynamique, Madame Amrani."
   - Problem: This is the only beat in section 2 that turns away from the pair, and the tension slackens.
   - Direction: Either tie it to what she is watching (time running out, a life measured in shifts) or shorten it to the fact of retirement.

6. [minor] "a murmuré Khadija… a-t-elle dit à personne… Écris ça."
   - Problem: The muttered running commentary is a strong device, but by mid-story its rhythm becomes predictable.
   - Direction: Let a few judgements stay silent, as gestures or glances, so the spoken ones keep their bite.

7. [minor] "— Vingt-six minutes, a dit Khadija tout de suite. Pas une heure."
   - Problem: Every other number in the story counts down the meter. This one counts elapsed time, and I briefly lost my place in the clock.
   - Direction: Make it unmistakable that this is time spent watching, not time left.

## Protect

- "— Khadija. / Deux femmes ont répondu. / … / Khadija a fermé la bouche sur rien."
  - The climax happens entirely in a mouth opening and closing. Nothing is explained. Do not add a word.
- "Puis Khadija a vu la main. Les doigts posés sur le genou. L'un d'eux était plus court que les autres."
  - The notebook's two dirhams, given away without looking, come back as "le train de ma fille." Her lifelong unclaimed kindness returns unseen while she watches the wrong thing. This is the moral structure of the story, and it is never stated.
- "Même prix pour une minute ou pour un jour" set against "*Passage de l'agent : 11 h 24*" and the minute-by-minute countdown.
  - The frame turns a parking fine into the cost of fleeing, which is no cheaper than staying. The countdown is also what carries momentum through the middle.

## Scores

**Hook: 3.**
- "Devant Bab Boujloud, Khadija a lu l'autocollant du parcmètre comme une étiquette de prix" gives a vivid voice, and "personne ne répondait non plus" is a strong fourth line.
- But no narrative question exists until "elle avait la paire," after a long stretch of texture.
- To earn a 4, the stakes or the watching would need to arrive within the first page.

**Momentum: 4.**
- From the pair onwards, the countdown ("Quarante-trois minutes… Vingt-quatre… Neuf… Six… Zéro") pulls hard, and section 3 and the climb are gripping.
- Points are lost to the section 1 inventory, the Hamid aside, and the opaque "Trois cent quatre-vingts" flash, which costs attention at exactly the wrong moment.

**Recognition: 4 (gate passes).**
- Her habit of judgement ("Une voleuse… Ça se reconnaît aux mains") turns back on her: the woman she suspects is caring for a woman with her name, her shoe size, her henna and grey, her hip. That woman laughs, lets herself be scrubbed, and goes into the sea "jusqu'au ventre," the life Khadija did not take.
- "son dos, que personne n'avait frotté" answers something about my own life without saying it.
- It falls short of a 5 only because the Fatiha link has to be deduced rather than felt, so some readers will miss what the recognition is about.
