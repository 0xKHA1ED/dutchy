## Versions compared

**A**
- **Best moment:** the Hamid aside. "Ma dynamique. Je passe des yaourts devant un laser à trois heures du matin. Écris ça." This is the funniest, most observed line in any version, and it is pure cashier voice. A also has the half-erased shoe size, "un trois, et la moitié d'un huit," which is a better image than any later wording.
- **Most serious problem:** the ending's logic is thin.
  - The agent is never planted at the start. "Le gilet… prend son café avant" refers to someone we have never seen.
  - Khadija arrives at "Zéro" with the ticket already issued at 11:24, and nothing helps the reader see that her perfect clock failed her.
  - "*Fatiha*" floats free of the fourteen-year-old memory of letters traced on a back, so the reader cannot connect them. The cold reader flagged exactly this.

**B**
- **Best moment:** the negative countdown, "Moins une. Moins deux… Moins six." It makes the cost of her flight audible, step by step.
- **Most serious problem:** B announces what the others enact.
  - "Vous aussi, vous avez déjà regardé une horloge comme ça" speaks directly to the listener and states the Recognition outright. This is the pretentiousness trap in one line.
  - "On ne regarde pas sa propre poche" hands over the mirror theme.
  - "pour une fois, elle a laissé son dos à tout le monde" explains her.
  - "Loin de sa main… plus facile pour…" spells out her suspicion.
  - Opening on the Nescafé pot instead of her voice reading the sticker gives a weaker hook.
  - Because she knows she is late before she arrives, the ticket no longer stings.

**C**
- **Best moment:** the stool. Khadija folds a scrap of soap wrapper "en quatre, coin sur coin," slides it under the short leg "sans lever les yeux," and the toc stops.
  - It turns her fold-in-four habit into quiet competence and care.
  - It is a small fix of the outside world, the exact opposite of what she will refuse to do with the face on the marble.
- **Most serious problem:** C swaps the yaourts/laser line for "Mon dynamisme, il est là, à deux mètres, avec un sac qui n'est pas à elle." The new line ties into the plot but is less alive. "Un trente-huit, à moitié effacé" is also flatter than A's half-eight.

## What changed between versions

**B, compared with A:** B mostly added explanation (direct address, thematic glosses) and moved the countdown into negative numbers. Every addition reduces trust in the listener. B also lost A's hook order.

**C, compared with A:** C kept A's structure and restraint, and made targeted repairs.
- **The agent is planted.** "Sur l'une d'elles, un agent en gilet orange buvait un café" appears at the start. At the end, "À la même table, le gilet orange avait un autre verre devant lui." Now the ticket is earned: he did his round at 11:24 and came back. Her theory about him was right, and she was still late.
- **Fatiha is connected.** "Dans sa tête, une lettre, puis une autre. *Fatiha.*" links the name to the memory of letters traced on a back without explaining either.
- **The broken sentence becomes a cover.** "… comme elle s'échauffe… Comme avant un match" turns A's unfinished "Regarde comme elle…" into a visible self-cover. She almost names the scrubber's gesture and rescues herself with a joke. That is the pitch's mechanism, performed rather than stated.
- **More clues, still unspoken.** C adds the phantom-sleeve gesture again just before "Khadija." It also adds "ma Dija" to "Ya está."
- **Losses:** the laser line, the half-eight, and the coin dropping into the biscuit box at the exit.

On balance, C fixed real problems: the ticket's logic, the Fatiha thread, and a planted payoff. It added one excellent invented action and lost two lovely details.

## Choice

**C.** It keeps everything that made A alive: the cashier's running commentary, the two-dirham coin travelling to the scrubber's headscarf, the finger reveal, the eyes climbing past the turning shoulder to the clock. It also repairs the two places where A asked too much of the listener, the agent and Fatiha. The stool repair is the best new moment in any revision, because it shows what Khadija's hands do when she is not afraid. Unlike B, C never tells the listener what to recognise. The ticket, the empty pocket and the thumb on the earring carry the ending alone.

## Final scores for the chosen version

- **Hook: 4.** "Cent dirhams. Même prix pour une minute ou pour un jour. Tu te rends compte." Voice and a ticking meter arrive at once. A 5 would need the opening to raise the question of the woman herself, not just the clock.
- **Momentum: 4.** The countdown ("Encore quarante-trois minutes au parcmètre… Vingt-quatre… Neuf") drives each room, and every room raises the stakes. The opening car-and-entry stretch is slightly long before "Trois minutes plus tard, elle avait la paire."
- **Surprise: 4.** The scrubber's short finger ("L'un d'eux était plus court que les autres. Le doigt.") rests on the March notebook entry planted on page one. "Deux femmes ont répondu" lands hard. Both feel inevitable on reflection. The doubling of the round woman becomes guessable by the warm room.
- **Psychological truth: 4.** She builds a thief case to avoid a face, and she refuses the paid scrub four times. Her eyes climb "Plus haut que les pieds. Plus haut que le sac" until they reach the clock. Contradiction is shown, never named.
- **Recognition: 4.** She chooses the clock over the turning shoulder, and then "choosing" the ticket is enacted. The 37 ballerinas "Sous le lit, encore dans le papier" are the unworn life. The listener feels the flinch. A 5 would need the double's identity to bite a little more personally, rather than staying half-symbolic.
- **Invention: 4.** A hammam chase timed by a parking meter, and a cashier's notebook of unpaid two-dirham debts that comes back as a bath scrubber. Phantom sleeves pushed up on bare arms. All of this is fresh.
- **Lightness: 4.** The voice is genuinely funny ("Je suis un musée, pas un… pas un tapis !", "Trente-neuf. Comme un bateau."), and the ending stays wordless, closing on "Son pouce est monté jusqu'à la boucle gauche, et il est resté là." It loses a little against A because the laser line is gone.
- **Ear: 4.** The repeated refrains read well aloud: "Ton gommage est payé," "La goutte tombait sur la claquette," the step counts. Every chapter ends on a number. Some ambiguities, such as the 380/190 jewellery flash, will not carry for a cold listener.
