## Decision

Skeleton 1 wins because it is the only plan where the surface question is answered by the deep one without a single line of explanation.

The chase gives the listener a clean case to enjoy: purse, slow glove, a thin woman who "doesn't look up." The rooms move them physically closer to a back that carries Khadija's shoulder, foot and joke. The answer to "Will she catch them before the meter runs out?" is no. She runs out of time because, at the one moment the back begins to turn, her oldest move fires: she answers a feeling with a number.

She pays the city on time, or tries to. The ticket was written anyway. So the account she closes is the wrong one, and closing it saved her nothing. That is the bitter ending the recipe asks for, and it is the deep question enacted: she spent the hour paying every small debt so she would never have to see the refused life lived. The hoops under her thumb as she folds the ticket are the only confession the story gets.

## Graft

**From Skeleton 2: the two-dirham coin pressed into Souad's hand.**

- In the warm room, Khadija buys off the scrub with the last coin from the car jar: "Later, aafak. Go and do someone who wants it."
- It is the same coin Souad owes her from the notebook. Neither of them knows this yet.
- In the hot room, when Khadija recognises the finger, the debt has closed backwards. She paid a debtor not to touch her.
- At the end, the ticket is already under the wiper. She reaches into the jar anyway to feed the dead meter. This is the paying instinct with nothing left to pay for. The jar is empty. Her hand goes to the djellaba pocket, finds nothing, and moves to the hoop.

This replaces Skeleton 1's vague "time to buy and no hand free" with a cause the listener watched happen. She spent her last coin to avoid being touched.

## Weakest rung and fix

The weakest rung is the main turn. "She looks at the clock" is the beat the premise has been promising, so it risks arriving on schedule.

The fix is to build the turn on tell #1. All story long, Khadija's eyes never go above anyone's collarbone. At the turn, they rise for the first and only time, past the turning shoulder and over the heads, to the clock above the door. The first face-height thing she looks at in the whole story is a clock.

Cut the direct address. The lifted eyes already let the listener catch themselves doing it, and "You have looked at a clock like that" would explain the image.

The hot room is also crowded: Souad's line, the finger, the name, and the turn all fall in one stretch. Spread them out:

- Souad's line and the finger come at about 20 minutes.
- The name comes at about 12.
- The turn and the clock come at 9.

## Chosen skeleton (final)

**Structure:** Countdown inside a closed room. The hammam's rooms are the chambers (cold, warm, hot, then the door and the alleys). Each chapter ends on a minute-mark and a step closer to the back.

**Deep question:** When we refuse a life, do we spend the rest of ours paying every small debt on time, so we never have to see that life lived by someone else?

**Surface question:** Suspense: Will Khadija catch the thin woman with the purse before her meter runs out? In the hot room it darkens into dread: will she see the face before she can get away?

**Ladder:**

1. **Cold room (60 → 43).**
   - At Bab Boujloud she feeds the meter from the car jar. One two-dirham coin is left, and she pockets it, counting it by touch.
   - She folds Naïma's voucher in four and presses the fold with her thumbnail.
   - Inside, she undresses with her back to the tiles and the towel over her shoulders.
   - Within three minutes she has the pair: a thin woman carrying a heavy woman's purse from bench to bench, never opening it, pushing up sleeves she isn't wearing. "She carries it like eggs. Like eggs!"
   - She gives running commentary in the Detective's voice: black soap from a tub, eight dirhams at Bab Ftouh, and the 2014 catch.
   - Souad appears in the doorway: "Your scrub is paid, lalla." Khadija: "Later, lalla. I'm busy."
   - The heavy woman goes ahead of her into the warm room, left foot landing flat. Khadija follows, one bench behind.
   - Chapter ends: *Forty-three minutes.*
2. **Warm room (43 → 24).**
   - The glove is far too slow. "Nobody scrubs a back that slowly. Nobody. Unless the other hand has a job."
   - From the wall directly behind, Khadija sees a henna-auburn line over a grey parting, a left shoulder lower than the right, and a back. Only a back.
   - Dija: "Slowly, Fatiha, I'm a museum, not a carpet." Khadija laughs under her breath, then stops. She has made that joke herself, to nobody.
   - Souad comes back with her bucket. Khadija presses the last coin into her hand: "Later, aafak. Go and do someone who wants it." Souad tucks it into her headscarf knot.
   - Souad says the glove will catch her earrings and she should take them off. "No." Khadija turns the left one.
   - Her towel grows heavy.
   - The pair rise toward the hot room, and Khadija rises too, on the bad hip.
   - Chapter ends: *Twenty-four minutes.*
3. **Hot room (24 → 9).**
   - The towel soaks through and slides off. Her back is bare to the room.
   - Around 20 minutes, Souad sits beside her, the hand with the missing fingertip flat on her own knee: "You've been watching her back for an hour. Do you want me to tell you what yours looks like?" Khadija: "Forty-one minutes. Not an hour."
   - Then she sees the finger. *The finger. Two dirhams, in the notebook.* The coin in the headscarf knot was hers already.
   - Around 12 minutes, the thin woman says "Khadija", low, to the back. Two women answer: the heavy woman a full second late, and Khadija's mouth already open.
   - A name comes into Khadija's head, *Fatiha*, once. She pushes it back like a coin across a counter.
   - The glove stops. The heavy woman begins, slowly, on the stiff left hip, to turn.
4. **The turn (9).** Khadija's eyes rise above collarbone height for the first time in the story, past the turning shoulder, to the clock above the door. *Nine minutes.*
5. **Door and alleys (9 → –4).**
   - Among the wet slippers stand black flats with a gold buckle, size 38, resoled three times, soft at the heels.
   - She steps into her Velcro pharmacy sandals, size 39.
   - The flash comes in numbers only: a biscuit tin, 380, half, 190, a jeweller's counter.
   - She takes the alleys on the bad hip, counting steps against minutes.

**Main turn:** She looks at the clock instead of the face. The only time her eyes lift above anyone's collarbone, they lift to a number. The Counter wins with "Nine minutes."

**Rejected likely beats:**

- The back turns and the face is a mirror, or a reunion.
- Khadija exposes the "con" and is proved right or wrong in front of the room.
- Khadija breaks and confesses to Souad, or finally lies down for the scrub.

**Ending sketch:**

- The ticket is already under the wiper, written four minutes after the meter ran out. Leaving bought her nothing.
- She reaches into the car jar to pay for the lost time anyway. It is empty. Her hand goes to her pocket and finds nothing.
- She folds the ticket in four, corner to corner, and presses the fold with her thumbnail.
- Her thumb goes to the left hoop and stays there.

**Reframe:**

- "She carries it like eggs" was trust.
- The slow glove was waiting, not a con.
- The earrings she would not take off were the only stolen goods in the building.
- The coin she spent to avoid a hand on her back was already owed to her. She paid every creditor except the one.

**Recognition moment:** At "Nine minutes" the listener is relieved she is going, because they don't want to see the face either. The ticket under the wiper shows them that the relief was paid for, and that it bought nothing. It was the same small, reasonable not-looking they have done themselves.

**Wildcard:** Dija is seen only from behind in every room, because Khadija always sits behind her. The one time Dija begins to face us, Khadija's eyes, and the story's, go to the clock.

**Chapters:** 4.

1. Cold room, ends 43.
2. Warm room, ends 24.
3. Hot room, ends 9 on the clock.
4. Door and alleys, ends –4 on the ticket.
