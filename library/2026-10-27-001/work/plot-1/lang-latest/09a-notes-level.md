**Words the script missed:**
- *"elles allaient se faire"* (shoes breaking in) is a rare sense of *se faire*.
- *"votre dynamique"* is corporate jargon. It reads as a joke, so the stakes are low.
- *"le gilet"* is used as a metonym for the parking agent.
- *"le seau"* is used as a metonym for the scrubber.

**Stretch words outside the palette: about 75 tokens (about 55 distinct). Budget: about 37. Story length: 3,737 words.**

Most of these are concrete and shown, but the total is about double the budget. It is concentrated in three clusters: the opening square, the changing room, and the uphill street in ch. 4.

## Grammar

The grammar is clean overall. There is no passé simple, no subjonctif passé or imparfait, and no *lequel*. The tenses are présent, passé composé, imparfait and futur proche. The plus-que-parfait is used only for clearly framed flashbacks: *"Khadija les avait mis de sa poche"*, *"En 2014… elle avait vu"*.

- *"Les talons avaient été refaits tant de fois qu'ils faisaient une petite pile"*: passive in the plus-que-parfait plus *tant… que*. This is a "use with care" structure. Recognisable, but stacked on a stretch word.
- *"jusqu'à ce qu'il soit bien plat"*: subjonctif after a trigger that isn't on the common list. Recognisable from context. Fine.
- *"Tu veux que je te dise à quoi ressemble le tien ?"*: subjonctif after *vouloir que*, plus an inverted possessive. Fine in speech.
- *"ne me fais pas demander deux fois"*: negative imperative with causative *faire*. It slows the reader, but the speaker's tone carries it. Fine.
- *"Il manquait un bout de doigt à cette main, et il lui manquait aussi deux dirhams"*: impersonal *manquer* twice. The *lui* refers to the customer, but a learner could read it as Khadija. Minor.
- *"C'est là qu'est la facture"*: inversion after *que*. Fine.
- *"Khadija n'a regardé que le seau"*: *ne… que*. Within level.
- *"Je ne vais nulle part"*: common. Fine.

## Sentences

**Average narration sentence length: about 9 words.** This is well within range. The dialogue is plentiful and short.

Long sentences:
- *"Le pot de Nescafé était dans le porte-gobelet de la Fiat, entre les deux sièges, au milieu des graines de tournesol vides de la semaine dernière."* (about 25 words) There are no subordinate clauses, but there are four prepositional phrases and two stretch words. Dense for the second paragraph.
- *"Ils ont passé l'épaule qui tournait, ils ont monté le mur, ils ont traversé la vapeur jusqu'à l'horloge blanche au-dessus de la porte, la seule chose nette dans la salle."* (about 30 words) The coordination is parallel and easy to follow. Acceptable, and it earns its length.
- *"Elle a plié le ticket en quatre, coin sur coin, et elle a passé l'ongle du pouce sur le pli, une fois, deux fois, jusqu'à ce qu'il soit bien plat."* (about 30 words) The gesture is already learned by this point. Acceptable.
- *"Un autre carrelage mouillé, elle avait quatorze ans, un doigt passait lentement sur son dos, une lettre, puis une autre, et elle devinait, elle devinait presque…"* This is a chain of short comma-spliced clauses. The breathless rhythm is the point, and *"elle avait quatorze ans"* marks the jump. Acceptable.

## Clarity

- **Countdown numbers.** The chapters end on bare numbers: *"Quarante-trois minutes."*, *"Vingt-quatre minutes."*, *"Neuf minutes."* Inside the chapters, *"Trente minutes. Vingt-neuf."* and *"Treize minutes. Douze."* appear with nothing linking them to the meter. Then *"Vingt-six minutes… Pas une heure."* measures elapsed time, while the others count down. A B1 listener could lose the clock that drives ch. 4.
- **"Le gilet."** It appears before the agent does.
- **The ch. 4 thought-flash** of numbers and objects (see Notes).
- **Seau metonymy (ch. 1).** *"Le seau est resté encore un moment. Puis il est parti, sans se presser."* "Sans se presser" hints at a person, but *"la femme au seau"* doesn't arrive until ch. 2.
- **Dija = Khadija.** *"Oui, Dija"* (ch. 2) is the only hint that the round woman shares the name. *"Deux femmes ont répondu"* largely rescues this.
- **Place changes** (salle froide → tiède → chaude → street) are always named at chapter starts. Clear.
- **Flashbacks** (March at the till, 2014, age fourteen) are each dated or marked. Clear.
- **"Fatiha."** It is marked as a thought by italics and *"Le nom est monté"*. Clear as language. Whose name it is belongs to subtext, which is outside my brief.

## Notes

1. **[fatal]** *"Devant Bab Boujloud, Khadija a lu l'autocollant du parcmètre comme une étiquette de prix."*, and the same density in ch. 1's changing room and ch. 4's street.
   - Problem: stretch vocabulary outside the palette runs at about 75 tokens against a budget of about 37. It is not spread evenly. It piles up where a learner has least context: the first page, the changing room, and the uphill race. Each word is guessable alone. Together they mean many more than "a few moments of guessing". This is why the Level gate fails.
   - Direction: the fix is swaps, not cuts. Replace incidental scenery nouns with core words in the three clusters (notes 2–4). Keep the stretch words that recur or are shown: *mule, essuie-glace, carton, scratch, cornet*. That brings the count near budget with no beat lost.

2. **[serious]** *"le porte-gobelet de la Fiat… au milieu des graines de tournesol vides"* / *"les chauffeurs penchés sur les capots"* / *"noué autour du poteau"*
   - Problem: the opening three paragraphs give the learner six or seven stretch words before the story has started.
   - Direction: lighten the noun stacks to core words. Keep the sunflower seeds, because they return.

3. **[serious]** *"Les talons avaient été refaits tant de fois…"* / *"Ressemelées trois fois."* / *"le caoutchouc a soupiré"* / *"une clé sur une ficelle rouge"*
   - Problem: the changing room adds a second cluster. The two cobbling words for one idea are the costliest.
   - Direction: use one core phrase for "repaired many times" and repeat it in ch. 2, so the echo teaches it. Simplify the door's rubber and one of the lost-property objects.

4. **[serious]** *"Une croix, un point, une capsule de bouteille enfoncée dans la pâte."*
   - Problem: three stretch words in a verbless list describing a cultural practice (bread marks on communal-oven boards). This comes at the moment the learner most needs to follow the countdown.
   - Direction: keep the bread boards, but use core words for the marks. *Rideau de fer, pavés* and *mule* can stay, because their scenes carry them.

5. **[serious]** *"Ma nouvelle hanche est dedans. Elle est payée."*
   - Problem: *hanche* is above level, missing from the palette, and used 7 times. This line is where the learner finds out what the bag is. A reader who doesn't know *hanche* loses why the bag matters.
   - Direction: add *hanche* to the palette. Make sure its first appearances in ch. 2 attach it unmistakably to a body part, through walking, a hand on it, or the uneven shoulder, before this line.

6. **[serious]** *"Le gilet commence par le bas de la rue, a-t-elle dit. Et il prend son café avant."*
   - Problem: *gilet* (vest) is used as a metonym for the parking agent before any agent or vest has appeared. A B1 listener cannot decode it. The ticket reveals the meaning only afterwards.
   - Direction: plant the orange-vested agent at the square in ch. 1, among the taxis and café chairs, so the metonym in ch. 4 points back to something seen.

7. **[serious]** *"Quarante-trois minutes."* / *"Vingt-quatre minutes."* / *"Vingt-six minutes, a dit Khadija tout de suite. Pas une heure."*
   - Problem: the bare numbers are never tied to the meter for a learner. One of them (*vingt-six*) counts up while the rest count down. A B1 listener may not realise there is a clock at all, and ch. 4 depends on it.
   - Direction: anchor the countdown once, early (for example, Khadija glancing at or thinking of the *fin 11 h 20*). Let the elapsed *vingt-six* stay visibly an answer to *"depuis une heure"*, which it already is in dialogue. One anchor is enough. Don't label every number.

8. **[serious]** *"Une boîte à biscuits. Trois cent quatre-vingts. La moitié. Cent quatre-vingt-dix. Un comptoir en verre. Un plateau de velours."*
   - Problem: six verbless fragments with large numbers and two stretch words. A learner cannot tell this is Khadija calculating. Nor can they connect *190* to *"Cent quatre-vingt-dix, a dit Khadija"* in ch. 2, which itself has no hook tying the number to the earrings.
   - Direction: keep the fragments and their withholding. Give the listener one physical hook, such as the thumb on the earring, either here or at the ch. 2 line, so *190* reads as the earrings' value. Don't explain the decision.

9. **[minor]** *"On m'avait dit qu'elles allaient se faire."*
   - Problem: *se faire* meaning "to break in" (of shoes) is a rare sense. The point that she bought them too small may be lost.
   - Direction: make "they would get bigger / stop hurting" available in core words.

10. **[minor]** *"Il manquait un bout de doigt à cette main, et il lui manquait aussi deux dirhams."*
    - Problem: the second *lui* could be read as Khadija.
    - Direction: make the customer the clear referent of the second *manquait*.

11. **[minor]** *"Le seau est resté encore un moment. Puis il est parti, sans se presser."*
    - Problem: the bucket-as-person device may be read literally until ch. 2.
    - Direction: let the words *la femme au seau* appear once in ch. 1. The avoidance device survives, because the label is still the bucket.

12. **[minor]** *"Oui, Dija."* → *"Khadija. Deux femmes ont répondu."*
    - Problem: a learner may not know *Dija* is short for *Khadija*.
    - Direction: let the learner hear *Dija* one more time before the climax, so the echo can register without being explained.

13. **[minor]** *"Amende : cent dirhams."*
    - Problem: the palette word appears only once.
    - Direction: let *amende* return in ch. 4 at the ticket.

14. **[minor]** *"Safi, a dit Khadija à la machine."* / *"Plus tard, aafak."*
    - Problem: uncued Darija.
    - Direction: optional. Nothing hangs on these words, and they add voice.

## Protect

- *"Ton gommage est payé, lalla." / "Plus tard."* A refrain in core words that recurs four times. The learner absorbs it, and its absence in ch. 3 is felt. Its simplicity is exactly what carries the story at B1.
- *"Elle l'a plié en quatre, coin sur coin, et elle a appuyé sur le pli avec l'ongle du pouce."* Repetition turns *plier, pli, pouce, ongle* into learned vocabulary by the end. The final fold of the ticket lands because the learner owns the words.
- *"— Khadija. / Deux femmes ont répondu."* The climax in four core words. Don't let any clarity fix add explanation here.

## Scores

**Level: 1 (fail).**
- What fails: stretch vocabulary runs at about twice the budget, clustered at the opening (*"autocollant… étiquette de prix… porte-gobelet… capots… poteau"*) and in the ch. 4 street (*"capsule de bouteille enfoncée dans la pâte"*). Two comprehension blocks sit at key moments: *"Le gilet commence par le bas de la rue"* and the unanchored countdown numbers.
- What already passes: the grammar is clean and the sentences average about 9 words.
- What would pass: word swaps in three clusters, *hanche* added to the palette, and one anchor each for the gilet and the clock. No beat needs cutting.
