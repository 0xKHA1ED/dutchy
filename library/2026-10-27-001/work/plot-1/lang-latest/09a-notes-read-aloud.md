## Speakers

Most dialogue is tagged well for audio. Khadija's muttering is almost always attributed (*a murmuré Khadija*, *a-t-elle dit à personne*), and the Hamid imitation is set up and closed in narration (*Elle a pris la voix de Hamid… Puis elle a repris la sienne*). A single TTS voice can carry that. Three places are risky:

- **Two "lalla" lines in a row.** "— Ton gommage est payé, lalla." then "— Plus tard, lalla. Je suis occupée." Only the narration about the bucket separates them. Both lines end or begin with *lalla*, so the ear may hear one speaker continuing.
- **The aside inside the round woman's line.** "Je suis un musée, pas un… — elle riait déjà — pas un tapis !" The TTS will read *elle riait déjà* in the same voice, inside the quote. The listener hears the character say "elle riait déjà."
- **The climax.** "— Khadija. / Deux femmes ont répondu." The name stands alone as a line of speech. The listener has heard *Khadija* begin narration sentences for three chapters, so the isolated word is likely to be heard as the start of a narration sentence that never arrives. The cue *Elle a parlé bas, presque dans l'épaule* comes before it and helps, but the bare name still sits where narration usually starts.

## Trip-ups

- "Le son montait pendant que le seau se remplissait": *son / seau / se* blur together on headphones in a noisy street.
- "Quelqu'un l'avait poussé en arrière et noué autour du poteau, comme un foulard tombé sur le cou": the *l'* (the plastic bag) is two sentences back. By ear the image is hard to assemble.
- "assez pour montrer presque la dent en or du fond": *presque* in this position makes the listener re-parse.
- **Number load across the whole story.** This is the biggest trip-up for the ear. Several counting systems run at once:
  - dirhams (2, 7, 8, 10, 100, 190, 380)
  - shoe sizes (37, 38, 39)
  - minutes remaining
  - minutes elapsed
  - years (14, 27, 63, 2014)
  - days (21)

  For a B1 listener, *quatre-vingt-dix* and *quatre-vingts* are slow to decode. The numbers that carry meaning collide with ones that don't.

## Homophones

- **"— Oui, Dija."** This is the plant for the climax: the round woman is also Khadija. Spoken after *Oui*, *Dija* is very close to *déjà*. "Oui, déjà" is grammatical and plausible as a reply to *Attention avec ça*, so a learner will hear it and the plant will be lost.
- **"— Na'am ?"** A French TTS will likely say [nam] or [na.am]. A learner who doesn't know Arabic can hear *nan ?* (familiar *non*), which reverses the meaning at the turn. "Deux femmes ont répondu" partly rescues it, but the ear's first impression is wrong.
- **"Le seau est resté encore un moment. Puis il est parti."** *Seau / sot* is a real risk here. The metonymy (the bucket stands in for the woman) makes the person-reading more tempting: "le sot est parti." The device itself is excellent, because Khadija never looks above the bucket. It just needs the bucket firmly anchored as an object first.
- **"— Cent dirhams."** (last spoken line). It comes right after *sans rien dedans*, so a listener may hear *sans dirhams*. This happens to resonate with the empty pot and pocket. I'd leave it: the mishearing points the right way.
- **"La Paire" / "elle avait la paire."** *Paire / père / perd* sound alike. The feminine article helps, and "elle avait la paire" is opaque by ear to a learner whatever they hear. It's minor, but the title's meaning depends on the page.

## Names

- **Khadija** will be fine in TTS. Two women sharing it is the design, and the narration wisely calls the other one *la femme ronde* throughout.
- **Fatiha** appears once, in italics, never introduced. See Page-only devices.
- **Naïma** ("Le bon de Naïma") is never placed. The ear gets a name with no person attached.
- **Foreign words:**
  - *Venga* and *Ya está*: French TTS will nasalise *Venga* (vin-ga). That's acceptable, and the narration says *De l'espagnol*.
  - *aafak*: the doubled *a* may produce an odd stutter.
  - *Safi*, *Balak*, *lalla*, *kessa*, *Bab Boujloud*, *Bab Ftouh*, *Batha*, *Hamid*: all should be fine.

## Rhythm

Generally well varied, and often strongly aural:

- "Clac. Sur le petit écran : dix minutes. Clac. Vingt."
- "Toc… Toc… Plus de toc."
- The bucket line: "Rouge, vert, rouge, jaune."

One weak stretch: chapter 1 runs a long time on setup before the pair appears ("Trois minutes plus tard, elle avait la paire"). That setup covers the meter, the door, the changing bench, the lost-property nail and the mirror, all in medium-length descriptive sentences. On the page it's texture; on a commute the listener may drift before the story's engine starts. The changes of pace are already there (the claquette exchange, "Toc"). The middle of the changing-room block could use one.

## Chapter hooks

- **Ch. 1: "Quarante-trois minutes."** Works. The countdown has been set up (11 h 20, *jamais trompée d'une minute*). It's bare, but the listener will feel the clock.
- **Ch. 2: "Vingt-quatre minutes."** Works, but the real hook is the line before it: "Sa main est allée à sa propre hanche." The count risks flattening it. It's still effective because the countdown is now a familiar refrain.
- **Ch. 3: "Neuf minutes."** Strong. She looks at the clock instead of the turning face. The number lands as an avoidance, and the listener will want to know whether she looks.
- **Ch. 4 (final): "Son pouce est monté jusqu'à la boucle gauche, et il est resté là."** Lands. The gesture has been repeated all story, so the ear recognises it without help, and *il est resté là* closes softly.

## Time and place markers

- **Clear:** chapter openings name the room aloud ("Dans la salle tiède", "Dans la salle chaude", "Salle tiède, salle froide"). The memories mostly carry spoken markers ("au mois de mars", "En 2014, à la caisse quatre", "elle avait quatorze ans").
- **Missing: the chapter 4 memory.** "Une boîte à biscuits. Trois cent quatre-vingts. La moitié. Cent quatre-vingt-dix. Un comptoir en verre. Un plateau de velours." There is no spoken signal that this is the past.
  - Worse, the ear's most recent *boîte à biscuits* is the hammam cash tin from chapter 1, so the listener will place this in the hammam.
  - The numbers then run straight into "— Six, a dit Khadija", the countdown, so the earring price and the minutes blur.
- **"Le gilet commence par le bas de la rue"** comes before the listener has met any orange vest. By ear it's a riddle until "un gilet orange" two paragraphs later.

## Page-only devices

- **"*Fatiha.*"** Italics are the only signal that this is a name rising in her mind and not spoken. Aloud it's an unattributed word in a scene where people have just been calling names. Many listeners will think someone in the room said it.
- **"Je suis un musée, pas un… — elle riait déjà — pas un tapis !"** The dashes mark the aside on the page. Aloud, the aside sits inside the dialogue.
- **"« Le doigt. Pain, briquet. 2 dh. Mars. »"** The TTS will likely read *2 dh* as "deux dé-ache."
- **"Le bon de Naïma était dans son autre main. *Bon pour un gommage.*"** The italics mark this as printed text. Aloud, *Le bon* then *Bon pour…* may be heard as the adjective "good."
- **Correctly framed for the ear:** *fin 11 h 20* ("L'écran a affiché"), *Vigilance 2014* ("un diplôme"), *Lalla, ton sac est dans la main de la mince* ("Elle avait déjà la phrase"), and the parking ticket ("elle l'a lu").

## Notes

1. **[serious]** "— Khadija. / Deux femmes ont répondu." The climactic call is a bare name in the slot where narration sentences begin, so the ear may hear it as narration and miss that it is spoken. Direction: give the name an audible speech frame, a tag or narration that marks it as a call, while keeping the beat before "Deux femmes ont répondu."

2. **[serious]** "*Fatiha.*" Only italics mark this as interior. Aloud it is an orphan word that sounds spoken, by someone unknown, in a scene about who answers to which name. Direction: make it audible that this name comes up inside Khadija. Give the ear a thread back to the fourteen-year-old memory, without explaining who Fatiha is.

3. **[serious]** "— Oui, Dija." This is heard as *oui, déjà*, which is grammatical and plausible, and the plant for the climax is lost. Direction: make the nickname land unmistakably as a name, through its placement in the sentence or a second occurrence.

4. **[serious]** "— Na'am ?" The TTS may mangle it, and a learner may hear *nan ?* (no), reversing the meaning at the turn. Direction: let the narration or an action make the round woman's answer read as yes to a French ear, without glossing it like a dictionary.

5. **[serious]** "Vingt-quatre minutes." (end of ch. 2), then "— Vingt-six minutes, a dit Khadija tout de suite. Pas une heure.", then "Treize minutes. Douze." The elapsed count and the remaining count are both bare numbers in adjacent passages. Aloud, the clock seems to go up, then down. Direction: give the two counts audibly different shapes or anchor words, so the ear never confuses elapsed with remaining.

6. **[serious]** "Une boîte à biscuits. Trois cent quatre-vingts. La moitié. Cent quatre-vingt-dix. Un comptoir en verre. Un plateau de velours." A memory with no spoken marker. The biscuit box points the ear back to the hammam till, and the numbers run into the countdown ("— Six"). Direction: give an audible signal that we've left the present, and separate this from the countdown so the earring price can be recognised as the 190 from chapter 2.

7. **[serious]** "Je suis un musée, pas un… — elle riait déjà — pas un tapis !" The aside is spoken in the character's voice inside the quote. Direction: move the laugh out of the dialogue into narration, so the voice stays inside the quote and the narration stays outside it.

8. **[minor]** "— Cent quatre-vingt-dix, a dit Khadija, sans arrêter de tourner." The number has no unit or anchor, so by ear it's just a number, yet the chapter 4 memory depends on it. Direction: give the figure one anchoring word so the listener files it.

9. **[minor]** "« Le doigt. Pain, briquet. 2 dh. Mars. »" The abbreviation will be read as letters. Direction: render the notebook entry so a voice can say it naturally while still sounding like a terse note.

10. **[minor]** "Le seau est resté encore un moment. Puis il est parti, sans se presser." *Seau / sot* plus the metonymy invites a "fool" reading. Direction: keep the device, but make sure the first appearance fixes the bucket as an object at someone's feet before the bucket starts acting.

11. **[minor]** "— Ton gommage est payé, lalla." / "— Plus tard, lalla. Je suis occupée." Two *lalla* lines with no tag on the second. Direction: a light attribution on Khadija's refusal.

12. **[minor]** "Le gilet commence par le bas de la rue." The shorthand comes before the referent. Direction: let the ear meet the parking agent's vest, at least in passing, before Khadija uses the shorthand.

13. **[minor]** "Le bon de Naïma était dans son autre main. *Bon pour un gommage.*" *Bon* can be heard as "good," and Naïma is unplaced. Direction: frame the voucher text as words written on the paper.

14. **[minor]** "Le son montait pendant que le seau se remplissait." *Son / seau / se* blur together aloud. Direction: break the sibilant run so *son* and *seau* aren't side by side.

## Protect

- **The step countdown:** "— Cent pas. Cinq minutes. Deux cents. Quatre." … "— Une minute pour une mule. Cinq cents. Une." … "— Zéro, a dit Khadija." This is the best audio writing in the piece. The first pair teaches the pattern, then the ear can follow it walking, and it falls into her own footsteps. The mule interruption is perfect timing. Keep it exactly.
- **The refrains:** "Elle l'a plié en quatre, coin sur coin, et elle a appuyé sur le pli avec l'ongle du pouce." / "Son pouce est monté à sa boucle d'oreille gauche." / "Ton gommage est payé, lalla." A listener can't flip back, but repetition does the re-reading for them. The fold appears four times, last on the parking ticket, and the ear completes it.
- **"Deux femmes ont répondu." … "Khadija a fermé la bouche sur rien."** A clean, sayable turn and a physical aftermath. The fix in Note 1 should support this beat, not replace it.

## Scores

**Ear: 3.** Much of the story is built for the ear:

- Room names open each chapter.
- The refrains carry memory.
- Onomatopoeia does real work ("Clac… Vingt", "Toc").
- The ch. 4 step-and-minute count is excellent audio craft.

But audibility breaks down exactly at the climax. "— Khadija." risks being heard as narration, "Na'am ?" risks being heard as *nan*, "*Fatiha.*" is italics-only, and the plant "Oui, Dija" sounds like *oui, déjà*. Add the colliding counts ("Vingt-quatre minutes" then "Vingt-six minutes… Pas une heure"), and the listener's grip loosens where it matters most. To earn a 4 or 5, the turn in chapter 3 and the chapter 4 memory need to be as clear to the ear as the walk up the ruelle already is.
