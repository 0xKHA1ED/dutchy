## Summary

Khadija Amrani is 63 and has worked nights as a supermarket cashier for 27 years. She has 21 nights left before retirement. She parks her Fiat by Bab Boujloud and feeds the meter six two-dirham coins for one hour, ending at 11:20. The machine rejects a seventh coin, and she pockets it. The coin reminds her of a customer in March who was missing part of a finger and was two dirhams short. Khadija paid the difference, as she always does, and wrote it in her notebook. She goes into a hammam with a scrub voucher from Naïma. She puts her size-39 sandals beside worn black ballerinas whose gold buckle matches a pair she owns but never wore, because they were too small.

Inside, she decides that a thin woman carrying a round woman's handbag is a thief. She watches the pair through the cold, warm and hot rooms. The scrubber with the blue bucket keeps telling her that her scrub is paid for. Khadija refuses each time and gives her the two-dirham coin, which the scrubber says is for her daughter's train to Rabat.

Small details pile up:
- The thin woman speaks Spanish.
- She calls the round woman "ma Dija."
- The bag holds the money for a new hip.
- The round woman shares Khadija's flat left foot, bad left hip, henna over grey and size 39.

In the hot room, Khadija sees that the scrubber is missing part of a finger: she is the March customer. Then the thin woman says "Khadija." Both women nearly answer, but the round woman says "Na'am?" The name "Fatiha" surfaces in Khadija's mind. As the round woman turns over, Khadija looks away at the clock and leaves without seeing her face, her back unscrubbed. She hurries up the street, counting the minutes, and arrives to find a 100-dirham ticket issued at 11:24. She has no coins left. Who the round woman really is stays open.

## Unclear moments

1. "Ça, c'est le geste d'une…" She breaks off twice when the thin woman pushes up sleeves she isn't wearing. It probably means a professional scrubber (the thin woman also says "je suis pas payée aujourd'hui"), but the text never says.
2. "Un autre carrelage mouillé, elle avait quatorze ans, un doigt passait lentement sur son dos, une lettre, puis une autre…" and later "*Fatiha.*" I can't tell whether Fatiha traced the letters, was the name being traced, or is connected to the round woman.
3. "Une autre boîte à biscuits, autrefois. Trois cent quatre-vingts. La moitié. Cent quatre-vingt-dix. Un comptoir en verre. Un plateau de velours." This seems to mean the gold earrings (worth 190) were half of a 380-dirham pair bought with someone else, perhaps Fatiha. That may explain the title, but it is my guess.
4. The round woman's identity. She is called Khadija/Dija and mirrors Khadija's body exactly. Whether she is a stranger, a long-lost person, or a kind of double is never resolved, apparently on purpose.
5. "Tu veux que je te dise à quoi ressemble le tien ?" The scrubber seems to be hinting that Khadija's own back looks like the one she is watching, but this is implied, not stated.
6. The parking timing. Khadija says "Zéro" on arriving, yet the ticket says the agent came at 11:24. Either the mule cost her more than she counted or her famous sense of time failed. The text doesn't say which.

## The last line

"Son pouce est monté jusqu'à la boucle gauche, et il est resté là." (Her thumb went up to her left earring and stayed there.)

I understood it as her nervous habit going still: she is left alone with the fine, the empty pocket and the old earring, half of a pair, still unpaid in some way.
