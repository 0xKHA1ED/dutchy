## Overview
**Structure:** A countdown inside a closed building. The hammam's rooms are the chapters: cold, then warm, then hot, then the door and the alleys. Each room is hotter and closer to the back. The meter runs outside the walls, and every chapter ends on a minute-mark and a step closer to the truth. Comic in Chapters 1–2. The comedy cracks at the end of Chapter 2 and turns tragic in Chapter 3. Chapter 4 is bitter, with one half-laugh left in it.

**Chapters:** 4. Approximate total: 4,100 words (Ch1 ~1,050, Ch2 ~1,000, Ch3 ~1,050, Ch4 ~1,000).

**Refrain:**
- **Main phrase refrain:** the paired exchange *"Your scrub is paid, lalla."* / *"Later."*
  - Returns in 1.4 and 2.3.
  - In 3.2 it comes back without the "Later": Khadija can't produce it.
  - Its last return is inverted in 4.4. Nobody offers her anything. A ticket demands payment, and she reaches for coins to pay for time that is already gone.
- **Structural refrain:** the minute count that closes every chapter: 1.4 *Forty-three*, 2.4 *Twenty-four*, 3.4 *Nine*, 4.3 *−4*, printed on the ticket.
- **Two short echo pairs:**
  - *"Same price for one minute or one day. Can you believe it."* (1.1). In 4.3 it is unsaid; the sum is read aloud instead.
  - *"If you're going, go."* (1.2 → 4.1).

**Symbolic object:** the shoes. Appearances:
- 1.2: black buckle flats at the door, plus her joke about her own size-37s "still in the tissue", plus her Velcro 39s.
- 2.1: her game of guessing shoe sizes from bare feet. The thin woman is "thirty-eight, narrow" and the heavy woman is "thirty-nine."
- 4.1: three sizes in one doorway.

**Secret object:** the gold hoops.
- 1.3: turned at "thief."
- 2.3: "Take them off." / "No." / "Old gold."
- 3.1: turned at "paid for."
- 4.4: the thumb stays.

**Closing echo:** the ticket, folded in four. The fold motif runs from the voucher (1.1) to the receipt (1.2) to the ticket (4.4).

**Sounds, one per place:**
- Bab Boujloud: the clunk of each coin dropping into the meter.
- Cold room: buckets scraping on tile, echoing.
- Warm room: the tap filling buckets, always running.
- Hot room: water hissing on the hot floor, and drips falling from the dome.
- Alleys: a porter's *"Balak!"* and hooves on stone.

**Hammam vocabulary (B1, keep to these six):**
- *le seau* (the bucket)
- *le savon noir* (black soap)
- *le gant* (the glove; *kessa* may appear once as colour)
- *les claquettes* (the slippers at the door)
- *le carrelage* (the tiles)
- *la serviette* (the towel)

Rooms are simply *la salle froide / tiède / chaude*.

**Upstream fixes applied:**
- Dija's joke in Ch2 does not contain "Fatiha." The name lands once, in Khadija's head, in 3.3.
- Souad's line stays *"for an hour"*, but Khadija's correction is changed from "Forty-one minutes" to **"Twenty-six minutes."** The arithmetic: meter at 60 when she parks, 11 minutes downhill, arrival at 49, pair spotted at about 46, Souad's line at about 20. That makes 26. Her precision is the joke, so the number must be right.

## Chapter 1: Like Eggs
**Exit hook:** *Forty-three minutes.* She sits down one bench behind a back.

### Scene 1.1
**Where and when:** Outside Bab Boujloud, mid-morning on women's day. The meter at the kerb. Minute 60.

**Who:** Khadija, alone.

**What happens:**
- She feeds the meter two-dirham coins from the coffee jar in her cup holder.
- The sticker says two-dirham coins only, one hour maximum, and a flat fine. She reads it the way she reads a price label: *"Same price for one minute or one day. Can you believe it."*
- One coin is left in the jar. The meter won't take more than an hour, so she pockets it and counts it by touch. Two dirhams reminds her of "the finger": a regular at the till she knows only by a hand with a short fingertip. Bread and a lighter, two dirhams short, March, still in the notebook. Twenty-one days before she leaves that notebook behind. *"Nights, really."*
- Optional: one Hamid impression ("a cake for your *dynamique*, Madame Amrani," in mocking *vous*).
- A price joke: for the fine you could take the night bus to Tangier. It is said once, as a joke. It is the only "Tangier" in her mouth all story.
- She takes Naïma's voucher out, a prepaid scrub. She folds it in four, corner to corner, and presses the fold with her thumbnail. Then she sets off: *"Eleven minutes. Downhill."*

**The turn:** From a woman who owns every minute and every coin to a woman walking down into a place where she will have to undress, holding something she didn't pay for.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Answering with prices and times.
- Counting coins by touch.
- Folding in four.
- Quick self-correction ("Twenty-one days. Nights, really.").
- *Safi*, *wallah*, "Can you believe it."

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious opening is backstory about retirement or loneliness, with her gazing at the gate. Instead it is pure logistics and jokes. The only interior is arithmetic.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - She is three weeks from retirement and came on a gift voucher.
  - Fatiha Kaddour, her girlhood friend who left for Tangier in 1981, is back in Fès. She and her companion of 28 years, Khadija "Dija" Trabelsi, are in this hammam today.
  - The meter's fine is flat, so the clock is meaningless as a reason to hurry.
- **Character believes:** This is an errand: a scrub, an hour, back to the car. She would never overstay a meter.
- **Listener believes:** A funny, exact, slightly prickly working woman on a day out. This is low-stakes comedy.
- **Clue planted:**
  - The flat-fine sticker.
  - "Eleven minutes. Downhill."
  - The last coin pocketed.
  - The finger, two dirhams, in the notebook.
  - Tangier as a bus-price joke.
  - The fold in four.

### Scene 1.2
**Where and when:** The hammam door and changing area. Minutes 49 → 46.

**Who:** Khadija; a woman dawdling at the door; the desk woman.

**What happens:**
- A woman stands in the doorway half-in, half-out, talking on her phone. Khadija says *"If you're going, go,"* and squeezes past.
- She lines her black Velcro pharmacy sandals, size 39, among the wet slippers. Beside them stand black flats with a gold buckle, resoled so many times the heel is a little stack. She jokes under her breath: *"Same buckle as mine. Mine are thirty-seven. They said they'd stretch. They're still in the tissue."*
- She pays the small entry fee and folds the receipt in four, thumbnail on the fold.
- She counts the coin in her djellaba pocket by touch before she undresses.
- She undresses with her back to the tiles and puts the towel over her shoulders at once.
- She unpins her scarf. Henna, two weeks old, the grey already showing at the parting.
- The small mirror stops at her collarbones. She gives it a cashier's nod.
- A body joke to nobody: *"Sixty-three years. Look, don't touch. Like the museum at Batha."*
- She crosses the cold room with her back to everyone, the only time she does, to the far wall. Only then does she turn.

**The turn:** From covered and in control to almost naked in a public room. Then safe again, back against the tiles.

**Tells and voice used:**
- "If you're going, go."
- The fold.
- Counting coins.
- Back to the tiles, towel on the shoulders.
- Body comedy under her breath.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she lingers over the twin shoes, frowning, a mystery seeded. Instead the shoes get a throwaway joke and she moves on. The listener files it as character colour.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - The flats are Fatiha's: size 38, the twin of Khadija's 37s, bought together for the 1981 journey.
  - As Khadija crosses the room, her back and her walk are seen by Fatiha, who knows them in one glance.
- **Character believes:** The flats are a common model with a funny story attached to her own pair.
- **Listener believes:** A charming detail about a woman who buys things and never uses them.
- **Clue planted:**
  - Her "If you're going, go."
  - The buckle flats, "same buckle as mine."
  - Size 37, "they said they'd stretch."
  - Velcro 39s.
  - Her henna over grey.
  - The mirror that stops at the collarbones.
  - The "museum" joke.
  - She crosses the room with her back exposed.

### Scene 1.3
**Where and when:** The cold room. Minutes 46 → 44. Buckets scrape and echo.

**Who:** Khadija; the thin woman (Fatiha) and the heavy woman (Dija), both seen from Khadija's eye line. Dija only ever from behind.

**What happens:**
- Within three minutes the Detective has a case. A thin woman carries a heavy woman's purse from bench to bench. She never opens it, and she pushes up sleeves she isn't wearing. *"She carries it like eggs. Like eggs! That's not her purse, I promise you."*
- The commentary runs:
  - Black soap scooped from a tub, *"Eight dirhams at Bab Ftouh. She's paying for the tub."*
  - Who has claimed which bucket.
- She recalls 2014, her proudest catch at the till: *"Lalla, your purse is in the thin one's hand."* It earned her the Vigilance Award, framed by her sink at home. At the word "thief" she turns her left hoop.
- The thin woman's head lifts once toward the wall where Khadija sits, then goes back down. *"See? A thief always checks for the guard."* Khadija drops her eyes to hands and feet, her usual height.
- Every person in the room is described collarbone-down.

**The turn:** From a day out to a case. Comedy now has a target, and the listener is recruited.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Eyes never above the collarbone.
- Mock-detective deadpan.
- Exact prices.
- Earring turned at "thief."
- Going quiet while watching the sleeves pushed up.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she looks at the women's faces and something nags at her. Instead she never looks up at all. The glance *at her* is read as guilt. The listener's view stays locked to hands and backs.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - Fatiha carries Dija's purse out of trust. It holds a clinic deposit receipt for Dija's hip.
  - Fatiha's glance means she has recognised Khadija.
  - The only stolen goods in the building are in Khadija's ears: the hoops were bought with the tin, 190 of the 380 dirhams Fatiha's.
- **Character believes:** A two-woman purse con like 2014. She is the guard.
- **Listener believes:** Same as Khadija, and enjoying it. Wants the thief exposed.
- **Clue planted:**
  - "Like eggs."
  - Never opened.
  - Sleeves that aren't there.
  - The glance at the wall.
  - Earring turned at "thief."

### Scene 1.4
**Where and when:** The cold room doorway, then the threshold of the warm room. Minutes 44 → 43.

**Who:** Khadija; Souad (first appearance); Fatiha and Dija.

**What happens:**
- Souad stands in the doorway with a bucket and towel, arms folded, unhurried. *"Your scrub is paid, lalla."* Khadija doesn't look up: *"Later, lalla. I'm busy. Your glove isn't going anywhere."*
- Souad does not move off at once. She just watches. Khadija sees only the bucket.
- The heavy woman rises and walks ahead toward the warm room, her left foot landing flat. The thin woman follows with the purse.
- Khadija checks the clock in her head, gets up, and follows. She takes the bench directly behind them, one bench back. *Forty-three minutes.*

**The turn:** From watcher at a distance to follower. She has chosen to go deeper, and chosen to refuse the touch she has already been given.

**Tells and voice used:**
- "Later, lalla."
- Time as an answer.
- Back to the wall at the new bench.
- Eyes on the bucket, not on Souad.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she takes the scrub now and drops the case. Instead she defers it and follows. The refrain is born here.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - Souad is "the finger", one of Khadija's 4 a.m. regulars, and has recognised her.
  - Dija's flat left foot is Khadija's own walk.
- **Character believes:** The scrub can wait. The case can't.
- **Listener believes:** The chase is on. Souad is local colour.
- **Clue planted:**
  - "Your scrub is paid" / "Later."
  - The left foot landing flat.

## Chapter 2: A Museum, Not a Carpet
**Exit hook:** *Twenty-four minutes.* She rises on her hip and follows them into the heat.

### Scene 2.1
**Where and when:** The warm room. Minutes 43 → 37. The tap fills buckets endlessly.

**Who:** Khadija; Fatiha (hands, feet, voice); Dija (only from behind).

**What happens:**
- Khadija claims a bucket and a spot directly behind the pair, back to the tiles, towel on.
- The glove starts on the heavy woman's back, far too slowly. *"Nobody scrubs a back that slowly. Nobody. Unless the other hand has a job."*
- From behind, Khadija sees:
  - a henna-auburn line over a grey parting;
  - a left shoulder lower than the right;
  - a back. Only a back.
- She plays her till game of guessing shoe sizes from bare feet. The thin woman: *"thirty-eight, narrow."* The heavy woman: *"thirty-nine, like a boat."* She says nothing about her own.
- Fatiha pushes up her non-existent sleeves before taking up the glove. Khadija stops mid-sentence and goes quiet watching it.
- Fatiha speaks low, practical: *"Venga, the other shoulder, Dija. I'm not paid today, don't make me ask twice."* Khadija hears a con artist's wink: *"Not paid today. Write that."*

**The turn:** The case gets stronger, because the slow glove is a classic distraction. But for the first time, the person being watched has a voice and a name.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Eyes at foot and hand level.
- The sleeves silence.
- "Write that."
- The size game as cashier competence.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she notices the heavy woman's hair is like her own. Instead she notices nothing. Only the listener holds both henna lines.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - The slowness is care for a bad hip. Fatiha scrubs backs for a living in Tangier, hence "not paid today."
  - "Venga" is Tangier Spanish.
  - Dija's feet are Khadija's size.
- **Character believes:** The slow hand is a decoy.
- **Listener believes:** Mostly with Khadija. A careful listener feels a first itch at the mirrored henna and "thirty-nine."
- **Clue planted:**
  - The heavy woman's henna over grey.
  - The lower left shoulder.
  - Size 38 (links the thin woman to the door flats).
  - Size 39.
  - "Venga."
  - "Dija."
  - "Not paid today."

### Scene 2.2
**Where and when:** The warm room. Minutes 37 → 34.

**Who:** Khadija, Dija, Fatiha.

**What happens:**
- The glove passes a sore place. Dija, loud and warm, works the room like a comedian: *"Slowly, slowly. I'm a museum, not a carpet."* She starts laughing two words before the end of her own joke. Two other women laugh.
- Khadija laughs under her breath, the gold molar almost showing. Then she stops.
- She doubles down: *"A woman who jokes like that doesn't watch her purse. That's why they picked her."* She shifts to watch the purse, set on the bench at the thin woman's knee.

**The turn:** From contempt to an unwelcome kinship she won't name. She hardens the case to cover it.

**Tells and voice used:**
- The rare real laugh, cut off.
- Finishing the "victim's" story for her.
- Detective deadpan.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she warms to Dija, or recognises her own joke out loud. Instead she stops laughing and makes the victim's charm part of the crime.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** Dija jokes about her body out loud to a room. Khadija makes the same joke ("museum", 1.2) to nobody.
- **Character believes:** The victim is easy prey.
- **Listener believes:** Laughs, then notices the echo of "museum." The first doubt arrives that this is only a crime story.
- **Clue planted:** The museum echo. Dija's laugh before the punchline (a tell that returns as singing in Ch3).

### Scene 2.3
**Where and when:** The warm room. Minutes 34 → 29.

**Who:** Khadija, Souad.

**What happens:**
- Souad comes back with the same bucket and towel. *"Your scrub is paid, lalla."*
- Souad: *"The glove will catch your earrings. Take them off."* Khadija: *"No."* She turns the left one.
- Souad: *"Old gold. They don't make that yellow now."* Optional: one line of Souad's own life, e.g. tips for her daughter's October train to Rabat, counted in the headscarf knot.
- Khadija presses her last two-dirham coin into Souad's palm without looking at the hand. Her eyes stay on the purse. *"Later, aafak. Go and do someone who wants it."*
- Souad tucks the coin into the knot of her headscarf, above Khadija's eye line, and goes. She says nothing.

**The turn:** From refusing a gift to paying to refuse it. She has now spent the meter's only usable coin to keep a hand off her back.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Earring turned at a money moment.
- "No" as a full sentence.
- "Later, aafak."
- Eyes never on the hand she is paying.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she sees the missing fingertip as the coin passes, since she is a woman who watches hands. Instead her eyes are on the purse. The hand she pays is the one hand she doesn't watch. The recognition is held for 3.2.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - The hoops are the 1981 tin money.
  - Souad is "the finger" and already owes Khadija two dirhams.
  - Khadija has just paid her creditor-in-reverse.
- **Character believes:** She has tipped an attendant to leave her alone. The earrings are just her earrings.
- **Listener believes:** A comic bribe. The "No" feels like stubbornness.
- **Clue planted:**
  - "Take them off." / "No."
  - "Old gold."
  - The coin leaves the pocket (the jar and pocket will both be empty at the car).

### Scene 2.4
**Where and when:** The warm room, rising toward the hot room. Minutes 29 → 24.

**Who:** Khadija, Fatiha, Dija.

**What happens:**
- The glove moves lower, slower. Without knowing it, Khadija leans forward a fraction from the wall.
- A sensory flicker: wet tiles at fourteen, a fingertip moving slowly on her own back, letters she guessed. No word, no person, no meaning.
- The Detective cuts in with a number: *"Thirty minutes. Twenty-nine."*
- The towel on her shoulders has grown heavy with steam. She hitches it up.
- Dija stands and holds out her purse behind her without looking. The thin woman takes it like eggs. Dija: *"Careful with that."*
- They go toward the hot room, Dija slow on a stiff left hip. Khadija rises, one hand going to her own hip, and follows. *Twenty-four minutes.*

**The turn:** From the chase as sport to something older pulling at her body. The comedy has cracked, and she covers it with a number and keeps going.

**Tells and voice used:**
- The Girl on the Wet Tiles (leaning toward the slow glove).
- The Counter interrupting.
- The towel.
- Answering a feeling with a time.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is a full flashback to the friend, or a confession of memory. Instead the memory is three physical details and is cut off mid-sentence by a number.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** Fatiha once wrote on Khadija's back in this kind of place. The word stays in the iceberg forever.
- **Character believes:** Nothing she will admit. "Twenty-nine minutes."
- **Listener believes:** There is something under the case. The listener does not yet connect the fingertip memory to the thin woman.
- **Clue planted:**
  - The fingertip on a back.
  - The purse handed over without looking (trust).
  - Khadija's hand on her own hip as Dija moves on hers.

## Chapter 3: Two Women Answer
**Exit hook:** *Nine minutes.* Her eyes rise for the first time in the story, to a clock, while a shoulder turns.

### Scene 3.1
**Where and when:** The hot room. Minutes 24 → 21. Water hisses on the hot floor, and drops fall from the dome.

**Who:** Khadija, Fatiha, Dija.

**What happens:**
- The steam is so thick that the room is shapes at her eye height.
- Her towel soaks through and slides off her shoulders onto the tiles. She reaches for it once. It is heavy, wet, useless. She leaves it. Her back is bare to the room.
- Fatiha sets the purse on the marble. Dija, over her shoulder: *"Careful with that, my new hip's in there. It's paid for."* Khadija turns her earring at "paid."
- The Detective tries: *"They always say paid for."* It comes out weaker.
- She watches the thin woman's hand go under the heavy woman's elbow and lower her onto the hot marble.

**The turn:** From certain to unsure. The "loot" has just been described, innocently, and her back is uncovered for the first time.

**Tells and voice used:**
- The towel sliding.
- Earring turned at "paid."
- Detective deadpan losing strength.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she realises she was wrong and drops the case. Instead she keeps the case alive with a weaker line, because the case is what keeps her eyes low.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** The purse holds the clinic deposit for Dija's hip operation on 12 November.
- **Character believes:** She tells herself it is still a con. Part of her knows it isn't.
- **Listener believes:** The purse is innocent, probably. The fun is draining out. The listener begins to suspect the case is a cover for something.
- **Clue planted:**
  - "My new hip's in there. It's paid for."
  - The bare back.
  - The earring at "paid."

### Scene 3.2
**Where and when:** The hot room. About minute 20.

**Who:** Khadija, Souad.

**What happens:**
- Souad sits down beside her unhurried and lays her hand flat on her own knee, at Khadija's eye height. *"You've been watching her back for an hour. Do you want me to tell you what yours looks like?"*
- Khadija, at once: *"Twenty-six minutes. Not an hour."*
- Then she sees the hand: the missing fingertip, laid flat and not hidden. *The finger. Two dirhams, in the notebook.* The coin in the headscarf knot was owed to her already.
- She says nothing. No "Later" comes. Souad waits, then stands: *"Your scrub is paid. I'm not going anywhere."* She leaves.

**The turn:** From watcher to watched. The one person Khadija knew only by a hand has known her face all along, and has seen her back.

**Tells and voice used:**
- A number when cornered.
- Recognising someone by hands.
- The refrain with the "Later" missing.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she breaks, confesses, or lies down for the scrub. Instead she corrects the arithmetic and is silenced by a debt.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - Souad has known Khadija for years at the till and chose not to say.
  - The coin was hers already. She has paid every creditor except the one who counts.
- **Character believes:** She is caught, and does not know at what.
- **Listener believes:** Laughs at "Twenty-six minutes", then feels it turn. The question shifts from "is she a thief?" to "what is Khadija avoiding?"
- **Clue planted:** The finger (paying off the 1.1 notebook clue and the 2.3 coin). The offer of being seen, refused in silence.

### Scene 3.3
**Where and when:** The hot room. Minutes 19 → 11.

**Who:** Khadija, Fatiha, Dija.

**What happens:**
- The glove moves toward the bad hip. Dija begins to sing under her breath, bright, and brighter as the glove gets closer. *"Ah! There. That's where the bill is."*
- The slowness is now plainly waiting. Khadija's commentary has stopped.
- Around minute 12, the thin woman says *"Khadija,"* low, to the back she is working. Two women answer:
  - the heavy woman, a full second late, *"Na'am?"*, without turning;
  - Khadija, whose mouth is already open. She closes it on nothing.
- A name rises in Khadija's head, once: *Fatiha.* She pushes it back like a coin across a counter. Next.

**The turn:** From a case about strangers to a name she knows. The thief has a face she has refused for forty years.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Dija singing brighter as the pain gets worse.
- Khadija's mouth opening.
- "Next" as the cornered move.
- Pushing a coin across a counter as a gesture of mind.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that Fatiha turns and says "Khadija" *to her*, and there is a reunion. Instead the name is spoken to the back. It is never clear whom it was for. Dija is the one who answers.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - Fatiha normally says "Dija." Saying the full name is her first act against forty years of silence, aimed at both women.
  - Dija is a Khadija. She is the life Khadija refused, lived out loud.
- **Character believes:** She will not think it.
- **Listener believes:** The thin woman is someone from Khadija's past. The heavy woman is a kind of her, the life that went. Dread: will the face be seen?
- **Clue planted:**
  - "Dija" → "Khadija."
  - "Fatiha", once.
  - The song as pain.

### Scene 3.4
**Where and when:** The hot room. Minutes 11 → 9.

**Who:** Khadija, Fatiha, Dija.

**What happens:**
- The glove stops. The thin woman's hands come to rest.
- The heavy woman begins, slowly, on the stiff left hip, to turn. The lower left shoulder comes round first.
- Khadija's eyes rise above collarbone height for the first time in the story. They go past the turning shoulder, up the wall, to the clock above the door. *Nine minutes.*
- **Direct address (optional, at most once, recommended only if it sharpens the turn):** one short second-person line just before the final minute-mark. It should let the listener notice their own clock, with no explanation. If it reads as commentary, cut it.

**The turn:** From the back turning to her eyes lifting, to a number. The Counter wins.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Eyes rising for the first and only time, and only to a number.
- The Counter's time-as-answer.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that the face is revealed, whether a mirror, a reunion or a stranger. Instead the camera goes where Khadija goes: to the clock. The face never appears in the story.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:**
  - Nine minutes is not enough to climb back up on that hip ("Eleven minutes. Downhill").
  - The fine is flat. The meter is permission, not a reason.
- **Character believes:** She has to go, or she'll get a ticket.
- **Listener believes:** Relief. She is going, and we don't have to see the face either. That relief is the setup for recognition.
- **Clue planted:** None new. This scene cashes in the eye-line rule.

## Chapter 4: Same Price
**Exit hook:** None. The story ends on the last line: *Her thumb went to the left hoop, and stayed there.*

### Scene 4.1
**Where and when:** The door and changing area. Minutes 9 → 7.

**Who:** Khadija. Dija's voice through the wall.

**What happens:**
- She comes out of the steam fast, eyes back at feet. The wet slippers stand in their line.
- She passes the buckle flats: size 38, the number worn half off the insole, resoled three times, soft at the heels.
- She steps into her Velcro 39s, bending no more than her hip allows.
- She pulls the djellaba straight over wet skin. It sticks to her unscrubbed back. She ties the scarf over dripping hair.
- Through the wall, Dija's voice sings brightly. Khadija doesn't turn toward it.
- Under her breath: *"If you're going, go."*

**The turn:** From the edge of the truth to leaving it behind, by her own rule.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Eyes at feet.
- Her own phrase turned on herself.
- Back covered, wet.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she hesitates at the door and goes back. Instead there is no hesitation on the page, only shoes and a phrase. Three sizes stand in one doorway, and no sentence connects them.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** The 37 never walked. The 38 walked to Tangier and back. The 39 is what her feet became anyway.
- **Character believes:** She is beating the meter.
- **Listener believes:** Connects the 38 to the thin woman (2.1) and to "same buckle as mine" (1.2). The twin is understood.
- **Clue planted:** None. This scene pays off earlier plants.

### Scene 4.2
**Where and when:** The street door, then the alleys uphill toward Bab Boujloud. Minutes 7 → −6. A porter's *"Balak!"*, hooves on stone.

**Who:** Khadija; a porter and a mule loaded with gas bottles.

**What happens:**
- As she counts coins that are no longer in her pocket, a flash comes in numbers and objects only: *A biscuit tin. Three hundred and eighty. Half. One hundred and ninety. A jeweller's glass counter. A velvet tray.* It cuts off as the street door opens.
- She climbs the alleys she came down in 1.1, now uphill. Her left foot lands flat, the walk the listener has watched all story on Dija. Nobody comments on it.
- She counts steps against minutes out loud under her breath.
- *"Balak!"* A mule loaded with gas bottles comes through. She flattens her back against the stone wall to let it pass, her old position. She loses a minute and recounts.

**The turn:** From escape to losing the race. The listener still hopes she makes it, and in that hope is complicit.

**Tells and voice used:**
- The Counter out loud.
- Back against a wall.
- Numbers instead of memory.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is a memory of the bus, the awning or the friend as she walks. Instead the flash is only arithmetic and furniture. There is no "I", no name, no why.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** She and Fatiha saved 380 dirhams in a tin. Khadija kept it all and spent Fatiha's half, 190, on the hoops.
- **Character believes:** She is counting minutes. She does not look at the other count.
- **Listener believes:** Pieces together that the money in the flash connects to the earrings. A careful listener half-knows this here, and is sure at the last line.
- **Clue planted:** The flash numbers. The left foot landing flat, on Khadija for the first time.

### Scene 4.3
**Where and when:** Outside the gate, at the car. Minute −6.

**Who:** Khadija.

**What happens:**
- A ticket is under the stiff wiper. She lifts the wiper with two fingers, like something hot.
- She reads the ticket twice, the way she reads a price that has scanned wrong. It hasn't.
- The printed time is four minutes after the meter's last minute. She reads the sum aloud, flat, the way she reads a total at 3 a.m. It is the same sum as the sticker.
- She does not say the joke. The listener does the arithmetic.

**The turn:** From racing the clock to discovering the race bought nothing. Relief becomes a bill.

**Tells and voice used:**
- Reading a total like a cashier.
- The joke from 1.1 left unspoken.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she laughs bitterly or says *"Same price for one minute or one day."* Instead there is silence after the sum. The punchline belongs to the listener.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** She could have stayed until the back turned, seen the face, and paid exactly this.
- **Character believes:** She will not let herself think it.
- **Listener believes:** Recognition. The relief at "Nine minutes" was paid for, and bought nothing.
- **Clue planted:** None. This scene pays off the sticker.

### Scene 4.4
**Where and when:** At the car. Just after −6.

**Who:** Khadija.

**What happens:**
- She reaches through the window into the coffee jar for coins to feed the dead meter, to pay for time already lost. The jar is empty.
- Her hand goes to the djellaba pocket. Nothing is there.
- She folds the ticket in four, corner to corner, and presses the fold with her thumbnail. She puts it in the empty pocket.
- The same thumb rises out of habit to the left hoop. It doesn't turn it. *Her thumb went to the left hoop, and stayed there.*

**The turn:** From paying to the one account she cannot pay. The wrong account is perfectly closed, and the real one is worn.

**Tells and voice used:**
- The paying instinct.
- The fold and the thumbnail.
- The earring, now still.

**Predicted beat refused:** The obvious beat is that she takes off the earrings, cries, or goes back. Instead the thumb only rests.

**Ledger:**
- **Truth:** The hoops are the only stolen goods in the story. The coin went to someone who already owed it to her.
- **Character believes:** Unsaid.
- **Listener believes:** Every earring-turn at "thief", "owe" and "paid" was a confession made with a hand. "No" in 2.3 meant Fatiha's glove would have caught the thief.
- **Clue planted:** None. This is the final payoff.
