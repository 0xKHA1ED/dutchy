## Jungian reading of Khadija Trabelsi ("Dija")

### Reading
Dija is the one person in the story whose shadow is mostly *lived*. The self-assertion other women disowned is hers in daily use: she took the bus, divorced the man who sold her machine, ran a shift floor in her head. To Khadija Amrani she is the golden shadow in flesh: the girl who left, paid, and was not destroyed.

But she carries a second projection, from Fatiha. For twenty-two years Dija has agreed to be the living stand-in for Fatiha's lost Khadija, and she has done it by giving up her own name. That is generous, and it is also a quiet self-erasure she treats as nothing. She has never asked to see a photograph. She declines to look at the double in the drawer.

Her persona is the comedian. The compensation is exact: the more it hurts, the brighter the song. What she disowns is unperformed pain, need, and the grief of being a substitute. The Fès dispute matters because it is her first refusal to be a vessel for Fatiha's past. Fès is where Fatiha's lost Khadija lives. Tangier, and the sea, is Dija's own life.

### Evidence
- She left alone at eighteen and divorced in 1996 *(dossier: short dossier, family)*.
- She accepts "Dija" and never the full name, and has never asked for a photograph *(dossier: secret)*.
- She sings more cheerfully as the pain gets worse *(dossier: habit)*.
- She refuses the move to Fès and wants the sea *(dossier: present tension)*.
- She jokes about her body: "I'm a museum, not a carpet" *(dossier: how they speak)*.

### Under pressure
- **First move:** a joke, louder, laughed at before the end.
- **Escalation:** the song goes jaunty, while she holds the wall and Fatiha carries the purse.
- **Worst five minutes:** the joke fails mid-line, and she is simply an old woman in pain who will not dance in December. The comedian goes silent.
- **Shadow carrier:** Khadija Amrani carries Dija's unlived life as precisely as Dija carries hers. Amrani is the woman who stayed for the cousin. Dija does not know she is in the room, and the story should keep it that way until she turns.
- **Enantiodromia:** the woman who always left now refuses to leave. If Fatiha says "Khadija" in full, Dija may answer to it for the first time, and in doing so take back the name she lent away.

### Tells
- She hums a cheerful tune that gets faster and brighter as she lowers herself onto a bench.
- She starts laughing at her own joke two words before the punchline.
- She hands her purse to Fatiha without looking at it or at her.
- When the street opens toward the water, she stops walking and faces the sea.
- She turns slowly, a full second late, when anyone says "Khadija."

### Arc
Change would mean letting pain be unfunny in front of Fatiha, and keeping her own name and her sea against Fatiha's need. The cost is risking the friendship's first real rupture. Not changing looks like a move to Fès with a brave song, and a takchita sewn for a wedding she watches from a chair.

### What my lens can't explain
Her happiness seems genuine rather than defensive. Not every song covers a wound, and my lens is tempted to over-read her cheer.

### Confidence
Medium. The sheet is rich, but she has no voice of her own here, so her inner life is inferred from habit and silence.
