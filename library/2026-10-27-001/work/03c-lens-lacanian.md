## Lacanian reading of Khadija Amrani

### Reading

Khadija's fundamental fantasy is a scene she has staged every night for 44 years: **I am the one across the road who watches the back of a head and is not seen.** The till at 3 a.m. "is like being on a roof." She reads hands, not faces. She caught the pickpocket "without looking up." What she dreads is the reversal: "I'm afraid of being seen. From behind." Her back is where Fatiha once wrote the word *TANJA*, which Khadija misread twice. A message from the Other was inscribed on her body and she failed to read it. That is why the physio's photo had to be deleted. A gaze from behind would make her the one being read, and read accurately.

The structure is obsessional. She counts, folds, and keeps desire alive by keeping it impossible. The shoes were "too small from the start." Tangier stays perfect because it was never lived. What holds this together is a **debt**, not a lost love. She wears the 190 dirhams on her ears every day. She displaces the debt onto "the five dirhams" she "has never found out" about. She inverts it in the notebook of debts she never collects, where she plays the creditor who forgives. This debt is her *jouissance*, her painful satisfaction. Guilt is the only bond with Fatiha she has allowed herself.

The mechanism holds, but with a twist. The double here is not the unlived Tangier life. It is the mother who covered a face with folded card and "didn't answer." Khadija already suspects it: "You don't know which side the silence started from." She folds every scrap of paper in four. She is still covering the face.

### Under pressure

**First move:** ritual intensifies. She scans faster, washes clean glasses, counts the drawer three times. She finds an outside grievance to carry the anxiety. The coin meters at Bab Boujloud are perfect for this: a new law that charges you for standing still.

**Escalation:** she prepares without acting, which is the obsessional's substitute for the act. She makes the searches, checks the coin jar for two-dirham pieces, and drives to Bab Boujloud in her slippers. Then she feeds the meter and sits in the car. She becomes the person she "has no patience for": the one in the doorway, letting the cold in.

**Worst five minutes:** she is in the hammam, face to the wall, and the attendant's glove is on her back. A woman's voice laughs behind her and she cannot turn around. If she does face Fatiha, she will reach for accounts before words. She will count 190 dirhams out of the 200 in her purse, or unhook the earrings and press them into Fatiha's hand. She will turn love into the till: "Don't cry on the receipts, they're for the accounts."

### Tells

- She folds any paper in her hands into four, corner to corner, and presses the crease with her thumbnail while someone else is talking about the past.
- When money, theft or debt comes up, she turns her left earring between finger and thumb.
- She tells the listener how to record her ("Write that," "Write Tarek," "Put comedy") and then takes it back with "Never mind."
- When a man pushes his sleeves up past the elbow, she stops mid-sentence and watches his forearms, not his face.
- In any room she chooses the seat with her back to the wall, and she will step aside to let someone pass rather than have them walk behind her.

### Arc

Change is possible only if she lets herself be read: lets someone write on her back, and says the debt out loud, *"I spent your 190 dirhams on these."* To say it is to swap places in the fantasy, from the watcher to the one who is seen leaving, or seen staying. The cost is real. She loses the Egyptian-film promise that "the person on the platform always has one more chance," because a chance that is taken can fail. She loses "Not a thief." She may lose the earrings, the most intimate thing she owns.

Not changing looks like this. The voucher expires, gets folded in four, and goes into the shoebox with the ticket. She takes the part-time night cover and stays on the roof of her till. The coins stay in the jar. She lives out her retirement as her mother did, with one face behind a square of card, and she tells herself, as she told Rachid, "Easy to say to other people."

### What my lens can't explain

- **Why she stayed in 1981.** My reading accounts for how the not-going has been kept alive. Nothing in the material says what stopped her that night: fear, money, family, a sudden refusal. I won't invent a cause.
- **The sardines.** I can't make that refusal speak.

### Confidence

**High.** The material converges unusually tightly on the back, the gaze and the folded square. Her own speech keeps leaking the fantasy ("roof," "behind," "twenty minutes"), and that kind of convergence is what this lens is built to read.
