## Decision

Premise 12. It is the only one where the hammam's central act, one woman scrubbing another, is itself the "crime" scene. That makes the collision complete. The cashier's professional suspicion reads tenderness as a con: a slow scrub must be a distraction, and a purse carried for someone else must be a theft. The listener reads it the same way, because the commentary is funny and pickpocket pairs are real.

This gives the purest form of the **complicity** route to recognition. The listener judges the pair as Khadija does, and the reveal shows that the judgement was her defence against seeing a life of being touched and accompanied, the life she refused at nineteen. The listener made that judgement too, and laughed while doing it.

The deep question, *do we spend the rest of our lives making sure we never have to watch the life we refused being lived?*, is answered by the meter rather than by a sentence. It also satisfies "comic first, turning tragic" and "bitter" more naturally than 9, because the double carries something Khadija wanted, not just something she despises. It still surprises me knowing the premise, because I expect the twist to be guilt and it turns out to be love. I would remember the glove next week.

## Weakest point

Two things threaten it. First, the Tangier backstory could arrive as an exposition dump. Second, the double's resemblance to Khadija is pure coincidence, so who exactly the scrubbed woman is stays muddled.

The fix is to make the resemblance a *choice*: Fatiha later found the nearest copy. The backstory then reaches the listener through objects (shoes at the door, a purse never opened) before any memory is told. Khadija must also watch hands, never faces. That is her trade, and it lets her not recognise Fatiha for as long as she needs.

## Graft

From **7**: the shoes at the door, the twin of Khadija's unworn pair but **worn soft at the heels**. This gives the "case" a piece of physical evidence that the listener sees before they understand it. It puts the box-under-the-bed life into a single object, and it lets the re-read land: the last image of her unworn pair sends you back to the shoes at the door in chapter one.

## Final premise

### The Pair

Khadija is 63 and three weeks from her last night on the till in the ville nouvelle. She parks outside Bab Boujloud with one hour on the meter and walks to the hammam on women's day. Within minutes she has spotted a pickpocket team: a thin woman carries a heavy woman's purse from bench to bench and never looks inside, and Khadija has caught a hundred pairs like this at 3 a.m.

She follows them through the cold, warm and hot rooms. As always, she watches hands, not faces, and keeps up a merciless running commentary on their soap, their henna, and the way the thin one "works" the heavy one's back with the glove: slowly, far too slowly, surely a distraction. The heavy woman is only ever seen from behind. She has Khadija's sloped shoulders and Khadija's stiff left hip. At the door, among the wet slippers, stand cheap night-market shoes worn soft at the heels, the twin of a pair still in tissue under Khadija's bed, still a size too small.

Piece by piece, through objects before memories, the listener learns what Khadija has refused to know. At nineteen she and Fatiha bought those shoes in a hurry, in the dark, for the night bus to Tangier, and Khadija never got on. The thin woman with the glove is Fatiha, and the back she is scrubbing belongs to the nearest copy she could find.

In the hot room the glove stops and the back begins to turn. Khadija looks at the clock instead, leaves, and hurries through the alleys to beat the ticket. It is already on the windscreen, and she folds it in four, the way she folds receipts.

Surface question: Will Khadija catch the pickpocket pair in the act before her meter runs out? (suspense, comic turning to dread)
Deep question (never to be stated in the story): When we refuse a life, do we spend the rest of ours making sure we never have to see it lived?
Invention: A crime investigation in which the "con" is tenderness. The evidence of theft is one woman scrubbing another's back with a care the detective has never received, and the double is the stand-in who got on the bus.
