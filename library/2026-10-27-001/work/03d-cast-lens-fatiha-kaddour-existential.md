## Existential reading of Fatiha Kaddour

### Reading
Fatiha is the one who chose. She got on the bus, and she has lived inside that freedom for forty-four years without self-pity (dossier: night bus; work). Her defence is not specialness or a rescuer. It is work: a life organised as *the next task*, sleeves up, so the givens never get a quiet room. Pushing her sleeves up while naked in the steam is the gesture of a woman who meets the void by getting busy (dossier: habit).

Her bad faith is the tin. She was robbed, and she has renamed the robbery "a wedding present she gave without being asked" (dossier: history). This turns being done-to into doing, and it is half Camusian revolt, half lie. Under it sits the cough on the phone: the knowledge that she was not lost but declined (dossier: secret). Isolation is her pressing given. *(Inference)* She has lived twenty-eight years eating from one dish with another woman called Khadija. Before the protagonist ever found a double in Fatiha, Fatiha made one.

Now death, in the form of her knees, is sending her home (dossier: want). She brings no grand immortality project, just a ground-floor flat. Her return breaks the arrangement: the life Khadija refused comes back to live three doors away.

**Question under the story:** Can you go home to someone who chose not to come to the phone, without needing them to pick up now?

### Evidence
- Heard Khadija's cough behind "She's out"; told no one (dossier: secret).
- Recognised the walk in the cold room, said nothing, kept working (dossier: second secret).
- Savings tin reframed as a gift (dossier: history).
- Shoes worn only for the Fès journey (dossier: the shoes).
- This is the only back she has scrubbed for free (dossier: surprise).

### Under pressure
Accused of theft, she does not defend herself. She keeps the same slow stroke and gives an order: "The other shoulder." She escalates into generosity as aggression, pushing her own purse across the tiles: "Take it. Money is water." In her worst five minutes the forty-four-year silence cracks, not about the tin but about the cough: "I rang twice. I heard you." Then she is ashamed of having needed anything and goes back to work.

### Tells
- She pushes up sleeves she isn't wearing before she touches the glove.
- When accused, her scrubbing speed does not change by a second.
- She puts on the gold-buckle flats at the door of the Tangier flat and takes them off the moment she arrives in Fès.
- A Spanish word slips out when she is hurt, never when she is happy.

### Arc
Change is possible: she could let the loss be a loss and stop calling it a gift. That would cost her the dignity she built out of not being owed. Ending image: in the steam she plays the old back-writing game and traces something on Khadija's back with one fingertip. We never learn what. Khadija stiffens and does not turn around. Fatiha rinses the glove.

If she does not change, she signs for the flat, scrubs this back every week for free, and never says the word "cough" until she dies.

### What my lens can't explain
Whether she sought out this particular back knowing whose it was, or only recognised the walk afterwards. The sheet allows both readings.

### Confidence
Medium. The secrets are vivid and point clearly at isolation, but there is no interview, so every reading of her self-deception is inferred from the facts alone.
