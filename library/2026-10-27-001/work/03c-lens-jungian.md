## Jungian reading of Khadija Amrani

### Reading
Khadija's persona is the Vigilant Till: the woman who sees everything, counts twice and is never short. She is almost completely fused with it, and it is built directly over what it guards against. She took 190 dirhams that were not hers and has worn them in her ears every day since. Her working identity is a forty-year compensation, a monument to *not being a thief*, and she hung its certificate where a mirror would go.

Her shadow has two halves. The dark half is the theft. The gold half is departure: the sleeves-up, whole-armed commitment that Fatiha had and that Khadija left under a café awning. Both halves are projected outward. Hamid carries the gold. He is young and "dynamique", a man who moves things, and he calls her *Hajja*, a pilgrim, which names the journey she never made. People "who stand in the doorway" carry her paralysis. The pickpocket carried her theft, and Khadija caught her without looking up.

The double is Fatiha, and the library mechanism needs adjusting for her. This is not fascination with a stranger. It is thirty years of taking the long way round. The double has aged alongside her: bad knees for Fatiha, a bad hip for Khadija. And the double has returned to the one place Khadija abandoned, the hammam, where her back would be exposed. That back is the side she cannot see, and it is where Fatiha once wrote a city. Her inferior function, intuition, is already breaking through as a reading of omens: night-bus timetables, a film rule about last chances, a voucher that arrives like a summons.

### Evidence
- The 380 dirhams from the Nido tin became the earrings. She turns an earring while saying "That I'm a thief" (dossier: §11; interview: Q10, Q24).
- The Vigilance Award hangs at eye level opposite the sink. There is no full-length mirror at home (dossier: scene C, §6).
- Hamid is "unbearable". He moved her till "half a metre to the left", onto her bad hip. He calls her Hajja, and she says "I've never been to Mecca" (dossier: §1, §10; interview: Q1, Q8).
- "If you're going, go… Easy to say to other people" (interview: Q9). At 19 she stood across the road from the bus station, under the awning of a closed café (dossier: scene B).
- The sleeves gesture was Fatiha's, and it is now her only stated attraction (dossier: scene A, §5; interview: Q19).
- "I'm afraid of being seen. From behind." She last saw Fatiha from behind. She sits with her back to the wall, and she made the physiotherapist delete the photo of her back (interview: Q13; dossier: scene B, §6).
- She folds everything in four. Her mother covered the exiled sister's face with a *folded square of card* (dossier: §2, §6).
- She keeps a notebook of debts owed to her that she never collects, and mourns "five dirhams of mine" (dossier: §7; interview: Q17).
- Her searches run CTM night bus, hammam hours, the parking meter tariff, stretching shoes (dossier: §7).

### Under pressure
**First move:** she speeds up. She scans faster, folds harder, washes clean glasses, and turns the feeling into an impression of Hamid. The humour is a lid on the box.

**Escalation:** the sensation function tries to manage what intuition is announcing. She sorts coins from the jar, checks the meter tariff, wets socks to stretch the shoes. She drives to Bab Boujloud, feeds the meter, and sits in the Dacia in her slippers. She is back under the awning, watching the door from across the road. Mohamed Kaddour's stall is the messenger she is walking around.

**Worst five minutes:** the shadow surfaces as accusation. At the hammam, or at her till, she accuses someone of stealing: the attendant, a girl with a butterfly clip, Naïma. Her own 200-dirham purse "is short", and she counts it four times with wet hands. Or, in the hot room, someone pushes up sleeves they don't have and begins scrubbing her back, and she cannot tell whether it is the attendant or Fatiha.

**Who carries the shadow:** Fatiha carries all of it, the unlived life and the wronged party at once. Hamid is the decoy carrier, and her locker full of folded memos is a prosecution brief aimed at the wrong defendant.

**Enantiodromia:** the woman who catches thieves without looking up is caught the same way. Someone says, quietly, "the money is in the lady's ears." The other reversal is that the one who never goes buys seat 22 on a night bus at 63.

### Tells
- She folds any paper she is handed in four, corner to corner, and presses the crease flat with her thumbnail while someone is still talking to her.
- When money, theft or owing comes up, her fingers go to one gold earring and turn it.
- She answers an emotional question with a price, as in "Twelve dirhams fifty."
- In any room she moves so that her back is against a wall, and she shifts her chair if someone stands behind her.
- She stops mid-task to watch a stranger roll their sleeves past the elbow, and she finishes other people's sentences for them.

### Arc
**Possible change:** Jung would say she cannot become whole by leaving, only by being *seen from behind*. That means letting the back she has hidden since 1981 be touched, read and named: a scrub, a word traced on it, guessed right this time. Integration also means returning the theft. She could not do it as 190 dirhams. She would have to give up the earrings, which means admitting that her marriage was bought with Fatiha's escape money.

**Cost:** the incorruptible persona, her innocence in the "five dirhams" story, and the posture of the wronged one. She would also have to see, as she almost does at Q16, that she is the one who covered a face with card: "maybe somebody didn't answer."

**Not changing:** the voucher expires. The shoes go back in the tissue. She takes the part-time night shift so the till can keep guarding the box. Solitaire passes 10,000 games. She becomes her mother: a silent dinner, and a face on the dresser under a folded square.

### What my lens can't explain
- Why she did not board at 19. The lens reads the aftermath richly but has nothing for the moment itself under the awning. Fear, loyalty to her family and simple practicality all fit equally well.
- The sardines, and why she sleeps only on her right side. Not every habit is a symbol, and a Jungian reading should resist forcing them.

### Confidence
High, because the projections (Hamid's "Hajja" and the till moved left, the doorway, the pickpocket) map onto the dossier's hidden facts with unusual precision, though her motive in 1981 stays opaque.
