# Persona collapse

**In one line:** The mask we wear for the world fuses to our face, and then it cracks in public.

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** the persona (the role a person plays: the expert, the strong one, the happy couple, the good son) is threatened by an event it can't absorb.
- **Inner move:** the person has forgotten there is a self behind the role. Losing the role feels like dying.
- **Visible behaviour:** desperate maintenance of the image; then a crack (a breakdown, a slip, an outburst, an absurd lie to hold it together), often witnessed.
- **Cost:** the energy of permanent performance; and when it cracks, the terror of having no idea who is underneath.

## From outside

- A person who is always "on".
- Perfect social media, perfect house, perfect answers.
- Disproportionate fear of small embarrassments.
- Behaviour that changes completely when they think nobody is watching.

## Under pressure

The story removes the audience, or adds the wrong one. The persona works harder and harder; lies stack; the gap between role and reality grows until it is visible to everyone. Endings: the collapse is public and catastrophic; the collapse is private and liberating; or the persona survives by sacrificing someone else, and the listener sees the price.

## Lenses that see it best

Jungian (persona, the core), IFS (managers maintaining the image), existential (bad faith, freedom), Lacanian (the big Other for whom the performance is staged).

## Story shapes

Reversal; closed room; countdown; comic collapse endings.

## Collisions

The shadow, the protector that harms, excess importance, pendulums.

## Seeds

- A motivational speaker's microphone keeps working after he walks off stage.
- The happiest couple in the building are both, separately, looking for a new flat.
- A famous chef's restaurant is reviewed by someone who knew him at school, when he was called something else.

## Traps

- Making the persona simply fake and the "real self" simply good. The persona often contains real virtues.
- Humiliation as entertainment. The listener should wince with recognition, not laugh down at the person.
- An ending that announces "he finally knew who he was".
