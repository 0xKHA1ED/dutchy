# Pendulums

**In one line:** Groups, institutions and ideas take on a life of their own and feed on the energy and loyalty of the people who belong to them (Vadim Zeland's "pendulums").

As with excess importance, we use this as a dramatic model. It describes something everyone recognises: the company, cult, family role, fandom, political movement or online feud that consumes its members.

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** a person joins, or is born into, a structure that offers identity, belonging or purpose.
- **Inner move:** the structure hooks them through fear (of exclusion), guilt (of disloyalty), hope (of reward) or outrage (against the enemy). Their energy now flows into the structure's survival.
- **Visible behaviour:** adopting its language, defending it against all criticism, sacrificing personal interests, fighting its enemies, recruiting others.
- **Cost:** the person's own life and judgement are slowly replaced by the pendulum's needs. Pendulums also fight each other, and their members become soldiers in conflicts that are not theirs.

## From outside

- A person who speaks in the group's phrases.
- Escalating sacrifices: more hours, more money, more distance from outsiders.
- Strong emotion (outrage, devotion) on cue.
- Leaving is unthinkable, or punished.

## Under pressure

Pendulums escalate by demanding more, especially when threatened, and they swing: devotion can turn into equally intense opposition, which feeds a rival pendulum. The person frees themselves not by fighting the pendulum (fighting feeds it) but by losing interest, stepping aside or making it absurd. Wild Wild Country is a pendulum story from start to finish.

## Lenses that see it best

Transurfing (the core), existential (bad faith; freedom), defences and attachment (belonging needs), Jungian (persona built by the group).

## Story shapes

Escalation spiral; dread as the surface question; rise and fall; two windows (insider and outsider).

## Collisions

Scapegoat (pendulums need enemies), mimetic desire, persona collapse.

## Seeds

- A yoga studio becomes a family, then a business, then something else.
- A man's identity as "the reliable one" in his family slowly costs him his marriage.
- Two neighbours' feud over a hedge recruits the whole street and outlives both of them.

## Traps

- A cartoon cult or evil corporation. The best pendulums give real gifts (belonging, meaning, friendship) before they take.
- A lecture about conformity.
- An ending where the hero simply walks away free. Leaving costs something.
