# Projection

**In one line:** We hate, fear or obsess over in other people exactly what we refuse to see in ourselves.

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** someone shows a trait, desire or weakness that the person has buried in themselves.
- **Inner move:** the buried trait is too threatening to own, so the mind relocates it. It is *out there*, in that person, not in me.
- **Visible behaviour:** disproportionate irritation, fascination or moral outrage at the other person; collecting evidence against them; an inability to leave them alone.
- **Cost:** the person never deals with the trait in themselves, and they damage or lose a relationship over a fight that was never about the other person.

## From outside

- A reaction far bigger than the offence. Everyone else shrugs; this person cannot stop talking about it.
- They know an unusual amount about the person they claim to despise.
- The accusation is oddly precise ("people who run away from things", "people who think rules are for others"), and the precision is a self-portrait.
- They are doing, in small ways, exactly what they condemn, and cannot see it.

## Under pressure

Projection escalates by needing more and more proof that the problem is outside. The person watches, reports, confronts and recruits others. It breaks in one of three ways: the screen turns out to be innocent; the person is caught doing the very thing they condemned; or someone holds up a mirror and they finally see it. The richest stories let the listener project too, then turn it round.

## Lenses that see it best

Jungian (shadow and projection are Jung's core terms), defences and attachment (projection as a defence), IFS (a manager part that keeps an exile hidden by attacking it in others), core dramatic (the lie: "the problem is them").

## Story shapes

Investigator investigated; two windows; unreliable narrator; dramatic irony as the surface question (the listener sees it before the character does).

## Collisions

Scapegoat (a whole group projects onto one person), envy as contempt, return of the repressed, persona collapse.

## Seeds

- A man reports his neighbour for being nosy, and his evidence is three years of notes on her life.
- A strict driving examiner fails everyone who hesitates, and he once drove away from an accident.
- A food critic who destroys "lazy" restaurants eats the same frozen meal alone every night.

## Traps

- Making the projection obvious from the first page. If the listener spots it in paragraph one, there is no recognition, only a lesson.
- A character who explains it at the end ("I guess I hated him because…").
- A screen with no life of their own. The person being projected on must want something for themselves.
