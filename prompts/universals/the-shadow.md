# The shadow

**In one line:** The parts of ourselves we deny don't disappear; they grow stronger in the dark and act without our permission.

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** a situation that calls for the denied part: anger in a person who has made themselves gentle, selfishness in a person who lives for others, desire in a person who has made themselves pure.
- **Inner move:** the conscious self refuses the impulse, so the impulse goes underground, gathering force.
- **Visible behaviour:** sudden, out-of-character acts (cruelty, theft, an affair, a lie), usually followed by disbelief; or secret, contained outlets (a private list, a hidden habit, a second life).
- **Cost:** the person is split. The denied part eventually acts in the worst possible moment, and the good self cannot understand it.

## From outside

- A person who is *too* consistent: always kind, always calm, always generous.
- A secret drawer, notebook, browser tab or habit that contradicts the public self.
- Moments where the mask slips for a second (a cruel joke, a flash of rage) and is instantly covered.
- Strong reactions to fiction, news or gossip about people who do what they never would.

## Under pressure

The more energy goes into being good, the more the shadow accumulates. Jung's *enantiodromia* applies: anything pushed to an extreme turns into its opposite. The kindest nurse keeps a list; the most honest man embezzles once and enormously. The story can end in eruption (the shadow acts), integration (the person admits it and becomes more whole, and often kinder), or a mirror (they meet someone who lives their shadow openly).

## Lenses that see it best

Jungian (shadow, persona, enantiodromia), IFS (exiles and the firefighters that act for them), defences (reaction formation: being the opposite of what you fear you are), Lacanian (jouissance: the secret enjoyment in the forbidden).

## Story shapes

Reversal; rise and fall; the double; closed room (the shadow comes out when there is no audience).

## Collisions

Persona collapse, projection, the double, the protector that harms.

## Seeds

- The kindest nurse on the ward keeps a list of patients she wished would die.
- A pacifist philosophy teacher discovers he loves boxing.
- A woman famous for her tolerance cannot stand one colleague, and starts sabotaging her.

## Traps

- Making the shadow simply "evil". The shadow often contains things that are healthy but forbidden: anger, ambition, sexuality, play.
- Revealing it as a twist with no seeds earlier. The listener must have seen the too-perfect surface first.
- Psychologising: no character should ever say "my dark side".
