# Envy disguised as contempt

**In one line:** We mock, dismiss or morally condemn what we secretly long for.

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** someone has, or is, something the person wants and believes they can't have: freedom, talent, beauty, ease, faith, love, recklessness.
- **Inner move:** wanting it and not having it hurts, so the wanting is converted into contempt. The thing is foolish, vulgar, naive, immoral.
- **Visible behaviour:** sneering, debunking, moralising, keeping close tabs on the envied thing "to criticise it", spending surprising time near it.
- **Cost:** the person cuts themselves off from what they most want and calls it principle.

## From outside

- Strong opinions about something that supposedly doesn't interest them.
- Detailed knowledge of what they despise.
- Contempt that sounds like a love letter turned inside out.
- Small, secret tastes of the forbidden thing.

## Under pressure

The story pushes the person into contact with what they scorn, often with no escape. Contempt grows louder as attraction grows stronger. Endings: they finally take what they want, clumsily and wonderfully; they destroy it to keep from wanting it; or they're caught enjoying it.

## Lenses that see it best

Jungian (shadow, projection), defences (reaction formation), Lacanian (desire disguised as its opposite), core dramatic.

## Story shapes

Comic collapse; dramatic irony; closed room (forced proximity); two windows.

## Collisions

Projection, mimetic desire, the shadow, persona collapse.

## Seeds

- An atheist debunker travels the world visiting miracles, "to disprove them".
- A serious critic who hates musicals knows every word of one.
- A woman who mocks her sister's "boring" marriage spends every Sunday at their house.

## Traps

- Revealing the envy in the first scene.
- Making the scorned thing obviously good. It should have real flaws, which is what lets the contempt feel justified.
- A redemption speech.
