# Mimetic desire

**In one line:** We mostly want things because someone we admire, envy or compete with wants them (René Girard).

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** another person (the *model*) desires or possesses something.
- **Inner move:** the object borrows its value from the model's desire. The person believes the desire is their own and the object is intrinsically wonderful.
- **Visible behaviour:** imitation, rivalry, sudden urgent wanting, loss of interest as soon as the rival stops wanting it.
- **Cost:** the person chases things they never wanted, becomes locked in rivalry with the very person they admire, and is empty when they win.

## From outside

- They want what the other has: the same job, flat, partner, coat, way of laughing.
- Their desire appears or intensifies exactly when someone else shows interest.
- Rivals who become strangely similar over time.
- Victory followed by indifference, or by immediately wanting the next thing the model wants.

## Under pressure

Mimetic rivalry escalates because each rival's desire feeds the other's. The object becomes irrelevant; the rivalry becomes the point. Girard argued that it ends either in violence (often redirected onto a scapegoat) or in renunciation. In smaller stories it ends with winning the object and discovering it is worthless, or with the realisation that the model was never happy with it either.

## Lenses that see it best

Lacanian ("desire is the desire of the Other"), core dramatic (want vs need), Transurfing (importance created by comparison), existential (bad faith: living by someone else's project).

## Story shapes

Rise and fall; two windows; escalation spiral; desire as the surface question ("will she get it, and should she?").

## Collisions

Scapegoat, objet petit a, envy as contempt, self-sabotage.

## Seeds

- Two sisters fight over an ugly chair neither cared about until the other wanted it.
- A woman spends twenty years wanting her sister's house, and gets it.
- Two rival bakers in one street copy each other until both shops sell the same strange cake nobody likes.

## Traps

- Making the copying conscious. The character always believes the desire is their own.
- A model who is a cartoon. The person being imitated has their own unhappiness, and revealing it is often the reframe.
- Moralising at the end about "being yourself".
