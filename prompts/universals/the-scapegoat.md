# The scapegoat

**In one line:** A group in conflict restores its peace by uniting against one victim, and believes the victim was really to blame (René Girard).

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** tension inside a group (rivalry, fear, loss, a crisis nobody can solve).
- **Inner move:** the tension is unbearable and can't be blamed on any insider without splitting the group, so it is discharged onto someone marginal: new, different, weak or simply nearby.
- **Visible behaviour:** gossip that hardens into certainty, exclusion, rituals of blame, and then a strange peace and closeness among the accusers.
- **Cost:** an innocent (or not very guilty) person pays for everyone's problem, and the group learns nothing, so it will need another victim.

## From outside

- Sudden consensus about one person, built from small, ambiguous details.
- A group that becomes warmer and more united the worse it treats someone.
- People who were rivals yesterday agreeing enthusiastically.
- The victim's ordinary behaviour reread as proof.

## Under pressure

The mechanism escalates until the victim is expelled, broken or destroyed, and then the peace makes the group feel righteous. It can be broken by one person refusing to join (often at great cost), by the victim's innocence becoming undeniable, or by the group turning on a second victim and someone noticing the pattern. The story is strongest when the listener joins in at first.

## Lenses that see it best

Transurfing (the pendulum: the group as an entity feeding on fear and unity), Jungian (collective shadow projection), defences (displacement), existential (bad faith; the courage to stand alone).

## Story shapes

Dread as the surface question; escalation spiral; two windows (the group and the victim); the investigator investigated.

## Collisions

Mimetic desire (rivalries feed the crisis), projection, pendulums.

## Seeds

- A village grows happier every week after it decides the new baker is a witch.
- An office finds unity in blaming the intern for a leak that someone senior caused.
- A family reunion goes smoothly for the first time in years because everyone agrees the absent cousin ruined everything.

## Traps

- Making the group villains and the victim a saint. Each accuser should have a sympathetic reason.
- Explaining the mechanism. The listener should feel the warmth of the group and only later feel sick about it.
- An easy ending where everyone learns a lesson.
