# Excess importance

**In one line:** The more desperately something matters, the more reality seems to push back against it (Vadim Zeland's "excess potential").

We use Zeland's *Reality Transurfing* as a dramatic model, not a claim about how the universe works. As plot physics it is excellent: it produces escalation, comedy and a natural resolution in letting go.

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** a goal, person, judgement or self-image becomes over-valued. Zeland distinguishes *inner importance* (I must be good enough, this must go well) from *outer importance* (this thing is enormous, everything depends on it).
- **Inner move:** the over-valuation creates tension, an "excess potential", like a balloon over-inflated.
- **Visible behaviour:** over-preparation, rigidity, desperation, control, fear of the smallest mistake.
- **Cost:** "balancing forces" arrive: things go wrong in direct proportion to how much they matter, often absurdly. Tension makes people clumsy, needy and repellent, which creates the very failure they fear.

## From outside

- Rehearsing obsessively; checking and re-checking.
- An unusual number of small disasters right before the big moment.
- A person who becomes worse at something the more it matters (the speech, the date, the interview).
- Neediness that makes others pull away.

## Under pressure

The comic version escalates absurdly: every attempt to secure the outcome causes a new problem. The tragic version escalates into obsession. Resolution comes from letting go: the person stops caring, or is forced to, and only then does the thing come easily, or it stops mattering. Gérard's story lives here: correctness over-valued, and the balancing force is the faultless candidate.

## Lenses that see it best

Transurfing (the core), defences (control, perfectionism), IFS (a manager part working overtime), Lacanian (the object moves away as it is chased).

## Story shapes

Escalation spiral; countdown; suspense as the surface question; comic collapse endings.

## Collisions

Self-sabotage, objet petit a, persona collapse, denial of death.

## Seeds

- A job interview goes wrong in exact proportion to how badly he needs the job.
- A wedding speech rehearsed for three months collapses when the glass breaks, and the improvised line is what everyone remembers.
- A woman determined to have the perfect last Christmas with her dying mother ruins every moment by controlling it.

## Traps

- Naming the concept or presenting it as mystical truth.
- Coincidental disasters with no link to the character's tension. The best balancing forces are caused by the tension itself (the shaking hand breaks the glass).
- A preachy "just relax" ending. Letting go should be forced, accidental or costly.
