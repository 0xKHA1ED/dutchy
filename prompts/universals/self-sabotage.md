# Self-sabotage

**In one line:** Some part of us fears getting what we want more than it fears not getting it.

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** success, love or freedom comes within reach.
- **Inner move:** getting it would threaten something deeper: loyalty to a family that never succeeded, an identity built on struggle, the safety of not being tested, the fear of being found out, or the fear of having nothing left to want.
- **Visible behaviour:** lateness, forgetting, picking a fight, choosing the wrong person, the "accident" on the eve of the big day, then a convincing explanation.
- **Cost:** a repeating pattern the person experiences as bad luck, and the slow loss of the people who believed in them.

## From outside

- The same kind of failure, at the same kind of moment, again and again.
- Very good reasons, every time.
- A mood change (irritability, illness, restlessness) as the good thing approaches.
- Relief, under the disappointment, when it fails.

## Under pressure

The story offers the thing again, bigger, maybe for the last time. The sabotage becomes more elaborate and more visible. Endings: the person sees the pattern in the moment and does not sabotage (often with a small, concrete act); the person sabotages and finally admits it; or someone else names the pattern and the person must decide whether to hear it.

## Lenses that see it best

IFS (a protector preventing a risky success), Lacanian (jouissance in failure; the object kept at a distance), core dramatic (the lie: "I don't deserve it", "success will cost me my people"), defences and attachment.

## Story shapes

The loop; suspense as the surface question; the choice as ending; countdown.

## Collisions

Objet petit a, excess importance, the protector that harms, mimetic desire.

## Seeds

- A painter burns every canvas the night before each show, and calls it bad luck.
- A woman who always wanted to leave her small town keeps missing the last bus.
- A man finally offered the promotion asks for a week to think, and spends it making himself unpromotable.

## Traps

- Making it fully conscious. The person believes their reasons.
- A happy ending that is too easy. The pattern is old and strong.
- Explaining the cause in a flashback.
