# The protector that harms

**In one line:** A part of us that once kept us safe keeps doing its job long after the danger is gone, and ruins our life doing it (Internal Family Systems).

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** anything that resembles the original danger: criticism, closeness, chaos, being seen, being left.
- **Inner move:** a protective part takes over. In IFS terms, *managers* act in advance to prevent pain (perfectionism, control, people-pleasing, planning), and *firefighters* act after pain breaks through (binging, rage, numbing, fleeing, an affair).
- **Visible behaviour:** the same strategy, applied rigidly, everywhere, including where it does harm.
- **Cost:** the strategy that saved a child now isolates an adult. The protected wound (the *exile*) is never healed, because the protector never lets anyone near it.

## From outside

- A person who is excellent at one thing (being useful, being funny, being in control, being fine) and cannot stop being it.
- The same response to very different situations.
- Sudden switches: calm competence, then an outburst or a disappearance.
- People around them feel managed rather than met.

## Under pressure

The protector works harder as the threat grows. Its behaviour escalates until it causes the very thing it was meant to prevent: the perfectionist's team turns against her, the people-pleaser is abandoned for being false, the joker is left alone at the funeral. The resolution is a moment when the protector steps aside, by exhaustion, accident or someone's patience, and the exile is seen.

## Lenses that see it best

IFS (the core), defences and attachment, core dramatic (the lie the protector is built on), Jungian (persona).

## Story shapes

Escalation spiral; closed room; the choice as ending; dramatic irony (the listener sees the protector before the character does).

## Collisions

Persona collapse, self-sabotage, return of the repressed, the shadow.

## Seeds

- Her inner manager makes her perfect at work, and that perfection is why her team quietly hates her.
- A man who has made a career of being funny is asked to give the eulogy for his father.
- A woman who always leaves first starts to fall for someone who never asks her to stay.

## Traps

- Naming parts in the story ("her inner critic said…"). Parts are shown as actions and voices, never labelled.
- Making the protector a villain. It is loyal and frightened, and the story should respect it.
- Therapy-session endings.
