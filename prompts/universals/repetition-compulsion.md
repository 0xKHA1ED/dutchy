# Repetition compulsion

**In one line:** We keep recreating the situation that hurt us, as if this time we could make it end differently (Freud).

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** a new situation that rhymes with an old wound: a partner who resembles a parent, a boss who resembles a bully, a loss that resembles a first loss.
- **Inner move:** an unfinished scene from the past wants completion. The person is unconsciously drawn to the same kind of person or situation, hoping to win this time.
- **Visible behaviour:** choosing the same kind of partner, job or friend; provoking the same kind of ending; saying "this time is different".
- **Cost:** the same pain, again and again, with growing exhaustion, and the feeling that life is a loop.

## From outside

- Friends see the pattern long before the person does.
- Strong attraction to people or situations that are obviously bad for them.
- Familiar arguments with new people.
- Endings that look like copies of each other.

## Under pressure

The story usually runs the loop again, close enough that the listener sees the rhyme. Pressure comes from the one variation in this repetition. Endings: the person repeats and finally sees it; the person breaks the loop with one small different act; or the loop completes and the old scene is finally mourned.

## Lenses that see it best

Defences and attachment (attachment patterns), Lacanian (the symptom repeats), IFS (an exile trying to be rescued), Jungian (complexes).

## Story shapes

The loop (natural fit, and excellent for B1 vocabulary recycling); two windows across time; kishōtenketsu; the choice as ending.

## Collisions

Return of the repressed, self-sabotage, the protector that harms.

## Seeds

- A woman who was always the one left behind takes a job seeing people off at an airport.
- A man keeps hiring assistants who remind him of his ex-wife and firing them on the same day of the month.
- Three generations of one family have the same argument at the same restaurant table.

## Traps

- Making the pattern obvious too early.
- Explaining the original wound in detail.
- A tidy cure.
