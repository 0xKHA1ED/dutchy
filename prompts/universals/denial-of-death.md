# Denial of death

**In one line:** We build heroic projects, routines and identities so we never have to feel that we will die (Ernest Becker; Irvin Yalom).

## How it runs

- **Trigger:** a reminder of mortality: a birthday, a diagnosis, a funeral, retirement, a child leaving, a near-miss.
- **Inner move:** the terror is converted into a project that promises a kind of immortality: a legacy, a record, a perfect body, a famous name, a family line, a cause, a collection, a technology.
- **Visible behaviour:** frantic purpose, a refusal to slow down, an obsession with being remembered or with staying young; or rigid routines that make time feel controlled.
- **Cost:** life is spent defending against death instead of living. The project often harms the people it was meant to impress.

## From outside

- Plans that extend beyond the person's lifespan.
- Statistics, records, achievements counted and recounted.
- Refusal to discuss illness, age or wills.
- Panic, anger or bargaining around endings of any kind: retirement, the last day of school, closing a shop.

## Under pressure

The story brings death or an ending closer, often with a countdown. The project intensifies, then reveals its emptiness. Endings: the person chooses a small, present act over the immortal project; the project succeeds and it doesn't help; or the person faces the fear and becomes, briefly, free. Gérard's failure statistics are his immortality project.

## Lenses that see it best

Existential (the core), Transurfing (importance attached to legacy), Jungian (the second half of life, individuation), defences (denial, sublimation).

## Story shapes

Countdown; kishōtenketsu; rise and fall; the choice as ending. Speculative stories with one changed rule (uploading minds, buying time) are natural fits.

## Collisions

Excess importance, objet petit a, persona collapse.

## Seeds

- A billionaire funds a machine to upload his mind and keeps delaying his own scan.
- A shopkeeper refuses to close the shop on its last day, and stays open all night.
- A woman plans her own funeral in such detail that she forgets to go to her granddaughter's wedding.

## Traps

- Long reflections on mortality. The fear shows in behaviour, not thoughts.
- Using a terminal illness as an easy emotional lever.
- Sentimental endings about "living every day to the full".
