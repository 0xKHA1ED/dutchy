# Kishōtenketsu

**How it works:** A four-part structure from Chinese and Japanese poetry and storytelling, with no central conflict. *Ki* introduces; *shō* develops; *ten* brings a twist of perspective, often something seemingly unrelated; *ketsu* reconciles the two and reveals a connection the listener didn't see coming. The surprise is a juxtaposition, not a battle.

**Seen in:** many Japanese short stories and four-panel comics; Studio Ghibli films (*My Neighbor Totoro*); Hirokazu Kore-eda's films.

**Suits:** existential questions, quiet recognition, denial of death, the fetish as symbol, rituals.

Use it rarely (see `config/preferences.yaml`), so it stays special.

## Beats

1. **Ki (introduction):** a person, a place, a routine, observed closely.
2. **Shō (development):** the routine deepens; small details accumulate.
3. **Ten (twist):** something apparently unrelated: another person, another place, another time, a change of scale.
4. **Ketsu (reconciliation):** the two are brought together, and the connection changes how the whole is seen.

## What it does to the listener

Calm attention, then a quiet shock of meaning. These stories feel like a held breath. Recognition comes from the connection, not from a defeat or a victory.

## Notes for simple language

Very suitable for B1: concrete observation, no complex plot logic. The *ten* must be clearly marked so the listener follows the jump.

## Common mistakes

- Sneaking a conflict back in because it feels safer.
- A *ten* that is random rather than surprising-then-meaningful.
- Explaining the connection in the *ketsu*. Place the two side by side and let the listener connect them.
