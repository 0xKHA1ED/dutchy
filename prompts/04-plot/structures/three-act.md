# Three acts

**How it works:** The classic shape. Setup, confrontation, resolution: a person with a want, an inciting incident, rising obstacles, a crisis that forces a choice, a climax and a new equilibrium. It is a good structure and it is the one every model reaches for by default, which is why it is only one option here and never the default.

**Seen in:** most films and novels.

**Suits:** any mechanism, which is its strength and its danger.

## Beats

1. **Setup:** the person, their want, their lie, the ordinary world.
2. **Inciting incident:** the event that makes the want urgent.
3. **Rising action:** attempts, obstacles, escalation; the midpoint shifts the stakes.
4. **Crisis:** the dilemma between want and need.
5. **Climax and resolution:** the choice and its consequence.

## When to choose it

When another structure would be a gimmick for this story, and the strength of the story lies in its characters and its turn. Combine it with a stronger device (a refrain, a countdown, a ledger with dramatic irony) so it doesn't feel generic.

## Common mistakes

- Predictable beats placed at predictable moments.
- A midpoint that is just more of the same.
- A climax that is an action scene instead of a choice.
