# Closed room

**How it works:** Few people, a sealed or semi-sealed space, a test, a game or a negotiation. Who is testing whom flips, sometimes more than once. The space itself presses on everyone: there is no exit, no audience, no distraction.

**Seen in:** *Ex Machina*; *12 Angry Men*; *Carnage*; *Sleuth*; *Rope*.

**Suits:** projection, mimetic desire, persona collapse, the shadow, power struggles of any kind.

## Beats

1. **Arrival** in the space; the rules of the game are set (explicitly or not).
2. **First positions:** who seems to hold power, who seems to be tested.
3. **First crack:** a small detail suggests the positions are not what they seem.
4. **The flip:** the tested becomes the tester, or a third party is revealed as the real player.
5. **The last move:** someone leaves the room changed, or doesn't leave at all.

## What it does to the listener

Claustrophobic attention. The listener reads every word and gesture for hidden meaning, which makes this structure perfect for subtext and for dramatic irony.

## Notes for simple language

Dialogue carries most of it, which suits B1. Keep the number of speakers to two or three and tag them clearly for audio. One object in the room (a glass of water, a contract, a phone face down) can carry the power struggle.

## Common mistakes

- Characters explaining their strategies.
- A flip that comes from nowhere. The first crack must be visible on first listen.
- A room that isn't really closed. If anyone could simply leave, give them a reason they don't.
