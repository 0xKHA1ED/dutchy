# Rise and fall

**How it works:** The character gets exactly what they wanted, and getting it destroys them, or destroys the thing that mattered more. The rise is real and exciting; the fall comes from the same qualities that powered the rise.

**Seen in:** *Breaking Bad*; *Macbeth*; *The Great Gatsby*; *Citizen Kane*; *Whiplash*.

**Suits:** objet petit a, mimetic desire, denial of death, the shadow, pendulums.

## Beats

1. **The want:** clear, concrete, and something the listener can root for.
2. **The first win:** it works; it feels wonderful.
3. **More:** the character pushes further; the costs start, small and ignored.
4. **The summit:** they have it. A moment of triumph with something wrong in it.
5. **The fall:** the thing turns out to be empty, poisoned, or bought with what mattered more.

## What it does to the listener

Complicity in the wanting, then the recognition of having wanted the wrong thing. In a short story, a miniature version works well: one wish, granted.

## Notes for simple language

Keep the want concrete and the costs physical (a missed call, an empty chair). The summit and the fall can be very close together in a short story.

## Common mistakes

- Moralising. The fall should feel inevitable, not deserved.
- A want the listener never shared.
- Too long a rise; in a short story, get to the summit fast.
