# Reversal

**How it works:** The story builds a clear picture, and the final scene flips its meaning. Everything before is reread in a new light. The best reversals are both shocking and fair: every clue was there.

**Seen in:** O. Henry; *The Twilight Zone*; Roald Dahl's *Tales of the Unexpected*; *The Sixth Sense*.

**Suits:** persona collapse, the double, projection, envy as contempt.

## Beats

1. **A clear situation** that invites a confident reading.
2. **Development** that seems to confirm the reading, while planting two or three clues that fit the other reading too.
3. **A moment of doubt**, quickly smoothed over (this is the fairest clue).
4. **The turn:** a single fact, image or line that flips the meaning.
5. **Afterglow:** at most a few sentences. Let the listener do the rereading.

## What it does to the listener

A jolt, then the pleasure of replaying the story in the head. If the reversal also flips the listener's judgement of a character, it becomes recognition, not just surprise.

## Notes for simple language

The turn should be one simple, concrete sentence. B1 listeners need the clues to be audible: a repeated detail, an object, a phrase.

## Common mistakes

- Banned twists (it was a dream; the narrator was dead).
- A reversal with no meaning beyond surprise.
- An explanation after the turn.
