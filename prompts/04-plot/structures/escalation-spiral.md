# Escalation spiral

**How it works:** Every solution the character tries makes things worse, by their own logic. Each step is caused by the previous one and costs more. The spiral ends when the mechanism runs out of road: collapse, a forced choice, or an absurd summit.

**Seen in:** Wild Wild Country; most South Park episodes; *Fargo*; *Uncut Gems*.

**Suits:** excess importance, pendulums, self-sabotage, the scapegoat, the protector that harms.

## Beats

1. **A small problem** that the character's mechanism makes them handle in their own way.
2. **The fix** works, briefly, and creates a new problem.
3. **The bigger fix**: more of the same strategy, because it's the only one they have. Others are drawn in.
4. **The point of no return**: the strategy now requires something the character would never have agreed to at the start.
5. **The summit or collapse**: the mechanism meets its limit. The ending can be the choice to stop, the crash, or a comic victory that is a defeat.

## What it does to the listener

Rising disbelief and pleasure: *surely they won't…* and then they do. The comic version makes the listener laugh at logic followed too far; the tragic version makes them watch a person walk into a wall they can see.

## Notes for simple language

The spiral is easy to follow in short sentences, because each step is a clear action. Repeat a phrase or ritual at each step ("Il a encore une solution.") so the listener feels the pattern.

## Common mistakes

- Steps caused by luck or outside villains instead of the character's own strategy.
- Escalation that is only louder, not deeper. Each step should cost something more personal.
- No breath. Put one quiet moment before the summit.
