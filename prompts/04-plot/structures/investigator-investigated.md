# The investigator investigated

**How it works:** Someone hunts for a culprit, a cause or a truth, and the trail leads back to themselves. The investigation is the surface plot; the self-discovery is the real one.

**Seen in:** *Oedipus Rex*; *Angel Heart*; *Memento*; *Shutter Island*.

**Suits:** projection, the scapegoat, return of the repressed, the shadow.

## Beats

1. **The case:** something is wrong, and the protagonist sets out to find who or why.
2. **The suspect:** a person who seems obviously guilty, often the protagonist's screen.
3. **Evidence that doesn't fit:** small inconsistencies, each explained away.
4. **The trail turns:** a clue points home. The protagonist resists.
5. **Recognition:** the protagonist (or only the listener) sees who was responsible, and what the investigation was really for.

## What it does to the listener

Mystery pull from the start, and a deep recognition at the end, especially if the listener suspected the same wrong person for the same reasons.

## Notes for simple language

Investigation scenes are clear and concrete (questions, places, objects), which suits B1. Keep clues physical and few. The turn should be one simple fact.

## Common mistakes

- Amnesia or dissociation used to make the twist possible. Avoid; the protagonist should have avoided knowing, not been unable to know.
- Too many clues. Two or three, well placed, are enough.
- The suspect as a cardboard figure. They have their own story.
