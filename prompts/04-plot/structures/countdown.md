# Countdown

**How it works:** A fixed deadline (the last week before retirement, the last night before the wedding, one hour before the bus leaves) and the character spends the remaining time revealing who they are. The clock creates pressure without needing a villain.

**Seen in:** *High Noon*; *Cléo from 5 to 7*; *Before Sunrise*; *Locke*.

**Suits:** denial of death, excess importance, self-sabotage, persona collapse.

## Beats

1. **The clock is set:** the deadline and what it means.
2. **Business as usual:** the character tries to carry on, and the clock makes every routine strange.
3. **The intrusion:** something that can't wait until after the deadline.
4. **The squeeze:** time runs short; the character must choose what to do with what is left.
5. **Zero:** the deadline arrives. What they did with the time is the answer.

## What it does to the listener

Steady, rising tension, and the bittersweet awareness of an ending, which is the listener's own awareness of time.

## Notes for simple language

Time markers make the structure easy to follow in audio: days of the week, hours, *plus que trois jours*. A sound or object can become the clock (Gérard's indicator).

## Common mistakes

- A deadline that doesn't matter emotionally.
- Wasting the countdown on logistics instead of character.
- A last-second rescue.
