# Unreliable narrator

**How it works:** The teller's mechanism distorts what we hear. The listener gradually sees more than the narrator does, through cracks: contradictions, other characters' reactions, details the narrator reports without understanding. The ending either confirms what the listener suspected or reveals that the listener, too, was inside the distortion.

**Seen in:** *Mr Robot*; *The Remains of the Day*; *Fight Club*; *Gone Girl*; Poe's *The Tell-Tale Heart*.

**Suits:** projection, the shadow, return of the repressed, envy as contempt, denial of death.

## Beats

1. **A confident voice** with a clear view of the world and of the other people in it.
2. **First crack:** another character reacts in a way the narrator doesn't explain, or the narrator contradicts themselves.
3. **Growing gap:** the listener now reads the narrator, not just the story.
4. **Pressure:** events force the narrator closer to the thing they can't see.
5. **Revelation:** the narrator sees it, or fails to see it and the listener sees everything. Either can work.

## What it does to the listener

Two pleasures at once: the story, and the detective work on the teller. The strongest version makes the listener complicit, believing the narrator for long enough that the reveal implicates them.

## Notes for simple language

First person and the present or passé composé work well. The distortion should live in what the narrator notices and repeats, not in complex syntax. A refrain ("Je ne suis pas jaloux.") can prove its own opposite.

## Common mistakes

- A narrator who is simply lying, with a twist that only reveals the lie. The interesting narrator believes themselves.
- Cracks so subtle that no one notices them, or so loud that there is no mystery.
- A final explanation scene.
