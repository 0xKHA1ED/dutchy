# The quality bar

This is the single definition of a good story in this project. Every judge, every critic and the final scorer measure against it, so they all mean the same thing by "good".

## What a story must do

A story works when the listener **cannot stop** and, at the end, **recognises something in themselves they had never put into words**. Everything else (humour, twists, ideas, atmosphere) serves one of those two effects.

- **Pull** is forward momentum: curiosity, suspense, surprise, the need to know what happens next.
- **Recognition** is the moment a story turns back on the listener. They see a mechanism working in a character, and then catch the same mechanism in themselves.
- **Invention** is meeting something you have never met before: a situation, a rule, a job, an image, a way of telling.

Pull without Recognition is a page-turner forgotten in an hour. Recognition without Pull is a beautiful story nobody finishes. Invention keeps both from feeling second-hand.

These stories are for adults. They can be dark, funny, strange, sexual by implication, morally uncomfortable. The aim is that someone finishes one and thinks, *that touched something*.

## Where Recognition comes from

The strongest recognition happens when the listener **performs the mechanism during the story without noticing, and then the story reveals it**. Four ways to cause it:

1. **Complicity.** The listener is led to judge a character the way the protagonist does. The reveal shows that judgement was a projection, and the listener made it too.
2. **The re-read.** The ending gives an early detail its true meaning, and the listener replays the story in their head. A good test: does the last line send you back to the first?
3. **The unspoken want.** A character never says what they really want, but the listener slowly knows. When the character gets close and flinches, the listener feels the flinch.
4. **Small and specific.** Recognition comes from precise, slightly embarrassing details (checking whether a message was read, rehearsing an argument in the shower), not from statements about the human condition.

## Where Pull and surprise come from

- **Two questions.** A surface question the listener needs answered (will he get caught?) and a deep, never-spoken question (can a person be forgiven?). Answering the surface question answers the deep one.
- **Escalation by honest logic.** Each step is caused by who the character is, never by luck. Surprising when it arrives, inevitable in hindsight.
- **Predict, then refuse.** The obvious next beat is known and deliberately not taken, while the chosen beat still follows from the characters.
- **Contrast.** Dread lands after a laugh, tenderness after cruelty.

## The pretentiousness trap

Pretentiousness is depth that is **announced** instead of **enacted**. Ideas live in objects, choices and consequences, never in what anyone says about them. Symptoms: characters explaining the theme, philosophy in dialogue, symbols that announce themselves, ambiguity used as a cop-out, misery with no relief, weirdness that obeys no rule, psychological jargon. A story with real depth usually looks simple on the surface.

## The rubric

Score each criterion from 1 to 5 and give a written reason that quotes the text. **Recognition and Surprise are gates**: a story scoring below 4 on either is not finished, whatever its other scores.

| Criterion | Serves | A 5 looks like | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Pull | The first three sentences create a question the listener needs answered | |
| Momentum | Pull | Every scene changes something; the main question is fed throughout; no stretch where attention can drift | |
| Surprise | Pull | The turns were not predicted, and on reflection they feel inevitable; twists rest on clues already in the story | Yes |
| Psychological truth | Recognition | Behaviour follows a real mechanism; characters contradict themselves the way real people do; nothing is explained that could be shown | |
| Recognition | Recognition | There is a moment where the listener's own judgement or habit is turned back on them; the deep question is answered without being stated | Yes |
| Invention | Invention | At least one element the listener has never met before | |
| Lightness | All three | No lectures, no jargon, humour or relief where it fits, an ending enacted rather than explained | |
| Ear | Pull | Works read aloud: clear speakers, good rhythm, a hook at every chapter break | |

## Calibration

Be harsh. The scale is calibrated against what a capable language model writes when simply asked for "a good short story":

- **1** — Broken: confusing, incoherent, or no story at all.
- **2** — Generic: stock situation, stock people, stock ending. You have read it a hundred times.
- **3** — Competent and forgettable. Clean prose, sensible structure, a twist you half-expected, a feeling that is named rather than caused. **This is what an unprompted model produces, and it is not good enough.**
- **4** — Good. Something here is specific and alive; you would tell a friend about one moment in it.
- **5** — The listener will still be thinking about it tomorrow.

A score of 5 is rare and must be argued for with quotations. When unsure between two scores, give the lower one and say what would earn the higher.
