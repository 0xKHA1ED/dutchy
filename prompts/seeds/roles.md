# Roles

The protagonist's job or social role. Good roles give a person power over others, a ritual, a vantage point, or a secret. Avoid the banned defaults (writers, painters, scientists on the verge of a breakthrough).

## Judges, gatekeepers and inspectors

- A driving-test examiner — decides who is safe; sits beside strangers in a car with his own brake
- A border customs officer — asks questions all day and learns to read lies
- A restaurant hygiene inspector — can close a family business with one signature
- A night-club bouncer — chooses who gets in, every night, by instinct
- A university admissions reader — reads thousands of life stories in a season
- A wedding registrar — has married three thousand couples and knows which ones will last
- An insurance claims adjuster — decides whether a disaster was an accident
- A school canteen supervisor — the only adult some children talk to
- A football referee in an amateur league — hated by everyone every Sunday

## People who see others at their most private

- A professional mourner, paid to cry at funerals
- A hotel night auditor — alone at reception from midnight to 7 a.m.
- A home-care nurse who visits eight old people a day
- A locksmith called out to emergencies at night
- A wedding photographer
- A lost-property office clerk
- A taxidermist
- A sign-language interpreter at hospitals and courts
- A tailor who makes alterations — knows everyone's real measurements
- A night-bus driver
- A cleaner in a prison
- An estate clearer — empties the homes of the dead
- A piano tuner who works in rich people's houses
- A voice actor who reads audiobooks alone in a booth

## Odd, specific and invented-feeling (but real)

- A man paid to queue for others
- A professional apologiser, hired by companies to say sorry in person
- A "rent-a-friend" who attends events as someone's friend
- A fortune cookie writer
- A crossword setter
- A golf-ball diver who retrieves balls from lakes
- A perfume tester (a "nose")
- A lighthouse keeper's replacement, for one month
- A mannequin dresser for department store windows
- A dog walker for wealthy, absent owners
- A medical actor who plays patients for trainee doctors
- A stand-in for a famous actor, never seen in close-up
- A call-centre agent who pretends to be in another country
- A matchmaker for parents seeking spouses for their children
- A night-time crossing guard at a hospital car park
- An elevator inspector
- A hearing-aid technician
- A professional sleeper for mattress tests

## Ordinary roles with a twist of pressure

- A primary school headteacher in her last year
- A small-town mayor no one else wanted to be
- A baker who has opened at 4 a.m. for forty years
- A pharmacist in a village where she knows everyone's prescriptions
- A bus driver on the same route for twenty years
- A security guard at an empty building
- A supermarket cashier on the night shift
- A school bus driver
- A janitor in a university philosophy department
- A barber
- A swimming-pool lifeguard who can't swim well
- A tour guide in a city he has never liked

## Social roles (not jobs)

- The family's reliable one, who everyone calls first
- The eldest daughter who raised her siblings
- The best man at his ex-girlfriend's wedding
- The neighbour who holds everyone's spare keys
- The executor of a stranger's will
- The last speaker of a family's language
- The person who found the body
- The witness who didn't come forward
