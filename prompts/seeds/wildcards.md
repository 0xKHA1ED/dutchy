# Wildcards

Odd creative constraints, in the spirit of Brian Eno's *Oblique Strategies*. Every story rolls one. A wildcard forces an invention nobody would have planned: Gérard's indicator ticking like a clock came from "a sound becomes a clock".

The plot stage may drop a wildcard only if it fights the mechanism, and must say why.

## Structure

- The most important line is spoken by a minor character
- The story covers less than one hour
- The story covers exactly one year, in four short visits
- The ending happens in the same place as the beginning
- One scene is told twice, from two places in the room
- The story begins with the ending, and is still surprising
- The middle chapter is a single conversation
- The climax happens off-stage and is only reported

## Characters

- One character never speaks
- Someone tells the truth and nobody believes them
- The antagonist is right about everything except one thing
- A character is only ever seen from behind
- Someone in the story is lying about their name
- The protagonist is wrong about who loves them
- An animal makes the decisive move
- A child understands what the adults don't, and says nothing

## Sound and senses

- A sound becomes a clock
- A smell returns three times and means something different each time
- Something is heard before it is seen
- Someone hums the same song all story; the last time, someone else hums it
- The story's most emotional moment happens in total silence
- Rain is not allowed; find another way to show grief

## Objects and language

- An object changes hands three times
- A word in a language the listener doesn't know appears three times and is never translated, but is understood by the end
- A list is the turning point
- A letter is written but never sent
- A single phrase is said at the start and the end, with the opposite meaning
- Someone counts something throughout the story
- Every chapter contains a door

## Tone

- The funniest moment is also the saddest
- Nobody in the story raises their voice
- The worst thing happens politely
- A small kindness from a stranger changes the ending
- The protagonist gets exactly what they asked for
- Someone apologises for the wrong thing
- A celebration is happening in the background of the worst moment
